How Dental Implants Work and Why They’re Worth It

An implant tooth replacement has three parts that work together. A titanium post joins with the jawbone to form a stable base. An abutment connects the post to the new tooth. A custom crown, bridge , or denture completes the smile.

This design offers everyday benefits. You can chew comfortably without special diets, enjoy a secure fit that does not slip, avoid sore spots, speak clearly, and care for your teeth with regular brushing and flossing.

Complete Implant Treatment Options

Single Tooth Replacement Excellence

Preserving Your Natural Teeth

A single implant replaces one tooth without shaping the teeth beside it, which helps preserve healthy structure. It also stimulates the jawbone like a natural root. The result is a replacement that feels independent and looks natural, thanks to a custom-designed crown. With proper care, single tooth treatment can serve for decades.

Multiple Tooth Solutions

Flexible Replacement Options

When several teeth are missing, we can place implants to support a bridge for 3 to 4 teeth in a row, secure a partial denture for better stability, or replace teeth in separate areas with individual implants.

These choices are designed to feel stable and comfortable while protecting the teeth you still have. If you prefer a removable option with more security, ask about an implant-retained denture.

All-on-Four Complete Arch Restoration

Same-Day Teeth with Proven Technology

The All-on-Four technique uses four strategically placed implants to support a full arch of replacement teeth with immediate function. This approach often avoids the need for bone grafting procedures while providing cost-effective full mouth restoration.

All-on-Four offers faster treatment time with predictable results, making it an excellent solution for patients missing most or all teeth in one or both arches.

Full Mouth Restoration for Complete Function

Ultimate Tooth Replacement Solution

A complete approach utilizes 8 to 12 implants placed across both arches to support fixed bridges that function similarly to natural teeth. This restores chewing ability, improves facial support, and helps maintain a youthful appearance while avoiding many of the challenges associated with traditional dentures. Candidacy and Health Considerations

Ideal Candidate Characteristics

The best candidates have adequate bone structure to support implant placement, healthy gums free from gum disease , good overall health that supports healing, a commitment to oral hygiene and regular care, and realistic expectations about treatment outcomes.

Factors Requiring Special Consideration

Certain conditions need evaluation before treatment, including insufficient bone potentially requiring grafting, active periodontal disease needing treatment first, smoking significantly reducing success rates, certain medications affecting healing, and systemic diseases such as uncontrolled diabetes requiring management.

Age and Implant Success

Treatment can benefit patients across various age groups, including young adults after completing jaw development, middle-aged patients replacing teeth lost to decay or trauma, and seniors seeking stable tooth replacement. There’s no upper age limit for healthy patients who can undergo surgery.

Long-Term Care and Maintenance

Daily Care Requirements

Dental implants stay healthy with the same steady habits that protect natural teeth. Brush twice a day with a soft-bristle toothbrush, clean between teeth and around the implant each day, and use an antimicrobial rinse if we recommend one. Avoid very hard foods that can chip a crown or bridge. If you smoke, quitting supports healing and long-term success.

Professional Maintenance Schedule

Long-term success requires regular dental checkups every six months, which involve monitoring implant health, professional cleanings to remove plaque and tartar, periodic X-rays to assess bone levels, early intervention to address detected problems, and restoration maintenance, including adjustments or replacements when needed.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants in Deland

Are implant procedures painful?

Placement is done with local anesthesia, so you stay comfortable during the visit. Most people describe the soreness after surgery as milder than they expected, similar to having a tooth removed. You will receive clear instructions after surgery and guidance on managing tenderness.

How long do implants last?

With good care, implants can last 20 to 30 years or even a lifetime. The attached crown, bridge, or denture may need replacement about every 10 to 15 years due to normal wear. When placed by experienced clinicians, success rates exceed 95 %.

Am I too old for implants?

Age by itself is not a barrier. Many people in their 70s and 80s undergo successful implant procedures. What matters most is overall health, adequate bone, and healing ability. Dr. Barney evaluates each situation individually.

How long does the complete process take?

The complete process typically takes 3-6 months from initial placement to final restoration. Immediate placement may be possible in some cases, while complex cases requiring grafting may take longer. Dr. Barney provides detailed timelines during consultation.
