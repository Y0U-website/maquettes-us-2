Who May Be a Candidate for Dental Implants?

Implant treatment requires enough bone and healthy surrounding tissues to support the planned restoration. Your general health, dental health, medical history, oral hygiene, and other factors can also affect treatment planning and healing.

Some patients can move directly into implant planning, while others may need additional dental care first. The consultation helps determine what your individual treatment sequence may require.

Bone Support and Implant Planning

Bone support plays an important role in dental implant treatment because the implant relies on the jawbone for stability.

When a tooth has been missing for some time, the bone in that area may change. Dental imaging and a clinical examination help the dentist evaluate the available area and determine whether the site can support the planned implant.

If the existing bone does not provide adequate support, the dentist may discuss whether an additional procedure, such as bone grafting, should be included in the treatment plan.

What Happens During Dental Implant Treatment?

Implant treatment usually involves several stages, but the exact sequence and timing vary from one patient to another.

Your dentist will explain the sequence that applies to your individual case before treatment begins.

  • Initial evaluation: The dentist examines your mouth, reviews your dental and health history, and discusses the missing tooth or teeth.
  • Diagnostic planning: Dental imaging helps the dentist evaluate the treatment area, available bone, nearby structures, and restorative needs.
  • Implant placement: The dentist places the implant into the planned area of the jawbone.
  • Healing: The implant needs time to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone.
  • Final restoration: Once the implant reaches the appropriate restorative stage, the dentist can complete the planned crown or other tooth replacement.

How Long Does Dental Implant Treatment Take?

There is no single timeline that applies to every implant. Healing time depends on the treatment site, bone condition, individual healing response, whether additional procedures are necessary, and the type of final restoration.

Some cases require several months between implant placement and the final restoration. Your dentist can provide a more accurate estimate after evaluating your specific treatment needs.

Single-Tooth Dental Implants

When one tooth is missing, a dental implant can support a custom crown without using neighboring teeth as anchors for a traditional bridge.

The dentist plans the implant and final restoration together because position, bite, available space, gum contours, and appearance can all influence the final result.

Can Dental Implants Replace Multiple Teeth?

Implant treatment is not limited to replacing a single tooth. Depending on the number and location of missing teeth, available bone, and treatment plan, implants may support restorations that replace several teeth.

Patients who need more extensive tooth replacement may also want to understand the differences between traditional removable dentures and implant-supported options.

Dental Implants and Gum Health

Healthy tissues around the implant are important for long-term maintenance. Active gum problems may need attention before or during implant treatment planning.

The dentist evaluates the gums and supporting tissues as part of the consultation. When periodontal treatment is appropriate, our Dental Periodontics in Miami page provides additional information about gum care.

Dental Implants vs. Dentures

Dental implants and dentures both replace missing teeth, but they work differently. Traditional dentures are removable, while implants can provide a fixed foundation in the jaw for certain restorations.

One option is not automatically better than the other. Your oral health, missing teeth, bone support, treatment goals, maintenance needs, and budget can all affect the decision.

During your consultation, the dentist can explain which tooth replacement approaches may fit your situation and why.

How to Care for a Dental Implant

Implants still require consistent oral hygiene and professional dental care. Although the implant restoration itself cannot develop a cavity, plaque and inflammation can affect the tissues surrounding it.

Daily brushing, cleaning between the teeth and implant restorations, and regular dental visits help the dental team monitor the surrounding gums, bite, restoration, and overall oral health.
