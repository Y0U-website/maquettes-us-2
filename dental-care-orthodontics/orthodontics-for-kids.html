Two-Phase Orthodontic Treatment

Phase 1 (Interceptive) — Ages 7-10

For some children, early treatment prevents or reduces more complex issues later. Phase 1 treatment might address palatal narrowness, crossbites, severe crowding (making space for permanent teeth), and early jaw growth guidance. This phase typically lasts 6-12 months and uses targeted devices like palatal expanders.

Phase 2 (Comprehensive) — Ages 10-14

Once permanent teeth have erupted, most kids are ready for comprehensive treatment with full braces or Invisalign Teen . This is when we align the full smile and correct any remaining bite issues. Note that many kids do not require Phase 1 and go straight to Phase 2.

Treatment Options for Kids

Metal Braces

The most popular option for kids, and often the best choice. Modern metal braces are smaller, smoother, and feature colorful elastic bands that kids love choosing at each visit.

Damon Braces

Fewer adjustment visits and easier cleaning make Damon self-ligating braces a convenient choice for busy families and older children.

Palatal Expanders

Expanders gently widen a narrow upper jaw during active growth to correct crossbites and create space for erupting permanent teeth.

Kids Orthodontic FAQs

At what age should my child first see an orthodontist?

By age 7, per the American Association of Orthodontists. Most kids don’t need treatment at that age, but early evaluation lets us monitor growth and catch issues early if they arise.

What’s Phase 1 (early) treatment and does my child need it?

Phase 1 treatment is early intervention at ages 7-10 that addresses specific issues (like jaw misalignment or crossbites) before all permanent teeth erupt. Not every child needs Phase 1 — many go straight to comprehensive braces or clear aligners. Your orthodontist will tell you honestly whether Phase 1 would benefit your child.

Will braces affect my child’s sports or music activities?

No — kids can participate in all activities with braces. We recommend a custom mouthguard for contact sports to protect teeth and braces. Wind instrument players adjust quickly after an initial adjustment period.
