What Are Dental Implants?

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

While getting dental implants takes longer than replacing your teeth any other way, their ability to last a lifetime makes them absolutely worth the wait. Plus, we can complete the entire dental implant process under one roof, making your treatment more convenient and less stressful. Every patient’s tooth replacement journey looks different, but the four basic steps are:

  • Initial dental implant consultation, when we’ll examine your mouth and create a personalized treatment plan.
  • Dental implant surgery, which is when the implants are surgically placed in your jaw.
  • Dental implant osseointegration/abutment, when the implants fuse with your jawbone and abutment pieces are put in.
  • Delivery of dental implant restorations, when we place your crown, bridge, or denture on top of your abutments.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

Before we can proceed with dental implants, we need to make sure you’re set up for success. A consultation serves a critical purpose, allowing us to examine your mouth, confirm your eligibility, and put together a detailed and custom plan to complete your smile. During this appointment, we can answer any question you have about cost, the implant process, treatment timeline, and more.

Dental Implant Surgery

Unlike some other practices in the area, we don’t refer our patients to specialists for the placement procedure. We handle the whole process, including this highly technical and advanced surgery, right here in our office, saving you the time and trouble of working with another team. During the surgery, we’ll make sure you’re completely comfortable, and we’ll precisely position your implants so that you experience minimal issues.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Recovering from the surgery typically takes less than a week, but the more essential healing process is just beginning. In three to six months’ time, the jawbone produces new growth and surrounds the implants, giving them security and reliability. Once this process, called osseointegration, is complete, the abutments or connection pieces can be inserted.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

After the abutments are in place, we can order your custom restoration. We’ll take impressions of your upper and lower arches with our digital impression system. Instead of gag-inducing goop, we use a small device that scans the inside of your mouth, precisely and comfortably measuring everything necessary to create personalized restorations in the dental lab. Finally, when the final products are ready, we’ll bond them to your dental implants. At that point, you have a complete smile and are free to use it like the real thing!

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants are unique in that they replace both the crown of the tooth as well as the root. No other treatment does this. As a result, there are multiple benefits of dental implants in Aspen Hill that traditional dental bridges and dentures simply cannot deliver. We’ll be happy to go over these unique advantages with you during your consultation, but for now, read more about the ways in which dental implants can improve your life below.

Day-to-Day Benefits

  • The implant posts act as stable foundations for your new teeth. This means you won’t have to worry about your teeth shifting or slipping out of place something for which dentures are notorious.
  • With dental implants, you’ll enjoy a chewing power almost as strong as what you had prior to tooth loss. You’ll be able to enjoy all of your favorite foods, including crunchy vegetables and tough steak.
  • Traditional dentures may warp if they’re exposed to hot beverages like coffee or tea. In contrast, your implant restoration will maintain its shape no matter what you eat or drink.
  • To top our dental implants in Aspen Hill, we use custom restorations made from 100 % porcelain. This allows them to completely blend in with your existing teeth, ensuring that only you and our dentists know which teeth are real!

Health Benefits

  • The fewer natural teeth you have left, the greater your risk of developing serious medical conditions, such as cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and osteoporosis. Since dental implants fill all of the functions of your natural teeth, it’s reasonable to assume that they reduce such risks.
  • Dental bridges require that some enamel be removed from two of your remaining teeth, and dentures can rub against the gums, causing irritation. Dental implants are self-supporting and stay in one place, preventing these oral health issues.
  • Did you know that dental implants can not only preserve the foundation of your remaining teeth, but make sure your face keeps its youthful shape over time? This is because dental implants actually prevent and reverse the jawbone loss that commonly occurs after teeth are removed.

Long-Term Benefits

  • Dental implants have a success rate of more than 95 %. They require very little maintenance beyond brushing and flossing them like natural teeth. You won’t have to purchase separate cleaning products like you would with conventional dentures.
  • With good oral hygiene and regular checkups with your implant dentist in Aspen Hill, you can expect your dental implant to last for 30 years on average maybe even for the rest of your life!
  • Because dental implants can last so long, they can actually save you money in the long term. Yes, bridges and dentures cost less at first, but they have to be adjusted or replaced every decade or so on average, compounding their initial lower price. Dental implants, on the other hand, should just be a one-time investment.

Dental Implants Post-Op Instructions

The care that you give your dental implants after they’ve been placed in your mouth is of the utmost importance. By taking the right steps, you can ensure that there aren’t any problems during recovery and maximize your chances for a successful dental implant process. To help you prepare ahead of time, here’s a brief look at some of the post-op instructions that you’ll need to follow.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Plan on resting for at least one or two days following your dental implant placement. Take whatever steps are needed to manage your post-surgical discomfort. Do not drink through a straw, use tobacco products of any kind, or directly touch the implant site. All of these actions could disturb the blood clot that will form at the area where the surgery was performed, which can disrupt healing.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

Once the local anesthetic used to numb your mouth for dental implant surgery wears off, there may be some mild discomfort. You can control it with either prescribed or over-the-counter pain medication. Additionally, you may experience some swelling (which can often be alleviated with a cold compress) or intermittent bleeding (which can be stopped with a piece of gauze and a slight amount of pressure). All of these symptoms should eventually go away on their own.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

Following your dental implant surgery, your diet will consist of soft or liquid foods, such as mashed potatoes, scrambled eggs, yogurt, and pasta. This is to avoid putting too much stress on your mouth while it is trying to heal. Remember not to eat or drink anything that is too hot; it could end up irritating the tissues around your dental implants.

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Good oral hygiene is always important, of course, but it becomes particularly vital while you are recovering from dental implant surgery. You need to do everything you can to prevent an infection from occurring before your mouth has fully healed.

After the first 24 hours, you can brush your teeth like normal. However, you will need to be gentle around the surgical area. On top of that, it is recommended that you rinse with salt water multiple times a day. Mouthwash can also be used as long as it does not contain alcohol.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

The final crown, bridge, or denture will be placed long after your mouth has finished recovering from dental implant surgery, so there shouldn’t be any swelling or bleeding by that point. At most, your gums may be mildly sensitive, which is an issue that pain medication can help with. Please let us know immediately if you experience any unexpected side effects that could indicate that a problem has occurred.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants are able to last upwards of 30 years or even the rest of your life, but it is essential that you take all the right steps to care for them. If you aren’t taking the time to protect your dental implants from damage, you may end up experiencing a dental implant failure and needing a replacement. The good news is that a few simple precautions and healthy habits can help you keep your new smile healthy for many years to come.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Dental implants are unable to get cavities themselves, but you still need to practice excellent oral hygiene. In addition to keeping your surrounding teeth healthy, sticking to a good oral hygiene routine can prevent other dental problems, like gum disease, that can cause your implants to fail. Remember to brush twice, floss, and rinse with mouthwash daily. Use a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoridated toothpaste.

Eat a Healthy Diet

One of the amazing things about dental implants is that you don’t need to deal with any eating restrictions. This means that you can easily enjoy a full, nutritious meal without too much trouble. Just remember that enjoying too many foods that are hard, sticky, or sugary can worsen oral health. Instead, consume foods with plenty of calcium and vitamin C to keep your jawbone in good health.

Break Bad Habits

Bad habits can cause your dental implants to fail. Avoid smoking and using tobacco products immediately after your procedure, as they can slow down healing and increase your risk of infection. Don’t use your teeth as tools either. You could easily end up damaging your implant or a surrounding tooth. Instead, find a tool that can help you complete your task safely.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Your dental implants are very strong, but they aren’t indestructible. Be sure that you always wear a mouthguard to protect your teeth while playing contact sports. If you grind your teeth at night, ask about getting a nightguard to protect your implants (and natural teeth) from wear and tear.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Attend your regular cleanings and checkups every six months. This way, your dentist can monitor your dental implants and keep an eye out for any issues as early on as possible. By catching problems early, we can prevent them from becoming more serious and leading to consequences.

Dental Implant Failure & Salvage

In the unlikely event that your dental implants fail, our team is ready to help. We can examine your implant posts and the area around them in order to identify the true source of the problem. Then we can put together a treatment plan to restore your oral health and protect your newly repaired smile. Even if we end up needing to remove your dental implants, you may be able to have new ones placed once the necessary treatments have been performed.

3D Cone Beam Imaging

We have an in-house cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) scanner. This remarkable piece of technology is essentially a highly advanced X-ray. It uses the same type of radiation in order to capture images, but the radiation is emitted in a cone-shaped beam. Therefore, the machine is able to capture incredibly detailed, three-dimensional images.

Our CBCT scanner can be used in a few different ways throughout the implant process:

Plus, our machine uses much less radiation than a regular CT scanner that you would find in a hospital.

  • Before we place implants, we can use it to verify that your jawbone and other oral structures are healthy enough for your new prosthetic tooth roots. We can also plan for any unusual features of your oral anatomy.
  • After you receive implants, we can perform a scan to check how well they are integrating with your jawbone.
  • If you ever have any questions or concerns about the health of your implants, we may use it to diagnose problems so we can formulate an appropriate treatment plan.

Digital Impression System

When you are getting ready to receive your new teeth, we will use our digital impression system to help design your new restoration. All we have to do is move a small, wand-like device around your mouth. It will capture countless images and then assemble them into an accurate digital representation of your smile. Based on it, we can plan the details of your beautiful crown, bridge or denture.

Digital impressions are simpler and easier than manual impressions. In the past, dentists would always have to use an unpleasant putty-like material to get molds of patients’ teeth. This process is not always accurate, and some people find it very uncomfortable. Digital impressions carry a lower risk of gagging, are precise the first time, and use fewer materials, which leads to a more eco-friendly dental environment. Plus, since we can send impressions to a laboratory via our computer system, digital impressions cut down on shipping times and costs.
