What is All-on-4 and how does it work?

All-on-4 replaces a full arch of missing or failing teeth using four titanium implants that anchor one fixed, non-removable bridge. Two implants are placed straight at the front and two angled at the back to maximize support, often avoiding a bone graft. Many patients receive a temporary fixed set of teeth the same day (immediate-load). See our dedicated All-on-4 in Miami guide for the full process, candidacy and financing.

All-on-4 vs. All-on-6 vs. snap-on dentures

All-on-4 uses four implants for a fixed full-arch bridge; All-on-6 (and broader All-on-X ) adds more implants for extra support in patients with adequate bone. Snap-on dentures (implant-supported overdentures) clip onto two to four implants and are removable for cleaning, a lower-cost option that is less stable than a fixed zirconia bridge.

Same-day dental implants: who qualifies?

Same-day (immediate-load) implants let qualifying patients leave with fixed temporary teeth the day of surgery. Good candidates have enough healthy jawbone and no active infection or uncontrolled gum disease. Dr. Garcia uses a 3D scan at your free evaluation to confirm whether immediate-load implants or All-on-4 are right for your case.

How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants are designed to be permanent. Once the titanium post fuses to the jawbone (osseointegration), it can last a lifetime with good hygiene and routine checkups, while the crown, bridge or denture on top typically lasts 10–15 years or more before it may need replacement. Implant success rates exceed 95 %.

Do I need a bone graft or sinus lift?

Not always. If a tooth has been missing for a while the jawbone can shrink, and a bone graft rebuilds a solid foundation. When placing upper-back implants, a sinus lift may add bone height. All-on-4’s angled implants often avoid grafting entirely. We confirm what your case needs with a 3D scan at your free evaluation.

Single tooth implant vs. bridge

A single-tooth implant replaces one missing tooth without touching the neighbors and can last a lifetime. A traditional bridge is faster and lower-cost up front but grinds down two healthy adjacent teeth and usually lasts 10–15 years. For most healthy patients, an implant protects more of your natural teeth over time. Compare options with a crown or bridge .
