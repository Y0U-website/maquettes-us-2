Dental Implant Procedure

During the consultation, we will take 3D scans of your mouth, create your custom treatment plan, and discuss your anesthesia options for your comfort during surgery. Our team will make sure your oral surgery treatment is as seamless as possible. We will walk you through every step and provide you with instructions to follow before and after your procedure.

On the day of your procedure, we will administer the selected form of anesthesia and make sure you are comfortable. Your oral surgeon will make an incision in the gum tissue and insert the implant post. Sutures are placed around the implant site, if necessary, and the healing process will begin.

Over the next few months, the implant post will fuse with the jaw bone. Once it has fully healed, you will return for a second surgical procedure where we uncover the post and connect the abutment. Sometimes, this is performed during the initial procedure and a second procedure is not needed. Finally, your restorative dentist will attach the final crown to your implant, completing your treatment.

Dental Implants: Frequently Asked Questions

The dental implant post itself integrates with the jaw bone and is designed to last a lifetime. If the crown breaks or requires a replacement due to wear and tear, your dentist can simply place a new one. With proper care, your implant will serve you for many years.

Good oral hygiene is essential for maintaining healthy teeth and gums, and for ensuring the longevity of your implant. Brush and floss your implant just like you do with your natural teeth. There are no special products necessary.

The dental implant procedure typically takes one hour per implant. This may vary if you require additional procedures, such as tooth extraction or bone grafting, or if you are receiving multiple implants.

If you’ve been missing a tooth for a year, you can get a dental implant as long as there is enough jaw bone to support it. Some people replace a missing tooth immediately, while others do not replace it for many years, but as long as your jaw bone is healthy and strong, you can receive a dental implant at any time.

The number of implants you need, if you require additional procedures, your dental insurance, and your choice of anesthesia will all factor into the cost of your dental implants. We create a custom treatment plan for every patient, so the cost of dental implant treatment will vary.
