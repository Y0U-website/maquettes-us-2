What Factors Influence the Price of Dental Implants?

Since no one has an identical mouth, that is the primary reason why prices for implants vary greatly from one individual to another. For instance, a person who requires only one implant in the front part of the mouth will normally have a smaller price tag than the individual whose molars have to be replaced.

Bone density is also something that influences prices more significantly than many people realize. If the bone has become weakened due to lack of teeth for many years. A patient may have to undergo a bone graft first, which will raise costs and prolong the procedure. An experience of the dentist, type of implant, and the laboratory making a crown are all factors that affect the price.

Do Dental Insurance Policies Cover the Cost of Implants?

While dental insurance has traditionally viewed implants as purely cosmetic, this is starting to change. Nowadays, many PPO policies do provide some coverage for either the crown or the abutment, even though the surgery procedure may not be included.

To know your implant cost with insurance, you just need to go through the details of your particular policy; annual limits, waiting periods, and tooth loss provisions will definitely play a role. Implant Treatment Procedure: Everything You Should Know

An implant process is not just one session but a series of sessions carried out over several months. The process includes an imaging consultation, the implant surgery, the time that the jawbone heals as it attaches to the implant, and the placement of the dental crown.

Patients find the procedure much easier to tolerate than anticipated due to advances in technology and the use of anesthetics during the process. The time taken for the healing process, which is known as osseointegration , usually ranges from two to four months.

Cost Comparisons of Implants vs. Dental Bridges and Dentures

An implant is always more expensive than a dental bridge or a set of dentures at the outset. But when one considers the entire lifespan of the three dental treatments, the comparison changes. Typically, a dental bridge will need replacement after 10 or 15 years. Also, dentures may require continuous re-lining due to changes in your jawbone structure.

This is because an implant mimics a natural tooth root and also maintains your underlying bone. Thus, in the long run, most people feel that the cost of the entire life span of an implant turns out to be favorable.

Dental Implant Patients’ Age and General Health

Since dental implants are primarily dependent on the quality of the bone rather than the age of the person, there is no maximum age when an implant can be installed. There is greater concern for patients with problems such as uncontrolled diabetes, osteoporosis, or a significant smoking history that might affect the healing and implant integration process.

Before treatment, a dentist must evaluate a patient’s general health, which means most dentists will ask about any medical problems and drugs in use on the very first visit. This way, even patients who manage these issues successfully become ideal candidates and heal as predictably as people without health problems.

Post-Dental Implant Care

After you have recovered from your implant placement and once your crown is placed, basic care for your dental implant is almost the same as the care of your regular teeth. This consists of simple brushing and flossing of your crown along with your bi-annual visits to the dentist.

Missing out on your appointments is what usually causes all the trouble, as gum inflammation around your implant will be hard to detect on your own. If you maintain good hygiene practices and regular visits, your implant can last several years easily.

FAQs:

What causes pricing variations for implants from one dentist to another?

The difference in pricing is determined by the brand of the implants, the laboratory for the crowns, and whether your dentist does bone grafting himself/herself or refers you elsewhere. You should ask what is included in the quote because what might be included in one dentist’s starting price may not be included in the next.

How long is the implant supposed to last before it has to be replaced?

If the implant is cared for correctly, the titanium rod lasts several decades, even outlasting all of your other dental work. The crown on the top is supposed to be replaced once in 10-15 years due to wearing, but it is cheaper than redoing the whole implant again.

Can you still receive an implant if you’ve had gum disease in the past?

Yes; however, gum disease should be treated and cured first since inflammation and infection prevent the area from accepting the implant successfully. You will most probably be advised to have a certain period of time to do cleanings and observations before proceeding to surgery.

Do front teeth implants heal any differently than back teeth implants?

Back teeth, which are molars, may take a little bit longer in order to heal, as they undergo greater stress from chewing forces and will need a wider-diameter implant. There will also be additional work to consider the gumline form, as the aesthetics in the front part of the mouth are important.

What if a bone graft is required, but the patient does not want to incur that additional expense?

In most cases, skipping a bone graft when one is actually needed is discouraged, as the implant requires a sufficient amount of bone in order to maintain stability, and a failed implant will require much more money spent on correcting it.
