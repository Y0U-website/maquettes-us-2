Dental implants are artificial roots that replace lost teeth due to cavities, accidents, or failure to erupt. These implants are primarily made of titanium, which is considered the best biocompatible material available today. Titanium is not only used for tooth replacement but also in various orthopedic prostheses such as knees or hips.

Fixed Denture Implants Rehabilitation represents one of the most significant advancements in complete tooth replacement. They result from the integration of two dental specialties, substantially enhancing patients’ dental conditions, restoring a natural and aesthetic smile, and improving overall chewing capacity.

In this type of denture, patients cannot remove them independently. These prostheses attach directly to the patient’s maxilla or mandible via previously placed implants. The number of implants required can vary based on the quality and quantity of bone present in the jaws.

In cases of bone deficiency, where additional bone is needed to support the implant, the periodontist addresses the lack of bone by introducing new bone into the deficient area where the implant will be placed.

The maxillary sinuses often obstruct the placement of implants in the upper posterior sector. For this reason, the periodontist may require an intervention to modify the condition of the maxillary sinus. The sinus lift is a procedure that must be performed before or during the implant placement.
