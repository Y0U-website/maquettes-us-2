Signs You May Need a Deep Cleaning

Red, swollen, or bleeding gums, persistent bad breath, gum recession, and deepening pockets between your teeth and gums can all point to gum disease. If we notice these signs during your exam, scaling and root planing is often the first line of treatment to halt the problem before it worsens.

What to Expect Afterward

Some mild tenderness or sensitivity is normal for a few days after treatment, and it typically fades quickly. As your gums heal, you’ll often notice they look pinker, feel firmer, and bleed less. We’ll give you simple aftercare guidance and, in many cases, transition you into a periodontal maintenance schedule to protect the results.

Protecting Your Long-Term Gum Health

Scaling and root planing is a powerful reset, but keeping gum disease away depends on what happens next. Consistent home care and regular maintenance visits keep bacteria from rebuilding below the gumline. Together, they help preserve the bone and tissue that support your teeth, protecting your smile for the long haul.
