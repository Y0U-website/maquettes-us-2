Implants vs. Other Tooth Replacement Options

When weighing your options for replacing a missing tooth, it helps to understand how implants compare to alternatives like dental bridges and dentures.

Why Implants Stand Apart

Bridges require the reduction of adjacent healthy teeth to anchor the restoration in place, which can affect those teeth long-term. Dentures, while effective for full arch replacement, can shift over time and may require periodic adjustments. Dental implants, by contrast, preserve the surrounding teeth, stimulate the jawbone to prevent bone loss, and are made to last for decades with proper care.

That said, the best option depends on your individual circumstances, budget, and oral health. Our team will walk you through every possibility so you can make a confident, well-informed decision.

While dental implants have a high success rate, there is a small chance that they can fail. Factors that contribute to implant failure include poor oral hygiene, inadequate bone density or quality, and certain medical conditions such as uncontrolled diabetes or autoimmune diseases.

However, most implant failures can be avoided with proper care and maintenance, including good oral hygiene habits and regular visits to your implant dentist. In some cases, if an implant does fail, it may need to be removed and replaced. Your implant dentist can discuss the risks and potential complications with you before the procedure.
