Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants offer numerous advantages , making them an ideal choice for those seeking a permanent tooth replacement:

  • Natural Appearance : Implants look and feel like natural teeth.
  • Improved Function : They restore full chewing ability and improve speech.
  • Durability : With proper care, implants can last a lifetime.
  • Bone Health : Implants prevent jawbone deterioration that typically occurs with missing teeth.
  • Convenience : Unlike dentures , implants are fixed, so there’s no need for adhesives.

Types of Dental Implants

Various types of dental implants are available to suit different dental needs and jaw structures. Here are some of the most common types:

Endosteal Dental Implants

Endosteal implants are the most common type. They are surgically placed into the jawbone, providing a strong foundation for single crowns, bridges, or dentures.

Single-Tooth Dental Implants

If you’re missing just one tooth, a single-tooth implant can restore it with a natural-looking crown, matching surrounding teeth seamlessly.

All-On-Four Implants

For those missing most or all teeth, All-On-Four implants offer a full arch replacement by using just four implants to support an entire row of teeth.

Implant-Supported Bridges

Implant-supported bridges are ideal for patients missing multiple teeth in a row. Instead of needing an implant for each tooth, these bridges use implants at each end to anchor the bridge securely.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Similar to traditional dentures but more stable, implant-supported dentures snap onto implants placed in the jaw, providing a secure fit that prevents slipping.

Mini Dental Implants

Mini implants are smaller and less invasive than traditional implants, making them suitable for patients with less bone density or limited space.

Immediate-Load “Same Day” Dental Implants

Also called “ teeth in a day ,” this option allows for a temporary crown to be placed on the same day as the implant surgery, providing instant results.

Zygomatic Dental Implants

Used when there is significant bone loss in the upper jaw, zygomatic implants are anchored in the cheekbone rather than the jawbone.

Subperiosteal Dental Implants

Subperiosteal dental implants rest on top of the jawbone, rather than within it, making them ideal for patients who cannot undergo bone grafting.

The Dental Implant Procedure at Dentistry of North PORT

The dental implant process involves several steps and takes place over a few months to ensure a successful, long-lasting result:

Initial Consultation

Dr. Rua-Perez will conduct an evaluation, including X-rays or 3D imaging, to assess bone density and determine the best implant type for you.

Implant Placement

In a minor surgical procedure, the implant post is placed into the jawbone. A healing period of a few months follows, allowing the bone to bond with the implant (osseointegration).

Abutment Placement

Once healed, an abutment is attached to the implant, preparing it to support the final crown.

Crown Placement

Finally, a custom-made crown is secured to the abutment, completing your new smile.

Aftercare Tips for Dental Implants

  • Maintain Good Oral Hygiene : Brush and floss daily to keep the implant area clean.
  • Visit Your Dentist Regularly : Regular check-ups with Dr. Rua-Perez help ensure your implants stay in great shape.
  • Avoid Hard Foods : Chewing hard foods can damage your crown, so avoid them when possible.
  • Quit Smoking : Smoking can hinder healing and affect the longevity of implants.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a missing tooth need to be replaced?

Yes, a missing tooth needs to be replaced as soon as possible . By not reversing tooth loss, you may experience:

  • Shifting of the surrounding teeth : When you experience tooth loss, the surrounding teeth may shift to fill the gap. Shifting teeth can lead to misalignment and bite problems.
  • Bone loss : The jawbone needs stimulation from the roots of the teeth to maintain its density. When a tooth is missing, the jawbone may shrink, leading to bone loss.
  • Increased risk of periodontal disease : Missing teeth can create space for bacteria to accumulate, increasing your risk of gum disease.
  • Difficulty speaking and eating : Missing teeth can make it difficult to speak clearly and chew food properly.

Who is qualified to place a dental implant?

A few different types of dental professionals can place dental implants, including:

Certifications and qualifications vary based on the country, state, or region. Some states or countries may have different laws and regulations regarding who’s qualified to place dental implants.

  • An Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeon : Dental professionals who have completed additional training and education in the surgical placement of dental implants. They have the most extensive training and experience in implant placement and are often considered the most qualified to place them.
  • A Periodontist : Specializes in the treatment of periodontal disease and placing dental implants. They have completed additional training in implant placement and have a good understanding of the underlying bone and tissue structure.
  • A Prosthodontist : Specializes in the replacement of missing teeth and the restoration of natural teeth. They’re also qualified to place dental implants.
  • A General Dentist : They’ve completed training in implant placement and are qualified to place them.

How long do dental implants last?

On average, dental implants can last for between 20 years and a lifetime . To increase the long-term success of your dental implant, maintain excellent at-home dental care and schedule regular dental visits for checkups and cleanings.
