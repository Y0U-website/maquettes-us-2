What Is Temporomandibular Disorder?

Temporomandibular disorder (TMD) is a condition that results from malfunction of the temporomandibular joints (TMJ’s) or from the muscles, ligaments and tendons that are responsible for the movement of your lower jaw. While TMD can occur due to a number of factors, common causes of the disorder include clenching or grinding your teeth, injury to the head and neck, stress, missing teeth, arthritis, autoimmune disorders and tension relieving oral habits such as: nail biting, lip biting and cheek biting.

There are many different levels of TMD. Some patients are locked and can not open wider than one finger distance whereas other patients may be suffering from headaches, inner ear pain and ringing in their ears.

The Most Common Symptoms of TMD:

• Night time grinding of the teeth (bruxism)

• Tinnitus (constant ringing in the ears)

• Waking up in the morning with tooth pain, jaw pain and facial pain

Patients who suffer from temporomandibular disorder (TMD) know exactly how debilitating it can be. Not only can TMD make talking, eating, and smiling unbearable, it can cause permanent tooth and jaw joint damage.
