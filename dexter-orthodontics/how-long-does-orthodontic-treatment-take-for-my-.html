Retention: The Phase That Never Really Ends

Once braces come off or aligners are complete, treatment isn’t quite finished. Retainers are what keep teeth from drifting back toward their original position. Most kids wear retainers full-time for a few months, then transition to nightly wear long-term. This final phase is just as important as the active treatment that came before it — skipping it is the most common reason results don’t last.

Every child’s timeline looks a little different, and the only way to know what to expect for yours is a personal evaluation. Frequently Asked Questions

  • Does every child need Phase 1 treatment? No. Phase 1 is only recommended for moderate to severe issues that benefit from early intervention. Many children go straight into comprehensive treatment once their permanent teeth have erupted.
  • What’s the average total time in braces for kids? Most children spend 18 to 24 months in active comprehensive treatment, though the full journey — including any early treatment and the resting period between phases — can span several years.
  • Can my child’s treatment finish faster than expected? Yes. Consistent compliance with elastics, retainers, and appointments is the biggest factor within a family’s control, and it can meaningfully shorten treatment time.
  • How long will my child need to wear a retainer? Retainers are typically worn full-time for a few months after treatment, then nightly on a long-term basis. Skipping retainer wear is the most common reason teeth shift back.
