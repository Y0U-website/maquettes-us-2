  • Improved function : Dental implants are strong and durable, allowing you to eat and speak with confidence.
  • Improved oral health : Dental implants can help to prevent bone loss and gum recession, which can occur when a tooth is missing.
  • Convenience : Dental implants are permanent and do not require special care or maintenance, unlike other tooth replacement options such as dentures or bridges.
  • Long-term cost savings : Although dental implants may initially cost more than other tooth replacement options, they can actually save you money in the long run by reducing the need for future dental work.

How is a Dental Implant Restoration Performed?

The dental implant restoration process typically involves several steps:

  • Consultation : The first step is to schedule a consultation with your dentist to determine if you are a candidate for a dental implant restoration.
  • Implant placement : If you are a candidate for a dental implant restoration, the next step is to have the implant placed into your jawbone. This procedure is typically done by an oral surgeon under local anesthesia and is relatively painless.
  • Healing process : After the implant is placed, it will need to heal and fuse with the jawbone. This process can take several months, during which time a temporary restoration may be placed.
  • Final restoration : Once the implant has fused with the jawbone, a final restoration, such as a crown, bridge, or denture, will be attached to the implant.

Is a Dental Implant Restoration Right for You?

If you are missing one or more teeth, a dental implant restoration may be a good option for you. However, it is important to consult with Dr. Fat to determine if you are a candidate for this procedure. Factors such as overall health, oral hygiene, and the amount of available bone in the jaw can all affect whether or not you are a good candidate for a dental implant restoration.
