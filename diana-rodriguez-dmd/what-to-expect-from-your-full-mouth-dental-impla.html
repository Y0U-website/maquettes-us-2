Full Mouth Dental Implant

Sometimes, the pattern tells us enough. If you keep needing another crown, another extraction, or another repair, there may come a point when treating each tooth separately no longer works for you. Full mouth dental implants allow us to replace failing teeth and provide a stronger, healthier foundation.

  • Advanced gum disease ( periodontitis ) has left several teeth loose or failing
  • You are missing several teeth and finding it harder to eat comfortably
  • You need to replace most or all of your teeth, but don’t want to rely on traditional dentures
  • Your current dentures feel loose, shift during meals, or click when you speak

Why We Use Mini Dental Implants

But what makes mini dental implants different from other implant options, and what does that mean for you?

Mini dental implants are significantly smaller than traditional ones, measuring less than 3 millimeters in diameter. They also feature a one-piece design that combines the implant post and the abutment into a single, solid unit. In contrast, conventional implants require multiple components that must be placed in stages. While this difference may seem subtle, it simplifies the procedure considerably and allows us to use a minimally invasive technique.

How We Plan Your Full Mouth Dental Implant Procedure

Before beginning your full mouth dental implant procedure, we take detailed CBCT scans to evaluate the condition of your jawbone and soft tissues. These 3D images help us plan exactly how many implants you will need and determine the best type of restoration to match your bite, bone support, and long-term goals.

Based on your case, we may recommend a fixed zirconia bridge or a removable overdenture .

Since we place mini dental implants using a minimally invasive technique, the procedure is typically faster and more efficient than traditional implant placement. We usually apply only a local anesthetic because we do not need to make incisions or lift the gum tissue. Instead, we create a small pilot opening through the gum and into the bone, then gently place and secure the implant.
