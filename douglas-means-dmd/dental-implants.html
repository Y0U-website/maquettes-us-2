Steps to Dental Implant Placement

Dental implant placement does take a bit of time. The dentist will assess the condition of your mouth, jaws, and existing teeth, as well as get several x-rays to get an internal look. If there is not sufficient bone available to anchor the implant, bone grafts may be necessary, which will require additional surgery.

During implant placement, local anesthesia, general anesthesia, or some form of sedation may be used to get you prepared for implant placement. Incisions are made in the soft tissue, and the implant is screwed into the jaw bone. The crown or prosthetic tooth will be placed on the implant soon after, and you will be closely monitored to make sure the implant fuses properly with the bone and your soft tissue properly heals around the newly placed unit.
