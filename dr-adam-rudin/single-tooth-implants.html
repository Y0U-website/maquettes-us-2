What Is a Single-Tooth Dental Implant?

A single tooth dental implant is a three-part restoration: a titanium post surgically placed into the jawbone, an abutment that connects the post to the surface, and a custom-crafted crown that matches your natural teeth. Unlike a dental bridge, an implant doesn’t rely on neighboring teeth for support. It stands entirely on its own, and that independence is one of its greatest strengths.

The titanium post integrates directly with the bone through a process called osseointegration, effectively becoming part of your jaw over time. This not only creates a remarkably stable foundation but also helps prevent the bone loss that typically follows tooth extraction. Research published on the National Institutes of Health database found single-tooth implants achieved a 95.6 % survival rate after 38 to 40 years in function, a remarkably long-term outcome that speaks to the durability of the treatment.

Why Choose a Single Implant Over Other Restorative Options?

A single tooth implant is widely considered the gold standard for tooth replacement, and for good reason. Because it functions like a natural tooth root, it preserves the surrounding bone and prevents the shifting of adjacent teeth that often follows a gap. It also requires no alteration to healthy neighboring teeth, unlike a traditional bridge.

Single implants are also far easier to maintain than removable partials. You brush and floss around an implant exactly as you would a natural tooth, with no removal, no adhesives, and no separate cleaning routine. For most patients, the long-term value and minimal upkeep make the investment in a single implant a no-brainer. Patients curious about restorative dentistry options at the practice will find that implants fit naturally alongside other solutions like crowns and bridges, depending on each individual’s needs.

Am I a Candidate for a Single Tooth Implant? Most adults in good general health are strong candidates for a single tooth implant. The most important factor is adequate bone volume at the implant site. In cases where bone loss has occurred following tooth extraction, a bone graft may be needed before implant placement, a procedure Dr. Rudin performs in-house as part of the practice’s comprehensive care model.

Systemic health conditions like controlled diabetes or a history of smoking may require additional evaluation, but they don’t automatically disqualify a patient. Dr. Rudin takes time to understand each patient’s full medical picture before making any recommendations.
