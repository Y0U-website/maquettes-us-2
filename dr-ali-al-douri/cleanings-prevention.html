The Cleaning Process

Comprehensive Examination

Plaque and Tartar Removal

Using specialized instruments, our hygienist carefully removes plaque and hardened tartar (calculus) from the surfaces of your teeth, especially near the gum line.

Polishing and Flossing

Once the tartar is removed, we polish your teeth with a high-powered brush and gritty toothpaste to remove surface stains, followed by professional flossing.

Fluoride Treatment (Optional)

Depending on your needs, we may apply a fluoride treatment to strengthen your enamel and protect against future decay.

Is Preventive Care Right For You?

Absolutely. Everyone, from young children getting their first teeth to seniors, requires regular preventive dental care. Maintaining routine cleanings every six months is the single most effective way to avoid painful dental emergencies, complex procedures, and unnecessary costs in the future. If you haven&#x27;t been to the dentist in a while, there is no judgment here—our goal is simply to get your oral health back on track.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about dental cleanings and preventive care.

For most patients, we recommend a professional dental cleaning every six months. However, if you have a history of periodontal (gum) disease, a high rate of cavity formation, or other specific oral health conditions, we may suggest coming in every three to four months. Regular visits allow us to monitor your oral health closely and address any potential issues before they become more serious.

A regular cleaning (prophylaxis) focuses on removing plaque and tartar from above the gum line and is meant for maintaining healthy gums. A deep cleaning, also known as scaling and root planing, is a specialized procedure for treating gum disease. It involves removing tartar from below the gum line and smoothing the root surfaces to help the gums reattach to the teeth.

Bleeding gums are often an early sign of gingivitis, the mildest form of gum disease. It usually indicates that plaque has accumulated along the gum line, causing inflammation. If your gums bleed regularly, it&#x27;s crucial to schedule a cleaning and exam. Improved home care and professional cleanings can typically reverse gingivitis before it progresses to more severe periodontal disease.

Yes, dental x-rays are a vital diagnostic tool. They allow us to see what&#x27;s happening beneath the surface of your teeth and gums—areas that are invisible during a visual exam. X-rays help us detect cavities between teeth, bone loss associated with gum disease, impacted teeth, and even tumors or cysts. We use low-radiation digital x-rays for your safety.

While fluoride is often associated with pediatric dentistry, it is highly beneficial for adults as well. Fluoride helps remineralize weakened tooth enamel, making it more resistant to decay. We may recommend professional fluoride treatments for adults who are prone to cavities, have dry mouth (which increases decay risk), or have exposed tooth roots due to gum recession.

Preventing cavities requires a combination of good home care and smart dietary choices. Brush twice a day with a fluoride toothpaste, floss daily to remove plaque between teeth, and limit sugary or acidic foods and beverages. Drinking plenty of water also helps wash away food particles and bacteria. Staying consistent with your routine is the best defense against tooth decay.
