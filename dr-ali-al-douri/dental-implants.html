The Implant Process

Initial Consultation & Imaging

Surgical Placement

The titanium implant is gently and precisely placed into the jawbone. We ensure your complete comfort using local anesthesia and sedation if requested.

Healing (Osseointegration)

Over the next 3-6 months, the jawbone naturally heals around the implant, securing it firmly in place. A temporary crown may be provided during this time.

Restoration Placement

Once fully healed, an abutment is attached, and your custom-designed porcelain crown is securely placed, completing your new smile.

Are Implants Right For You?

If you are missing one or more teeth and desire a permanent, hassle-free solution that looks and feels like your natural teeth, implants are likely an excellent choice. Good candidates generally have healthy gums and sufficient bone density to support the implant. However, even if you have experienced bone loss, preliminary procedures like bone grafting can often make you a successful candidate.

Frequently Asked Questions

With proper care and maintenance, dental implants are designed to last a lifetime. The titanium post fuses with the jawbone, providing a permanent foundation. The crown attached to the implant may need replacement after 10 to 15 years due to normal wear and tear, but excellent oral hygiene can extend its lifespan.

Most patients report that getting a dental implant is less uncomfortable than having a tooth extracted. We use local anesthesia to ensure you feel no pain during the procedure. Post-operative discomfort is typically mild and can usually be managed with over-the-counter pain relievers.

The entire process usually takes several months from start to finish. After the implant is placed in the jawbone, it needs time to heal and fuse with the bone (osseointegration), which typically takes 3 to 6 months. Once healed, an abutment and custom crown are attached.

Ideal candidates for dental implants are in good general and oral health, have adequate bone in their jaw to support the implant, and have healthy gum tissues free of periodontal disease. Even if you have experienced some bone loss, procedures like bone grafting may still make implants an option for you.

Yes! Implants are highly versatile. A single implant can replace one tooth, while multiple implants can support a dental bridge to replace several consecutive missing teeth. They can also be used to anchor a full set of dentures (implant-supported dentures) for a secure, slip-free fit.

You care for dental implants exactly as you would your natural teeth. This includes brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and attending regular dental checkups and professional cleanings. Good oral hygiene is crucial to prevent infection around the implant.

Absolutely. The crown attached to your implant is custom-made to match the color, shape, and size of your surrounding natural teeth. Once placed, it is virtually indistinguishable from your other teeth, both in appearance and function.

Dental implants have a very high success rate, typically around 95 % to 98 %. Success depends on various factors, including the location of the implant in the jaw, the patient&#x27;s oral hygiene habits, and their overall health. Smoking, for example, can lower the success rate.
