Is Full Arch Restoration Right For You?

If you are tired of the daily struggles of traditional dentures—slipping, discomfort, messy adhesives, and diet restrictions—or if you have multiple failing teeth and face total tooth loss, full arch implants are an incredible solution. Because of the angled implant technique, even patients who have been told they lack sufficient bone density for standard implants are often excellent candidates for this procedure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about full arch dental implants.

Traditional dentures rest on top of the gums and rely on natural suction or messy adhesives to stay in place, which can often lead to slipping while talking or eating. Full arch implants are securely anchored to your jawbone using 4 to 6 titanium posts. They do not slip, do not require adhesives, provide a significantly stronger bite force, and feel much more like natural teeth.

Most patients who have lost all or most of their teeth are good candidates for full arch implants. Even if you have been told in the past that you do not have enough bone for traditional implants, the All-on-4® technique angles the implants to maximize the use of available bone, often eliminating the need for bone grafting.

In many cases, yes. We can often place a temporary, functional set of teeth on the same day the implants are placed. This means you will leave our office with a complete smile. Once the implants have fully healed and integrated with the bone, your final, permanent bridge will be attached.

Initial recovery from the surgical placement usually takes a few days, during which you may experience mild swelling and discomfort, easily managed with medication. However, the complete healing process (osseointegration) takes a few months. During this healing phase, you will follow a softer diet to avoid putting too much stress on the implants.

Maintenance is very similar to caring for natural teeth. You will brush them twice daily using a soft-bristled toothbrush and use a water flosser or specialized floss to clean underneath the bridge. Regular dental checkups and professional cleanings are still essential to maintain the health of the surrounding gum tissue.

The surgery is performed under local anesthesia, and we offer sedation dentistry options to ensure you are completely comfortable and relaxed during the procedure. Post-operative discomfort is generally mild and easily controlled with pain medication.

The titanium implant posts are designed to be a permanent, lifelong solution. The customized prosthetic bridge attached to them is incredibly durable, but may eventually require replacement after 10-15 years due to normal wear, depending on your habits and care routine.
