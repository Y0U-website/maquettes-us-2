Is All-on-4 Dental Implants Right for You?

All-on-4 Dental Implants is a good fit if you are:

  • Adults with multiple failing teeth or a full arch of missing teeth
  • Patients with ill-fitting or uncomfortable traditional dentures
  • Patients with moderate bone loss who want to avoid grafting
  • Healthy adults looking for a permanent, non-removable solution
  • Patients with severe uncontrolled diabetes or other conditions that impair healing
  • Heavy smokers (implant failure rates are significantly higher)
  • Patients with severe bruxism without commitment to night guard use
  • Patients who cannot commit to regular maintenance visits

What to Expect During Your All-on-4 Dental Implants Visit

Here is exactly what happens at your appointment at Goldberg Marcus Monteiro Dental Associates. Knowing each step makes the experience far less intimidating — and lets you relax knowing what comes next.

  • 1. CBCT scan and planning. Your dentist takes a 3D scan of your jaw and digitally plans the exact position, angle, and depth of each of the four implants — avoiding nerves and sinuses.
  • 2. Surgery day. You arrive, get numbed with local anesthetic (and optional sedation), and your dentist places four titanium implants — two straight, two angled — in the denser anterior bone. Most of the surgery is pressure and vibration, not pain.
  • 3. Same-day temporary bridge. Before you leave, a provisional acrylic bridge is attached to your new implants. You walk out with teeth that day — no gap, no denture.
  • 4. Healing phase. Over the next 3–4 months, the implants fuse with the bone (osseointegration). Soft foods are recommended. You return for check-ups to monitor healing.
  • 5. Final zirconia bridge. Once healing is complete, the temporary is swapped for a final zirconia or porcelain bridge that matches your natural smile — durable, lifelike, and built to last.

Risks and Considerations of All-on-4 Dental Implants

Every procedure has tradeoffs, and transparent conversation about them is part of informed consent. Your dentist reviews these at your consultation and answers every question before treatment begins.

  • Surgical infection at the implant site
  • Implant failure (failure to osseointegrate) — rare
  • Nerve irritation or injury (minimized by CBCT planning)
  • Sinus involvement for upper-arch implants
  • Peri-implantitis (bone loss around the implant) if oral hygiene is neglected
  • Prosthetic wear or fracture over many years of use
