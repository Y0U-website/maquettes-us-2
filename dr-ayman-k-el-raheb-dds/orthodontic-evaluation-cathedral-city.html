Orthodontic Evaluation in Cathedral City, CA

The first step toward a straighter smile — for children by age seven, and for adults at any age.

Crooked teeth, a noticeable overbite, or crowding that makes flossing difficult affect both appearance and oral health. For patients considering an orthodontic evaluation in Cathedral City, CA, below is what the evaluation covers, when to schedule one, and which treatment options may follow.

What an Orthodontic Evaluation Is

An orthodontic evaluation is a comprehensive assessment of the teeth, jaws, and bite alignment. It determines whether orthodontic treatment is needed and, if so, what kind of correction would be most effective. Since every patient’s anatomy is different, the evaluation is the clinical foundation for a personalized plan.

What the Evaluation Includes

A thorough evaluation typically involves a visual examination of tooth alignment and spacing, dental x-rays to assess root positions and jaw structure, photographs of the teeth and face, and impressions or digital scans of the dental arches. Those records let the dentist analyze the relationship between the upper and lower jaws, identify skeletal or dental irregularities, and map the most efficient path to correction.

Concerns Assessed During an Evaluation

Crowding and Spacing

Crowding occurs when the jaw does not have room to accommodate all the teeth in proper alignment, causing them to overlap, rotate, or emerge in irregular positions. Spacing problems are the opposite situation. Both affect chewing efficiency and make certain areas harder to clean, which is often what makes intervention worthwhile.

Bite Alignment Issues

Malocclusion takes several forms. An overbite means the upper front teeth extend too far over the lower front teeth; an underbite positions the lower jaw ahead of the upper; a crossbite has upper teeth sitting inside the lower teeth on one or both sides; an open bite leaves a gap between the upper and lower teeth when the mouth is closed. Untreated bite problems contribute to jaw pain, uneven wear, and difficulty chewing, so identifying them early allows more conservative correction.

When to Schedule an Evaluation

Children and Adolescents

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic evaluation by age seven. The jaw is still developing at that stage, and conditions such as crossbites or severe crowding can be intercepted early — addressing skeletal issues during growth can simplify, or sometimes eliminate, more complex treatment later. Evaluations often follow naturally from a pediatric dental exam .

Adults

There is no upper age limit for orthodontic treatment. Adults who were never treated as children, or whose teeth have relapsed after previous orthodontic work, benefit from a current evaluation. Clear aligners such as Invisalign have made treatment considerably more discreet for adults.

Treatment Options That May Follow

Invisalign Clear Aligners

Invisalign uses a series of custom-made clear trays to shift teeth gradually into position. The aligners are removable, virtually invisible, and replaced roughly every one to two weeks as treatment progresses. With no metal brackets or wires, many patients find the experience more comfortable and convenient than traditional braces.

Traditional Braces

Traditional braces use metal brackets bonded to the teeth and connected by archwires that apply controlled pressure over time. They remain the most effective option for complex cases — severe crowding, significant bite discrepancies, and situations requiring precise tooth rotation — and modern brackets are smaller and more comfortable than earlier versions.

Early Intervention Appliances

For younger patients with developing jaws, the dentist may recommend interceptive appliances such as palatal expanders, space maintainers, or partial braces. These guide jaw growth and create room for permanent teeth to emerge properly, reducing the likelihood of more extensive treatment later.

The Benefits of Early Intervention

Treating orthodontic problems early can reduce both the complexity and duration of treatment. Interceptive care during childhood guides jaw growth, creates space for emerging teeth, and corrects harmful habits such as thumb sucking before they cause permanent change. Because the jaw and facial bones are still developing, early intervention sometimes avoids the extractions or jaw surgery that would otherwise become necessary. Not every child needs early treatment — the evaluation is what settles the question.

What Happens After the Evaluation

Treatment Planning Discussion

The dentist reviews the specific alignment or bite issues identified, the recommended approach, the estimated duration, and the expected outcome. This is the point to ask questions and raise preferences about treatment type, timeline, and cost.

Beginning Treatment

Next steps depend on the option chosen. Invisalign patients receive their custom aligners after digital impressions go to the lab; patients choosing braces schedule a bonding appointment for bracket placement. Once active treatment finishes, retainers hold the result in place.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an orthodontic evaluation cost in Cathedral City?

Does insurance usually cover orthodontic evaluations?

Plans that include orthodontic benefits typically cover the initial evaluation as part of the diagnostic process. Coverage levels vary, so verify your specific benefits with your provider.

Can new patients schedule an orthodontic evaluation?

Yes. No referral is required, and the evaluation can serve as your introduction to the practice.

What happens during an orthodontic evaluation appointment?

It usually lasts 30 to 60 minutes and includes x-rays, photographs, impressions or digital scans, and a discussion of the findings. You leave with a clear understanding of your alignment and any recommended next steps.

How do I know if I need an orthodontic evaluation?

Crowded or crooked teeth, difficulty biting or chewing, jaw pain, uneven tooth wear, and dissatisfaction with your smile are all common reasons. Parents should consider an evaluation for children by age seven, when early assessment is most useful.
