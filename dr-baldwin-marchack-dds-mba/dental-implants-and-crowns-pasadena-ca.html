What is a Core Buildup for a Dental Crown? Pasadena Prosthodontics Explains

W hen you need a dental crown , you may wonder what the next steps are if your tooth doesn’t have enough structure. That is where a core buildup becomes essential. This procedure rebuilds part of your tooth, providing a stable foundation to anchor your crown. At Pasadena Prosthodontics, we do everything we can to save your tooth. When is a core buildup necessary?

When a tooth is too damaged because of decay, accident, or a large filling, you may need a dental crown to restore your smile’s function and appearance. However, sometimes the damage may be too severe to support a crown. In such cases, the dentist recommends a core buildup as an essential part of preparing for a crown.

Traditionally, amalgam or other metal-based materials were the go-to materials for a core buildup. However, since metal acts mechanically rather than by adhesion, it requires invasive tooth preparation to achieve better retention. Your Pasadena dentist uses composites to create a core buildup. Unlike metal, resin-based core buildup helps preserve more of the natural tooth because they act through adhesion. In addition, the material can be prepared and used on the same day, leading to a fast turnaround.

Sometimes, your tooth may need a post if a substantial portion of the tooth is missing, but the root canal is intact. Your dentist adds a post to your tooth to support the core buildup. The ability to take advantage of the canal adds more support for the new foundation.

Do I need a core buildup for an implant crown?

No! Typically, you do not need a core buildup for an implant crown. That’s because dental implants work differently. The crown is attached to an implant post (via an abutment), which acts as an artificial root, so there’s no need to build up existing tooth structure.

Preparatory procedures for dental implants focus on rebuilding the jawbone. For instance, if you have significant jawbone loss, we will recommend a bone graft to replenish lost tissue, creating a sufficient foundation to anchor the implant. A thorough evaluation with X-rays and scans will determine what’s needed for stable, long-lasting results.
