How Is Socket Preservation Done?

Socket preservation is a minimally invasive bone grafting procedure. This treatment involves rehabilitating the empty socket using a biocompatible grafting material, which may include bone from the patient, a donor, or synthetic bone. These materials take on the role of a scaffold, upholding the structure and volume of the natural bone to encourage bone growth.

The Procedure

The socket preservation bone graft procedure is typically quick and well-tolerated with minimal downtime. At Dr. Batz & Weiner Family Dentistry, the socket preservation process includes:

  • Tooth extraction
  • Placement of grafting material
  • Application of a protective membrane to keep the grafting material in place.
  • Suturing of the socket to encourage expedited healing

The Benefits of Socket Preservation

Socket preservation is an investment in sustainable bone integrity and a healthy smile. The key benefits of this procedure include:

  • Prevent jawbone atrophy and the associated complications
  • Achieve a stable base for dental implants
  • Stabilize adjacent teeth to avoid shifting and/or misalignment
  • Minimize the need for recurring restorative treatments

The Impact of Socket Preservation on Oral Health

Socket preservation can transform the future of your smile. By prioritizing proactive socket preservation treatment, you can maintain the integrity of your teeth, gums, and bite. This keeps challenging side effects, such as bite misalignment and changes in chewing and speaking abilities, at bay while deterring further oral health complications.

Is Socket Preservation Necessary?

Tooth extraction doesn’t always require socket preservation. However, in some cases, socket preservation is a prerequisite to future treatments. This procedure is necessary to prepare for dental implant placement but may also be required for individuals facing multiple extractions with a higher risk of bone atrophy.
