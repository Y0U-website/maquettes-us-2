What to Expect During the Dental Implant Procedure

The dental implant procedure follows a systematic approach designed to ensure optimal results:

Consultation and Oral Health Evaluation: The process begins with an examination where we assess your oral health, discuss your goals, and review your medical history. We check for any conditions that could affect treatment success.

Digital Imaging and Treatment Planning: Advanced 3D imaging technology maps your jaw structure, allowing precise implant placement planning. We determine the optimal implant size, angle, and position for your specific anatomy.

Dental Implant Placement: During a surgical procedure performed under local anesthesia or sedation, the titanium implant post is carefully placed into your jawbone. The gum tissue is then sutured closed over or around the implant.

Healing and Integration with the Jawbone: Over the next several months, osseointegration occurs as your bone grows around the implant, creating a secure bond. This healing period is crucial for long-term implant success.

Final Crown or Restoration Placement: Once integration is complete, an abutment (connector piece) is attached to the implant, and your custom crown, bridge, or denture is secured. Your restoration is carefully matched to your natural teeth in color, shape, and size.

How Long Does Dental Implant Surgery Take?

Dental implant surgery timing varies by case complexity. Placing a single tooth implant often takes about one to two hours, depending on the case. Multiple implants require longer appointments proportional to the number being placed.

Designed for long-term tooth replacement - With proper care, dental implants can last for many years, and some may last decades. The healing period between implant placement and final restoration accounts for most of this time, as osseointegration cannot be rushed. Factors affecting timeline include bone quality and density, whether bone grafting is needed, your overall health and healing capacity, and the number of implants being placed.

FAQs about Dental Implants

  • Do dental implants hurt? The dental implant procedure is usually not painful because it is performed under local anesthesia, and some patients may also receive sedation. You may feel pressure during placement, but no pain. After surgery, some discomfort, swelling, and minor bleeding are normal for a few days and can be managed with prescribed or over-the-counter pain medication. Most patients report that recovery is less uncomfortable than they expected.
  • How long do dental implants last? With proper care, dental implants can last for many years, and some may last decades. The implant post itself, made of titanium, can remain functional indefinitely when well-maintained. The crown attached to the implant may need replacement over time because of normal wear. Success depends on good oral hygiene, regular dental checkups, avoiding smoking, and addressing teeth grinding if present.
  • How much do dental implants cost? The cost of a dental implant varies based on the number of implants needed, whether bone grafting or other preparatory treatment is required, the type of restoration, and your location. Multiple implants or full arch restorations cost more. Factors affecting price include the number of implants needed, whether bone grafting is required, the type of restoration, and your geographic location. We provide detailed cost estimates during your consultation.
  • Does dental insurance cover implants? Some dental insurance plans provide partial coverage for dental implants, but coverage amounts, deductibles, and annual maximums vary by plan. Some plans may limit or exclude coverage for implants. We help patients verify their coverage and maximize available benefits.
  • What is the recovery process for a dental implant? Initial healing from implant surgery takes about 7-10 days. You may experience swelling, bruising, and mild discomfort during this time. Stick to soft foods for the first week and avoid disturbing the surgical site. Complete osseointegration often takes several months, during which the implant fuses with your jawbone. You can usually return to normal activities within a few days of surgery.
  • How do you care for a dental implant? Care for dental implants just as you would natural teeth. Brush twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush, floss daily using regular floss or interdental brushes designed for implants, use an antimicrobial mouthwash if recommended, avoid smoking, which can compromise implant success, and attend regular dental checkups and professional cleanings.
  • How many teeth can be replaced with dental implants? A dental implant can replace anywhere from one single tooth, to an entire arch of teeth.
