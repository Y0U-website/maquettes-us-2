What is Sleep Apnea?

Sleep Apnea is a condition that affects more than 30 million Americans, and is explained as a partial or complete collapse of the upper airway during sleep (i.e. the nose and throat).

Sleep Apnea has become a somewhat common sleep disorder, yet, it is under-diagnosed and oftentimes patients are unaware that they may have a sleep or airway condition. It is increasingly recognized as an important cause of many health problems in the United States, including stroke.

  • What causes sleep apnea? Sleep Apnea is a partial or complete collapse of the upper airway (i.e. nose and throat). The hallmark cause of sleep apnea has been being overweight and snoring. These days, further research regarding sleep disorder breathing conditions have shown that narrow upper jaws, narrow sinuses or throat, hormone changes related to menopause, atrial fibrilation, mouth breathing, alcohol, large neck size, smoking, and aging are all potential causes of sleep apnea.
  • Are there severities of sleep apnea? Sleep Apnea has 3 stages and its severity is defined by the number of apneas that an individual has per hour. An apnea is marked by a cessation of breathing for 10 seconds or more. Normal: 5 apneas per hour Mild: 5-14 apneas per hour Moderate: 15-29 apneas per hour Severe: 30+ apneas per hour
