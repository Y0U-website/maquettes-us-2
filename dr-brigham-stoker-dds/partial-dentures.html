Who Is a Good Candidate for a Removable Partial Denture?

A removable partial denture works well for patients who have lost some teeth in an arch but still have several healthy natural teeth to anchor the partial. You may be a good candidate if you want to restore function and appearance without surgery, or if you are not a candidate for dental implants due to health considerations or bone density. During your consultation, Dr. Brigham Stoker will evaluate your oral health and discuss whether a partial denture or another tooth replacement option is the best path forward.

What to Expect During the Partial Denture Process

Initial Consultation and Impressions

Your first visit includes a thorough exam with digital X-rays to assess your remaining teeth and gum tissue. Dr. Stoker will take precise impressions of your mouth, which serve as the foundation for your custom partial. If any teeth need treatment before the partial is made — such as a filling or crown — that work can be completed in-house as well.

Fitting, Adjustments, and Aftercare

Once your partial denture is ready, you will return for a fitting appointment. Dr. Stoker checks the bite, comfort, and appearance, making any necessary adjustments on the spot. Most patients need a brief adjustment period as they get used to wearing the partial. Follow-up visits ensure the fit stays comfortable over time.

Partial Dentures vs. Dental Bridges

Both partial dentures and dental bridges replace missing teeth, but they work differently. A removable partial denture clasps onto existing teeth and can be taken out for cleaning. A bridge is permanently cemented to adjacent teeth and stays in place at all times.

If you are unsure which option is right for you, Dr. Stoker can walk you through both during your consultation. For patients interested in a more permanent solution, implant-supported dentures or full dentures may also be worth exploring.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do partial dentures last?

With proper care, a well-made partial denture may last five to ten years or longer. Normal wear and changes in your mouth over time may require adjustments, relining, or eventual replacement. Regular checkups help us monitor the fit and condition of your partial.

Can I eat normally with a removable partial denture?

Most patients adjust to eating with a partial denture within a few weeks. Starting with softer foods and cutting items into smaller pieces helps during the transition. Over time, many patients find they can enjoy a wide variety of foods comfortably.

How do I care for my partial dentures?

Remove your partial daily and brush it gently with a soft-bristle brush and denture cleaner — avoid regular toothpaste, which can be abrasive. Soak the partial overnight in a denture solution to keep it moist and clean. Continue brushing your natural teeth and gums as usual.

What happens if I don’t replace missing teeth?

Leaving gaps may cause surrounding teeth to shift out of alignment, leading to bite problems and potential additional tooth loss. Missing teeth also contribute to bone loss in the jaw and can make chewing and speaking more difficult. Replacing missing teeth promptly helps preserve your oral health and facial structure.
