Single Tooth Implant Salt Lake…

For patients who value long-term oral health and want to avoid altering healthy teeth, a single dental implant is often the stronger choice.

  • Impact on adjacent teeth: A bridge requires the teeth on either side of the gap to be permanently altered to support the restoration. An implant stands on its own and leaves neighboring teeth intact.
  • Bone health: Only an implant stimulates the jawbone the way a natural root does. A bridge sits above the gumline and does not prevent the bone loss that follows tooth removal.
  • Maintenance: An implant is cared for just like a natural tooth — regular brushing and flossing. A bridge requires threaders or special floss to clean underneath the false tooth.

What Does a Single Tooth Implant Cost?

We understand that single tooth implant cost is one of the first questions patients ask, and we believe you deserve a straightforward answer. The total investment depends on several factors specific to your situation.

Factors That Affect Tooth Implant Cost

Dr. Stoker will provide a detailed cost breakdown during your consultation so there are no surprises.

  • Bone density: If bone loss has occurred, a bone grafting procedure may be needed before the implant can be placed, which adds to the overall cost.
  • Location of the missing tooth: Front teeth and molars may require different implant sizes and crown materials.
  • Crown material: The type of crown selected — porcelain, zirconia, or porcelain-fused-to-metal — influences the final price.
  • Sedation preferences: Sedation options such as oral conscious sedation or nitrous oxide are available and may carry an additional fee.

What to Expect During the Single Tooth Implant Process

The entire process is completed in-house at Stoker Dental & Orthodontics, typically over two to three visits spread across several months.

Consultation and CT-Guided Treatment Planning

Your first visit includes a comprehensive exam and a Cone Beam CT scan, which gives Dr. Stoker a detailed 3D view of your jawbone, nerves, and surrounding structures. This imaging allows for computer-guided implant planning — precise positioning that helps improve outcomes and reduce healing time.

Implant Placement and Healing

During the placement appointment, the titanium implant post is surgically positioned into the jawbone. Most patients are comfortable with local anesthesia, though sedation is available for those who prefer it. Over the following three to six months, the implant fuses with the bone through a process called osseointegration, creating a stable foundation for the crown.

Custom Crown Attachment

Once healing is complete, a custom crown is fabricated to match the color, shape, and size of your natural teeth. The crown is attached to the implant via a small connector called an abutment. The result is a restoration that blends seamlessly with your smile and is built to handle everyday chewing forces.

Frequently Asked Questions

Am I a good candidate for a single dental implant?

Most adults with a missing or failing tooth and adequate jawbone density are good candidates. During your consultation, Dr. Stoker will evaluate your bone structure using Cone Beam CT imaging to confirm whether an implant is right for you. If bone loss is present, a bone grafting procedure may help build the foundation needed for a successful implant.

How long does a single tooth implant last?

With proper care, a dental implant post is designed to last a lifetime. The custom crown typically lasts 15 years or longer before it may need replacement. Regular checkups and good oral hygiene help maximize the lifespan of both the implant and the crown.

Is the implant procedure painful?

Most patients report less discomfort than expected. Local anesthesia is used during placement, and oral conscious sedation or nitrous oxide is available if you experience dental anxiety. Post-procedure soreness is typically manageable with over-the-counter medication and resolves within a few days.

What happens if I don&#x27;t replace a missing tooth?

Leaving a gap where a tooth was lost can lead to jawbone deterioration, shifting of surrounding teeth, bite misalignment, and changes to your facial structure over time. Replacing the tooth promptly helps preserve your oral health and may prevent more complex and costly treatment later.
