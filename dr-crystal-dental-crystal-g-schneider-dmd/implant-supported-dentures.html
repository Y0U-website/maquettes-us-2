The Procedure for Receiving Implant Supported Dentures

With this temporary denture, we can also determine the ideal position for artificial crowns in the permanent denture. When the temporary denture is finished, we can also use a copy of it as a guide to direct the implants into the precise positions. It can take about four appointments for the entire completion.

We will now administer a local anesthetic to numb the immediate area for implant placements. Next, we will create small incisions and proceed to surgically place each tiny titanium post into your jawbone. We will close the sites with sutures.

Dr. Crystal Dental will wait several months to allow the jawbone and the implants to integrate or attach and fuse together.

After we have confirmed with X-rays that the implants have become fused with the jawbone, the second surgery is scheduled. This surgery is much simpler than the first. A small incision is again made to expose the tops of the implants. We then attach an abutment or extension to the titanium post, so it reaches the surface of your gumline. There is minimal gum healing and then we will make a final impression of your gums and the abutments. This is the roadmap for the final denture framework, either ball or bar, specific and precise locations.

With the final denture now fabricated, we may have some minor adjustments at your final fitting for either a bar-retained or a ball-retained final implant supported denture.

The Aftercare of Your Implant Supported Dentures

Dr. Crystal Dental will strongly suggest for you to remove your dentures at night and for proper cleaning. We would also like you to carefully clean around the attachments.

We will test the appliance to make sure it is secure. Even though your new denture is stable, it could still move slightly when you chew. This slight movement might cause minor rubbing against your gums, which can cause sore spots. We will check your gums and will check your bite after the insertion of the final denture.

The clips or other attachments on your dentures will need to be replaced every 6 to 12 months. They are made of a plastic material (nylon) and will show wear with continued use. This is an expensive and simple procedure to replace these over time, and ensure a well fitting prosthetic for years to come.
