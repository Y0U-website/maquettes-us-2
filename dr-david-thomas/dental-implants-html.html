What is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a titanium post that can be placed below your gumline to restore the root structure of a missing tooth. Since it’s made from a biocompatible metal, it’s able to naturally fuse with the jawbone through a process called osseointegration once it’s inserted. This allows it to provide the necessary blood flow to your jawbone and unmatched support for your restoration. Once the gums have healed after implant placement, we can attach a customized dental bridge, crown, or denture to it, completing your smile.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Due to their realistic structure resembling a natural tooth, dental implants offer a wide array of unique benefits, making them a popular tooth replacement choice among patients. Implants are completely natural-looking and feeling, which means it shouldn’t take you any time to adjust to them and others should not be able to tell your teeth are prosthetic. Compared to other replacement options, implants come the closest to giving you your real teeth back!

Day-to-Day Benefits

If you’ve spent any amount of time in dentures, you know how frustrating and difficult they can be while you’re trying to eat. They can slip out of place even while you’re simply talking! This instability can make you feel self-conscious. However, dental implants hold your replacement teeth firmly where they belong. Consequently, you can talk, laugh, eat, and smile in front of others without worrying about awkwardness from your teeth.

Also, dental implants, unlike dentures, require easy upkeep. You just maintain them as you would real teeth, which means you will need to brush and floss every day. In addition, you will need to come to Seahurst Park Dentistry at least every six months for a checkup and cleaning. It’s really that simple!

Health Benefits

Did you know that the moment a tooth root is gone, the jawbone starts to lose strength and density? This process is called resorption, and traditional dentures do nothing to stop it, which is why many denture wearers still have a sunken, older look. On the other hand, implants keep the jawbone strong and preserved.

With dental implants, you can enjoy not only better oral health but also overall health. Being able to eat basically anything, you can make more nutritious choices in your diet, which leads to better digestion and well-being.

Long-Term Benefits

About every couple of years, five at most, traditional dentures have to be refitted and replaced because of the ever-changing jawbone. Even normal bridges require frequent replacement about every ten years. However, dental implants have a lifespan of more than 30 years! In fact, there is about a 98 percent success rate even 20 years after the implants have been put in.

When you calculate the cost of treatment over time, the more traditional tooth replacements can add up to quite a bit of money. In fact, you may even find that dental implants can actually help you save in the long run!

Missing 1 Tooth

Even one missing tooth can lead to serious problems if left untreated. Eating and talking can become increasingly difficult, and you will likely suffer additional bone and tooth loss the longer you wait to address it. Fortunately, we can fill the gap left by a single missing tooth by placing one dental implant in the area below your gums. Once you’ve healed and the implant has fused with the jawbone, we’ll attach a customized dental crown to the post to complete your arch of teeth.

Missing Multiple Teeth

If you’re missing multiple teeth in a row, instead of getting a traditional bridge that alters the structure of your existing healthy teeth, you can get an implant bridge. Rather than being secured to healthy natural teeth, the prosthetic will be attached to two dental implants located on either side of the gap in your smile. And if you are missing multiple teeth that are not next to each other, an implant partial can be created to fit like a puzzle piece on top of your implants, eliminating the need for metal clasps or clips.

Missing All Teeth

To replace an entire arch of missing teeth, we’ll place anywhere from four to eight dental implants below the gums. These will secure your customized full denture, eliminating any instability like shifting and slipping. Implant dentures are far greater stability, functionality, and longevity. Not to mention, you’ll never need to worry about paying for dental adhesives, special cleaning supplies, or seeing your implant dentist for regular adjustments or replacements.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

After the implants have been surgically positioned in the jawbone, your mouth will need time to recover. More specifically, the jawbone will need to grow and surround the implant posts in a process called osseointegration.

Once this part of the treatment is complete and you’ve healed, our team will take over from there by attaching the abutments, which are connective pieces between the post and the restoration. In this quick procedure, Dr. Thomas will make a very small incision in the gums and insert the abutment into the implant with a tiny portion of the abutment protruding above the gumline.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

A couple of weeks after the abutments have been placed, we can focus on creating your prosthetics that will complete your smile. We will take impressions of your mouth and send them to our dental lab, where your implant-retained crown, bridge, or denture will be fabricated. Finally, when they are finished, you’ll return to our office and have them permanently bonded to your implants, making your smile whole again.

Making Oral Hygiene a Priority

Your dental implants cannot decay; however, the natural teeth surrounding them certainly can. Also, your soft oral tissues can be at risk for gum disease if you do not commit to practicing good oral hygiene at home. Brushing twice a day for two minutes, flossing at least once a day, and rinsing with an ADA-approved mouthwash will ensure that your regular teeth and dental implants in Burien remain healthy and fully intact for the future.

Breaking Bad Habits

Everyone has bad habits they need to break. If yours is smoking, chewing on non-food items, biting your fingernails, or using your teeth as tools, you’ll want to make sure you quit long before you ever receive dental implants. Your implant dentist in Burien will explain why this is important, but if you want to keep from damaging your artificial teeth and spending additional time and money on replacing what are supposed to be permanent prosthetics, you’ll give up your bad habits sooner than you think.

Whether it is your pearly whites grinding against each other while you sleep or the threat of an accidental fall while out for a run, a mouthguard is one of the best forms of mouth protection you can invest in to safeguard your smile.

Is Getting Dental Implants Painful?

As with any surgical procedure, with dental implants you’re likely to experience at least a bit of discomfort. However, it usually can be managed with over-the-counter pain reliever. Your oral surgeon will give you more detailed aftercare instructions for smoother healing. You’ll need a few days after the placement procedure to recover, and you’ll need to eat soft food for a while. Once your restoration is bonded in place, however, your mouth should feel pretty natural!

Is There Anything I Shouldn’t Eat with Dental Implants?

In the immediate aftermath of your placement procedure, you should stay away from hard, crunchy, or chewy foods and have softer options like scrambled eggs or oatmeal. Again, your oral surgeon will likely provide more detailed instructions for what you should and shouldn’t eat following the procedure. However, once you fully recover from the surgery and your implant is restored, you are free to eat basically anything you would with real teeth!

What Can Cause Dental Implants to Fail?

The two main causes of implant failure are an infection called peri-implantitis, which is a form of gum disease, and failed integration with the jawbone, which occurs within a few months of the placement procedure. Other culprits include chronic teeth grinding, smoking, and certain medications or conditions. That’s why Dr. Thomas gets your medical history and makes sure you understand dental implants before moving forward with treatment. We also emphasize the importance of maintaining your smile through healthy habits like daily flossing and regular checkups.

Are Dental Implants Safe?

For the majority of patients who are in good oral health, dental implants are highly successful when placed by a trained, qualified dental professional. If you have low jawbone density or gum disease, these issues can often be addressed with preliminary treatments to make the surgery safer and more successful. If you have diabetes, cancer, or another medical condition that could make the surgery or healing risky, we will discuss it during your consultation to determine if implants are the right option for you at this time.
