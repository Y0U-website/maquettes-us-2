The practice

Most people think of dental care as something separate from the rest of their health. Dr. Arias — a Pankey Institute graduate trained in whole-patient dentistry — knows differently.

People with periodontal disease are nearly twice as likely to develop heart disease. Oral bacteria can enter the bloodstream and contribute directly to arterial inflammation and cardiovascular risk.

Emerging research links oral bacteria to increased risk of Alzheimer’s disease and cognitive decline. Good oral hygiene may be one of the simplest, most overlooked things you can do for your long-term brain health.

Gum disease and diabetes worsen each other in both directions. Treating periodontal disease consistently leads to meaningful improvements in blood sugar control — a connection most patients never hear about.

A smile you love changes how you show up — in a boardroom, in a photograph, in a conversation. Patients describe it the same way: they stand taller, laugh more freely, stop hiding. That’s not cosmetic. That’s life.

Dr. Eric Arias grew up in Tampa. He went to Jesuit High School, earned his undergraduate degree at Spring Hill College, and completed his Doctor of Dental Surgery at The Ohio State University. When he finished, he came home to Tampa Bay — and he has been right here ever since, taking care of the same community he was raised in.
