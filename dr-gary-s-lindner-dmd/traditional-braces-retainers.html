Signs Your Child May Need Braces

These are common signs that children and adults should seek orthodontic care:

Underbites The lower teeth sit in front of the upper teeth, which can affect bite function and jaw alignment.

Overbites The upper front teeth overlap the lower teeth too much, sometimes leading to wear or jaw strain.

Crossbites Some upper teeth sit inside the lower teeth instead of outside, which can cause uneven wear or shifting.

Crooked Teeth Teeth are out of alignment, making them harder to clean and maintain.

Impacted Teeth Teeth fail to fully erupt through the gums and remain stuck beneath the surface.

Crowded Teeth There is not enough space in the jaw, causing teeth to overlap or twist.

Excessive Space Between Teeth Noticeable gaps exist between teeth due to spacing or missing tooth structure.

Missing Teeth One or more teeth never developed or have been lost, affecting alignment and bite balance.

Extra Teeth Additional teeth beyond the normal set can disrupt alignment and eruption patterns.

Misaligned Teeth Teeth do not fit together properly when biting, even if crowding or spacing isn’t obvious.

Thumb Sucking A prolonged habit that can push teeth forward and alter normal jaw development.

Mouth Breathing Breathing primarily through the mouth may influence jaw growth and dental positioning over time.

Difficulty Chewing Trouble biting or chewing food comfortably, often a sign that the bite is not properly aligned.

Why Traditional Braces Remain a Trusted Orthodontic Solution

Traditional braces continue to be one of the most versatile treatment options in orthodontics. They can effectively address a wide range of alignment concerns, from minor crowding to more complex bite and jaw issues. Because braces work continuously throughout treatment, they offer a reliable path toward achieving precise tooth movement.

In addition to improving the appearance of your smile, properly aligned teeth are often easier to clean and maintain. Orthodontic treatment can support better oral health by reducing areas where plaque and bacteria tend to accumulate while helping create a more balanced bite. For patients comparing braces vs clear aligners, traditional braces may be recommended when more controlled or complex tooth movement is needed.
