Tooth implants are a reliable and effective way to replace missing teeth and restore your smile and oral function. With proper care, they can last for many years or even a lifetime. If you’re ready to learn more about how these implants work, contact Indian Trail Dental Studio today.

  • Planning: If you’re a candidate for dental implants, your dentist will create a customized treatment plan based on your individual needs and circumstances. This plan may involve multiple appointments over the course of several months.
  • Implant placement: The next step is to surgically place the titanium post, or implant, into the jawbone. Your dentist will make a small incision in the gum tissue and drill a small hole into the jawbone. The implant is then placed into the hole and the gum tissue is closed with sutures.
  • Osseointegration: Over the next several months, the implant will fuse with the surrounding jawbone in a process called osseointegration. During this time, it’s important to follow your dentist’s instructions for oral hygiene and avoid putting undue pressure on the implant.
  • Abutment placement: Once the implant has fully integrated with the jawbone, your dentist will place a small metal connector called an abutment on top of the implant. The abutment serves as a base for the replacement tooth or teeth.

Consequences of Improper Implant Installation

It’s important to choose a qualified and experienced dental professional for the installation of your dental implants to minimize the risk of these complications and ensure the long-term success of your treatment. Here’s a closer look at some of the consequences of having implants improperly installed:

  • Infection: If the implant is not properly placed or cared for, bacteria can build up around the implant and cause an infection. In some cases, this infection can spread to other parts of the body.
  • Implant failure: If the implant is not properly placed or integrated with the jawbone, it may fail to provide a stable foundation for the replacement tooth or teeth. This can result in the implant becoming loose or falling out completely.
  • Nerve or tissue damage: Improper placement of the implant can cause damage to surrounding nerves or tissues, leading to pain, numbness, or other complications.
  • Bite problems: If the implant is not properly placed or aligned with the other teeth, it can cause problems with your bite, leading to discomfort or difficulty chewing.
  • Bone loss: If the implant is not properly integrated with the jawbone, it can cause bone loss or resorption, which can weaken the surrounding teeth and gums.
  • Aesthetics: Improperly placed implants can lead to cosmetic issues such as asymmetry or an uneven smile
