Professional Dental Cleanings

Professional dental cleanings, also known as dental prophylaxis, are typically performed by registered dental hygienists. Even patients with excellent brushing and flossing habits benefit from routine professional cleanings.

Removal of Calculus (Tartar): Hardened plaque that attaches to tooth surfaces and below the gumline can only be removed with professional instruments.

Removal Of Plaque: Sticky bacterial film contributes to gum inflammation and periodontal disease. Removing plaque reduces your risk of infection.

Teeth Polishing: Polishing removes surface stains and any remaining plaque, leaving your teeth feeling smooth and refreshed.

Professional cleanings help maintain healthy gums and reduce the progression of periodontal disease.

Dental X-Rays for Early Detection

Dental radiographs (X-rays) are essential diagnostic tools that allow hidden issues to be detected beneath the surface. Many dental concerns such as cavities between teeth, bone loss, infections, or developmental abnormalities cannot be seen during a visual exam alone.

The amount of radiation exposure from dental X-rays is low and considered safe. Necessary precautions, including protective shields and modern imaging technology, are used to minimize exposure.

  • Abscesses or cysts
  • Bone loss
  • Decay between teeth
  • Problems below the gumline
  • Tooth and root positioning

Fluoride Treatments and Sealants

Fluoride is a mineral that strengthens tooth enamel and helps prevent decay. Professional fluoride treatments may be recommended for patients with increased cavity risk, deep grooves in teeth, exposed root surfaces, or a history of frequent decay.

Sealants are thin protective coatings applied to the chewing surfaces of molars. More than 75 percent of dental decay begins in the deep grooves of back teeth. Sealants create a smooth surface that is easier to clean and less vulnerable to cavities.

These preventive treatments are especially beneficial for children and teenagers but may also be appropriate for adults with cavity-prone teeth.

Oral Cancer Screenings

Oral cancer screenings are an important part of preventive dental care. During routine exams, the dentist evaluates the lips, tongue, cheeks, throat, and surrounding tissues for abnormal changes.

Supporting Your Home Care Routine

Strong preventive care extends beyond the dental office. Daily brushing, flossing, and proper oral hygiene practices are essential to controlling plaque and bacteria.

Consistent home care combined with professional visits creates a strong defense against dental disease.

  • Brushing at least twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush
  • Flossing daily to clean between teeth and below the gumline
  • Rinsing when brushing is not possible
  • Using additional hygiene aids as recommended
