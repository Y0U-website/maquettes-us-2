What Are Immediate Dental Implants?

Immediate dental implants, sometimes called same-day implants, involve placing a titanium implant post directly into the extraction socket at the same time as the tooth removal. Rather than two separate procedures with a healing period in between, extraction and placement happen in one visit.

How the Procedure Works

When the conditions are right, the process is more straightforward than many patients expect. The damaged or failing tooth is carefully extracted, the socket is cleaned and evaluated, and the implant is secured in the existing bone. In many cases, a temporary crown can be placed on the same day, giving you a functional and natural-looking tooth while the implant integrates with the surrounding bone over the coming months. The permanent crown is typically placed once osseointegration is complete, locking in a result built to last.

How Immediate Implants Differ From Traditional Implants

Traditional implants require a waiting period of several months between extraction and placement, allowing the socket to heal before the implant is introduced. Immediate implants skip that waiting period entirely when anatomy allows. This reduces the overall treatment timeline, the number of appointments required, and the disruption to your daily life. According to a 2025 narrative review published in the National Library of Medicine , immediate implant placement has demonstrated survival rates ranging from 93.8 % to 100 %, making it a well-supported option when candidacy is properly assessed.

Am I a Candidate for Immediate Dental Implants?

Not every patient qualifies for same-day implant placement, and part of what makes this procedure work is ensuring it is only recommended when the clinical picture supports it. Before any treatment begins, we conduct a comprehensive evaluation using our 3D CBCT scanner to assess bone density, bone volume, and the condition of the surrounding tissue.

Factors That May Make You a Good Candidate

Several factors tend to support candidacy for immediate implant placement. These include having adequate bone density at the extraction site, healthy gum tissue without active infection, a tooth that is being removed due to trauma or non-infectious decay, and strong overall oral health. Patients who are good candidates for immediate implants benefit from a streamlined process that preserves the natural contour of the gum and prevents the bone loss that can begin shortly after extraction.

Our restorative dentistry approach at Precision is built around precision and care, not one-size-fits-all solutions. If immediate placement is not the right path for your situation, we will walk you through alternatives and make sure you understand exactly why and what comes next.

When a Staged Approach Is Recommended

In some cases, immediate implant placement is not the safest or most predictable choice. Active infection at the extraction site, insufficient bone volume, and certain anatomical considerations can make delayed placement the smarter long-term decision. When that is the case, we may recommend bone grafting prior to implant placement to build up the necessary support before moving forward. The goal is always a result that holds up over a lifetime, not just in the short term.

Why Advanced Technology Matters

Successful immediate implant placement is not just about surgical skill; it depends heavily on the quality of pre-surgical planning. Our office uses CBCT scanning to create a three-dimensional image of your jaw before any procedure begins. This allows us to map the exact dimensions of the bone, identify the position of surrounding structures, and determine the ideal angle and depth for implant placement.

Our digital dentistry capabilities, including intraoral scanning, allow us to gather detailed impressions digitally, improving comfort and accuracy from the very first appointment. When every measurement is precise before we begin, the outcome is far more predictable.

What to Expect During Recovery

Recovery from an immediate dental implant procedure is generally manageable, and most patients return to their normal routines within a few days. Some swelling, mild tenderness, and sensitivity around the site are expected in the first 48 to 72 hours. Following post-operative instructions carefully is key. You will be asked to avoid hard or crunchy foods on the treated side during the healing period, maintain gentle oral hygiene around the implant site, and attend follow-up appointments so we can monitor integration and progression toward your permanent crown.

The implant itself fuses with the jawbone through a process called osseointegration, which takes several months to complete. During that time, the temporary restoration keeps your smile intact while the foundation beneath it strengthens.
