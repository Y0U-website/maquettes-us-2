Single Tooth Implant

Receiving a single implant is a straightforward oral surgery procedure that takes about 45 minutes. We take 3D scans of your mouth and plan exactly where the implant will be placed. It will take several months for the post to fuse with your jaw. Once healed, we will attach the abutment and your dentist will place the final crown.

  • An abutment that connects the post to the crown
  • A crown that is customized to match the size, shape, and color of your existing teeth

Should I Get a Single Tooth Implant or a Dental Bridge?

Dental implants and dental bridges are options to consider when replacing a single tooth. While they both aim to restore your smile, they are different solutions that come with their own advantages.

Dental Implants

Dental implants are the only tooth replacement option that replicates the function and appearance of natural teeth. They prevent jaw bone loss, preserve the alignment of your other teeth, and allow you to eat comfortably with no dietary restrictions. Your implants remain in your mouth at all times and require no special care beyond regular brushing and flossing.

Dental Bridges

Dental bridges can be cemented in place or removable. They restore some biting force, but are not as durable as implants. Bridges rely on your existing teeth for support, which can compromise their health and integrity. If your bridge is removable, you must take it out every night, which many patients find inconvenient. Dental bridges do not prevent jaw bone loss, but they can prevent your existing teeth from shifting out of place.

Am I a Good Candidate for Single Tooth Implants?

Many patients who are missing a tooth qualify for dental implants. As long as there is adequate jaw bone to support the implant, you would likely make a great candidate for single tooth implants. If your jaw bone is not strong enough, we can place a bone graft to rebuild the bone and create a sturdy foundation for your implant.

Dental implants are not ideal for patients with periodontal disease. Healthy gums are essential for successful implant placement, so any gum disease should be treated prior to receiving a dental implant. We generally use IV anesthesia for dental implant placement, so if you are healthy enough to undergo anesthesia for a general surgical procedure, you are likely healthy enough for dental implant surgery.
