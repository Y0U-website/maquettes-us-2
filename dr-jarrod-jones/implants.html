Every smile is unique, but most dental implant treatments follow a structured 3- to 6-month journey from initial evaluation to final restoration.

1. Comprehensive Consultation & Digital Imaging

We examine your mouth, capture detailed digital X-rays, and assess your jawbone health. We discuss your goals, explain all your replacement options, and map out a personalized, transparent treatment plan.

2. Implant Placement Surgery: Single outpatient visit.

The titanium implant post is gently placed into your jawbone under local anesthesia (with sedation options available for your comfort). Most patients report that the procedure involves less discomfort than a standard tooth extraction.

3. Healing & Osseointegration: Typically 3 to 4 months.

Over the next few months, your jawbone naturally fuses with the biocompatible titanium post through a biological process called osseointegration . This creates a rock-solid, permanent foundation. A temporary tooth can often be worn during this period so you never have to go without a smile.

4. Abutment Placement & Impressions: After bone integration is confirmed.

Once healing is complete, we attach the abutment to the post and take precision digital impressions of your mouth to design your final crown, bridge, or implant-supported denture.

5. Final Restoration & Smile Reveal: Your permanent smile.

Your custom porcelain crown or restoration is securely anchored onto the abutment. We verify your bite alignment, ensure a perfect color match, and send you out the door with a fully restored, functional smile.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

How long do dental implants last?

With thorough daily brushing, flossing, and routine dental cleanings at our Main Street office, the titanium implant fixture can last a lifetime . The custom porcelain crown attached to the implant typically lasts 15 to 20 years before requiring minor adjustment or replacement due to normal wear.

Is the dental implant procedure painful?

Most patients are pleasantly surprised by how comfortable the procedure is. Because jawbone tissue has relatively few nerve endings, implant placement under local anesthesia causes minimal discomfort. Mild post-operative soreness can usually be managed easily with over-the-counter pain relievers for a day or two.

Am I too old to get dental implants?

Age is rarely a limiting factor! As long as you are in good overall health and have sufficient bone density, patients in their 70s, 80s, and beyond regularly receive dental implants with high success rates.

How do I care for my dental implants?

You care for them just like natural teeth: brush twice a day, floss daily (using floss or a water flosser), and visit eyeSMILE every six months for routine exams and professional cleanings.
