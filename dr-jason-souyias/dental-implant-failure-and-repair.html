Signs & Symptoms of a Failing Dental Implant

Most patients are not dental professionals, so it can be difficult to tell if something is wrong with an implant. Keep an eye out for the following warning signs:

If you’re experiencing any of these symptoms, it’s important to contact your dental provider immediately for an evaluation.

  • Persistent pain or discomfort around the implant
  • Swelling, redness, or inflammation of the gums
  • Bleeding when brushing near the implant site
  • A feeling that the implant is loose or shifting
  • Difficulty chewing or biting
  • Receding gums around the implant
  • Pus or discharge near the implant site
  • Visible bone loss on an X-ray

How Is a Failed Dental Implant Diagnosed?

Your dentist or oral surgeon will perform a thorough evaluation, which may include:

The goal is to identify the cause of failure and determine whether the implant can be salvaged or needs to be removed.

  • A clinical exam to assess gum health and implant mobility
  • X-rays or CBCT scans to evaluate bone support and check for hidden infections
  • Periodontal probing to measure gum pocket depth
  • Review of your medical history, oral hygiene, and habits like smoking or bruxism

Is Dental Implant Repair Painful?

Implant repair procedures are typically performed under local anesthesia or sedation, so you should feel little to no pain during the process. Afterward, patients may experience mild discomfort, swelling, or tenderness for a few days—similar to the recovery from the original implant procedure.

Pain management may include over-the-counter or prescription medications, antibiotics, and follow-up visits to ensure proper healing.

How to Prevent Implant Failure

While not all implant failures are preventable, you can take steps to significantly reduce your risk:

  • Choose an experienced provider : Success starts with proper placement. Choose a periodontist or implant specialist with extensive training.
  • Maintain excellent oral hygiene : Brush and floss daily, and use antimicrobial rinses if recommended.
  • Attend regular dental checkups : Your dentist can monitor your implant and surrounding tissue for any early signs of trouble.
  • Quit smoking : Smoking impairs healing and increases the risk of infection and bone loss.
  • Use a nightguard if you grind your teeth : Protect your implant from excessive forces.
  • Follow all post-operative instructions : Healing time, diet restrictions, and hygiene routines are critical to implant success.

Early Action Makes a Difference

Dental implant failure is rare, but when it happens, early diagnosis and treatment are key. Ignoring symptoms or delaying care can lead to more extensive damage, bone loss, and the need for additional procedures.

If you suspect something isn’t right with your dental implant, don’t wait. Contact your provider to schedule an evaluation. In many cases, the problem can be addressed successfully—and your smile restored.
