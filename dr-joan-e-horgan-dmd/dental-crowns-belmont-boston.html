How Long Do Crowns Last? With proper care, dental crowns can last between 5 and 15 years, depending on the material and how well they’re maintained.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) About Dental Crowns 1. What is the process of getting a dental crown?

The process typically involves two appointments. During the first visit, the dentist prepares the tooth by removing a small portion of the enamel to make room for the crown, takes impressions, and places a temporary crown. The permanent crown is fitted and cemented during the second visit.

Common materials include porcelain, ceramic, metal, and porcelain-fused-to-metal. Your dentist will recommend the best option based on factors like tooth location, function, and aesthetic preference.

Care for crowns like natural teeth by brushing twice daily, flossing, and avoiding very hard foods. Regular dental check-ups are essential to monitor crown condition.

Crowns cannot be whitened. If you plan to whiten your teeth, consider doing so before getting a crown for a closer color match.

The cost varies depending on the material, location, and insurance coverage. Many dental plans cover part of the cost for crowns if they’re medically necessary.

If a crown comes loose, contact your dentist immediately. They may be able to re-cement the crown if it’s in good condition.

Dental crowns are a reliable solution to restore and protect teeth, and with proper care, they can contribute to a healthy, long-lasting smile.

  • Restoring an already broken or severely worn tooth
  • Covering and supporting a tooth with a large filling
  • Covering a dental implant
  • Improving the appearance of discolored or misshaped teeth
