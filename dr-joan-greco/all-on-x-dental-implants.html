A Permanent Replacement for Dentures

All on X dental implants in Hawaii provide a thorough solution for replacing missing teeth. This procedure entails placing a fixed bridge on four strategically located dental implants in the front jaw. These implants stabilize the prosthesis, guaranteeing a natural-looking and functioning smile with no slippage or movement.

With only four to six implants supporting a full arch prosthesis, this safe, effective procedure contributes to the health of the jaw, providing a more comfortable and stable alternative to traditional dental implants.

Understanding the All on X Procedure

Osseointegration, the fusion of the implants with the surrounding jawbone, is vital for the long-term stability of the All on X dental implants. This technique offers the ‘teeth in a day’ option, enabling patients to leave the dental office with a functional and aesthetically pleasing set of teeth after same-day surgery.

High-quality materials such as titanium and zirconia contribute to the durability and success rate of the implants, with survival rates of up to 10 years in the mandible and 5 years in the maxilla.

  • Inserting four dental implants into the jawbone
  • Supporting a fixed teeth-in-a-day permanent bridge prosthesis
  • Using angled implants to create a more secure bond with the jawbone and ensure the stability of the prosthetic teeth.

Ideal Candidates for All on X Dental Implants

All on X dental implants are not for everyone. The ideal candidate should have sufficient healthy bone mass, as the jaw must be able to support the implant fixtures. A lack of jaw bone density, often caused by long-term missing teeth or periodontal disease, can disqualify a candidate, but such conditions should also be absent from the oral health evaluation.

Underlying health issues such as uncontrolled diabetes, high blood pressure, and autoimmune diseases must be carefully considered as they can affect the success rate of dental implant surgery, particularly the All on X implant procedure. However, this implant surgery is especially suitable for patients who have lost all or most of their natural teeth due to decay, injury, or advanced gum disease and are considering teeth implants or removable dentures as an alternative.

For those who initially lack the bone density or gum tissue needed for the implants, eligibility for All on X can potentially be widened by bone and gum grafting procedures.

Insurance Coverage for All on X Dental Implants

Dental insurance typically does not cover implants outright due to their categorization beyond basic care, but they may cover related components such as implant-supported crowns, bridges, or dentures. Insurance providers such as Delta Dental, Metlife, Cigna, Humana, Guardian, AETNA, and Blue Cross Blue Shield may cover a portion of the cost for All on X dental implants.

The initial dental consultation, which assesses teeth, gums, and bone health, may influence the extent of coverage based on the presence of pre-existing conditions. Patients should have their dental insurance details reviewed by a professional, who can directly discuss coverage specifics with the insurance company.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the downside of All on X dental implants?

The downside of All on X dental implants is the risk of a failed implant, which can lead to difficulty in speech and is expensive for some patients to afford.

What is the failure rate of All on X dental implants?

The failure rate of All on X dental implants is usually less than 5 %, but can vary based on factors such as oral health, dentist’s skill, aftercare quality, and patient’s overall health and lifestyle habits.

Who is not a candidate for All on X dental implants?

Patients with a history of poor oral health and underlying conditions that impede healing are not good candidates for All on X dental implants. These conditions could make the healing process difficult or unusually slow.

Who is the ideal candidate for All on X dental implants?

The ideal candidate for All on X dental implants has sufficient healthy bone mass and a jaw able to support the implant fixtures. Conditions affecting jaw bone density could disqualify a candidate, but these should be absent from the oral health evaluation.
