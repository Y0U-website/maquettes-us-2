Advantages of Dental Implants

Dental implants are inserted directly into the jawbone where they act as replacements for the roots of missing teeth. This is the primary function of dental implants, and also their most significant benefit.

When a tooth is missing, so too is its root. The roots of your teeth both anchor the teeth to the jaw and ensure proper mineralization remains around each tooth. When a root goes away, the signals it sends are lost, allowing the body to reabsorb minerals around the missing tooth and produce a sunken or collapsed appearance. By replacing the roots of missing teeth, dental implants prevent jaw collapse and preserve the natural structure of your face.

In addition to preventing changes to the structure of your jaw, dental implants offer the following benefits:

Implants are generally considered the best way to replace a missing tooth, but they are not right for everyone. Depending on your unique situation, dentures or bridges may be a better option.

  • Complete restoration of bite tension, strength, and function
  • Prevention of adjacent tooth slippage
  • Entirely natural-looking end results
  • Long-term durability, often lasting a lifetime

Are You a Candidate for Dental Implants?

Most people who are missing teeth are candidates for implants. However, a comprehensive examination by an experienced dentist is necessary to determine if you are a good candidate. Generally, an ideal candidate for dental implants has:

Smoking significantly increases the risk of implant failure and surgical complications. If you smoke or use tobacco products, you will need to stop for at least one month before surgery. Chronic health conditions such as diabetes can also affect implant outcomes, so it is important to have any ongoing health issues under control and to receive clearance from your primary care doctor before moving forward.

  • One or more missing teeth
  • Adequate bone tissue at the site of the missing tooth
  • Healthy gums
  • The ability to quit smoking, if applicable
  • No uncontrolled chronic health conditions

Implant Supported Dentures

If you have several missing teeth in a row, or if an entire arch needs to be replaced, implant supported dentures can be a great option. They offer more stability than adhesive dentures and do a better job of preserving jaw shape over time. Dr. Schmid offers both full and partial implant supported dentures, depending on your individual needs.

Full dentures use small bars running the length of the jaw that the dentures clip onto. Partial dentures are secured with a ball and socket setup. During your consultation, Dr. Schmid will walk you through all available options and help you determine which is the best fit.

Consequences of Missing Teeth

Leaving a missing tooth unreplaced can have far-reaching effects over time, including:

Once a tooth is lost, the roots no longer stimulate the jawbone, triggering bone loss and gum recession. This can weaken neighboring teeth and set off a chain reaction of additional tooth loss. Without this tooth support, your lower face will start to appear hollow and the skin will begin to sag. Missing teeth also affect speech and make eating more difficult depending on which teeth are involved.

  • Bone loss in the jaw
  • Receding gums
  • Weakened neighboring teeth
  • A hollow or sagging appearance in the lower face
