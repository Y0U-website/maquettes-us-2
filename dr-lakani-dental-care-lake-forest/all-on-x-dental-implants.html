What All-on-X doesn’t give you — and why it matters

  • Pink acrylic gum base visible when smiling/speaking
  • Single bar: if a section fails, whole arch affected
  • Harder to clean underneath
  • Bone under prosthetic bar can continue to resorb
  • Not backed by lifetime guarantee in most cases

Who is a candidate for All-on-X?

A CBCT scan at consultation is what actually determines candidacy — including how many implants your case needs and whether grafting comes first.

  • Missing most or all teeth in an arch, or facing multiple failing teeth
  • Wearing a loose or uncomfortable traditional denture and wanting a fixed alternative
  • Adequate bone volume at the implant sites, or willing to complete grafting first
  • In general health that supports oral surgery and a multi-month healing process
  • Wants a fixed, non-removable result rather than a removable overdenture

What to expect, start to finish

CBCT Consultation & Digital Planning

A 3D scan maps your bone volume and anatomy; implant position and number ("X") are planned digitally before surgery.

Implant Placement

4–6 implants per arch are placed, with rear implants typically angled to maximize contact with available bone.

Same-Day Temporary Bridge

In most protocols, a temporary full-arch bridge is attached the same day — you leave with fixed teeth, not a gap.

Osseointegration (typically 3–6 months)

Implants fuse with the jawbone. You function on the temporary bridge during this period, with scheduled check-ins.

Final Permanent Bridge

Once integration is confirmed, the temporary is replaced with the definitive full-arch bridge.

Total cost depends on the number of implants placed per arch, whether bone grafting is needed first, and the type of final bridge chosen. Dental insurance often contributes toward diagnostic imaging and portions of the restorative work, though coverage for the implants themselves varies significantly by plan — we verify your benefits and provide an itemized estimate at your consultation.
