What Are Dental Implants?

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Getting dental implants takes more time than traditional tooth replacements, but since implants can last for decades with the right care, they’re definitely worth waiting for! Everyone’s treatment process can vary, but there are four basic steps that each patient goes through:

Initial Consultation

The first step of your journey towards a rejuvenated smile is a detailed consultation with Dr. Korte , our local implant dentist in Chaska. During this visit, she will review your X-rays, conduct a thorough examination of your mouth, and help you determine whether dental implants are right for you.

If you’re a good candidate for the procedure, our team at White Oak Dental will present you with a treatment plan and help you figure out additional details, like cost. We’ll also be happy to answer any questions you may have.

Dental Implant Placement

As a lifelong student Dr. Korte has the advanced knowledge and skills to provide in-house dental implant placement, making your treatment as streamlined as possible. This part of your procedure will include surgically placing the dental implants below the gumline and within the jawbone by creating a small window in the gums. After the dental implants have been placed, the tissue will be sutured to heal, and Dr. Korte will provide you with post-operative instructions to help you make a speedy recovery!

Osseointegration & Healing

Over the following three to six months after your dental implant surgery, your posts will fuse with the jawbone through a process called osseointegration. It’s one of the most important determinants for a successful treatment, as fusion is what makes the dental implants act as a sturdy foundation for your restorations. Although this process can take some time, many of our patients feel well enough to go to work and carry on with their daily routines within a day or two of their procedure.

Attaching the Final Restoration

After your healing process is complete, Dr. Korte will attach small metal connector pieces to the tops of your dental implants and take impressions of your gumline. These impressions will be sent to a dental laboratory we work with to craft your replacement teeth. Once we receive them back, and after your gums have healed around your abutments, we will then be able to attach your dental bridge, crown, or denture to your implants and finish your treatment!

Benefits of Dental Implants

More dental implants are being placed every year by highly trained dental professionals like Dr. Korte , and it’s easy to see why once you look. These ultramodern prosthetics have many major benefits for your oral health, your confidence, and your life! Traditional replacement solutions like bridges and dentures only replace the crown, which is the visible part of the tooth. While this is good enough for looks and gives some functional benefit, dental implants take it to the next level. If you’re curious how dental implants from White Oak Dental may benefit you, read more below!

Day-to-Day Benefits

You’ll be using your teeth every day to perform many tasks, some you may not think about consciously. With dental implants, you’ll never need to worry about the stability of your smile again!

  • Enjoy Your Favorite Foods: Since dental implants restore over 70 percent of your biting and chewing power, you’ll be able to eat virtually anything you want!
  • Easy Oral Hygiene: Dental implants don’t require any special treatment or maintenance. Simply brush them along with the rest of your teeth twice a day and ensure you floss daily to keep them clean.
  • Smile with Confidence: Our dental implants are topped with highly aesthetic porcelain crowns that look just like natural teeth. If you regularly hide your smile behind your hand or make a habit of not showing your teeth in pictures dental implants can give you the confidence to grin again.

Health Benefits

Dental implants are the only tooth replacement options that can create an artificial root. This makes them the strongest and most beneficial prosthetic for your mouth in terms of your health. After your surgery, you’ll enjoy:

  • A Healthy Jawbone: Tooth loss causes your jawbone to deteriorate over time due to lack of stimulation. The titanium implant fixtures stimulate the bone as you chew, causing them to rebuild and strengthen over time just like natural tooth roots!
  • Digestive Support: The more teeth you lose, the harder it becomes to chew food properly. This first step of digestion is vitally important as larger chunks that haven’t been processed by chewing can cause stomach issues. Dental implants change the game, allowing you to effectively grind down food.
  • A Lower Risk of Oral Health Issues: Since dental implants are easier to clean, you’ll enjoy a more hygienic mouth overall. Less plaque in and around your teeth means fewer oral health issues.

Long-Term Benefits

Your new implants will last a long time and have a 95 percent success rate! Other long-term benefits include:

  • May Never Need Replacing: Most tooth replacement options like bridges and dentures need to be replaced over time. Dental implants can last decades or longer!
  • Saves You Time and Money: Without the need for consistent replacements, your investment with dental implants goes further, saving you time at the dentist office and money for repeated services.
  • Keeps Your Face Looking Young: As your jawbone deteriorates, it can give your face a more shallow or sunken appearance. Dental implants prevent this shrinkage, allowing you to stay looking youthful longer!

What Are the Stages of Dental Implant Treatment?

First, you’ll come into our office for a consultation. You’ll discuss your smile goals with Dr. Korte, and he will carefully examine the condition of your oral health, your jawbone, and how many teeth you’re missing. After developing a personalized treatment plan, we will schedule your dental implant surgery.

During surgery, small incisions will be made in your gums to reveal your jawbone. Then, small holes will be created in your jaw for the placement of your dental implants. Once your implants are placed, it will take about three to six months for them to completely fuse to your bone. While this seems like a lengthy process, this osseointegration allows for a long lasting and permanent replacement.

Once your implants have healed, you’ll come back into our office, where Dr. Korte will permanently place your restoration after making sure you’re completely satisfied.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants are designed to last several decades sometimes even a lifetime! However, that’s only the case if you take proper care of them. By diligently following the aftercare instructions Dr. Korte provided you with, you can keep your smile safe from damage and infection. At White Oak Dental, we’ll explain which precautions you should take to ensure your implants stay firmly in place for a lifetime. If you’d like to learn how to maintain your implants, continue reading or call our office today !

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Even though dental implants can’t get cavities, that doesn’t mean you can slack off on your oral hygiene. You must continue to take proper care of your teeth and gums to ward off oral health issues. That’s primarily because problems like gum disease can result in dental implant failure. To keep your smile in optimal condition, make sure you’re brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and rinsing with an antiseptic mouthwash.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Once you’re all healed up and your implants have successfully fused with the jawbone, you can eat almost anything you want. However, make sure you avoid overindulging in hard, sticky, or sugary foods as they can cause serious issues for your implants. Instead, you should stick to a diet full of nutrient-rich foods (i.e., fruits, vegetables, whole grains, dairy products, lean proteins). Also, eating foods rich in calcium and vitamin C will help keep your jawbone strong and gum tissues healthy!

Break Bad Habits

Smoking, using tobacco products, chewing on your fingernails, biting pens or pencils, using your teeth as tools are you guilty of any of these poor habits? If so, now’s time to break them! Not only can they destroy your tooth enamel, but your dental implants as well. It’s best to keep some sugar-free gum in your pocket for times when you feel tempted to gnaw on a non-food item. You can also ask a medical or dental professional for resources that will help you quit smoking and using tobacco.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Dental implants may be strong, but they’re certainly not indestructible. If you regularly play sports or grind your teeth at night, you should ask Dr. Korte about a custom-made oral device. An athletic mouthguard can protect you against oral injuries, while a nightguard can prevent serious damage to your enamel and implants.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Aside from brushing and flossing, it’s incredibly important that you visit Dr. Korte every six months. These preventive checkups and cleanings allow him to detect, prevent, and treat oral health problems before they turn into complications. With early detection, you can avoid needing more complex and costly procedures down the line. Plus, it’ll ensure your newly rebuilt smile remains in its very best shape for a lifetime!

Dental Implants Post-Op Instructions

After we’ve wrapped up your dental implant procedure here at our office, it will fall on you to ensure that you’re able to heal properly but we’ll be sure to let you know exactly what to do! We’ll provide you with very specific instructions and guidelines to follow, but in the meantime, here is some additional information and advice on how to manage your discomfort and ensure a smooth, speedy recovery.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

One of your top priorities immediately following your surgery is protecting the blood clots that form over your surgical sites. These clots are responsible for protecting your gums while they heal around the implants. To prevent any complications with them, keep the following in mind:

  • Do not use a drinking straw and avoid spitting
  • Refrain from smoking or using tobacco products
  • Avoid touching the surgical sites with your tongue or fingers

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

You’ll need to stick to a soft food diet immediately following your surgery, but within a few days, you’ll be able to slowly begin adding more solid foods back into the mix, so long as you avoid chewing directly on your implant sites. Here are some items to stock up your fridge and pantry with before your procedure:

  • Soups and broths (room temperature)
  • Mashed potatoes
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Cooked pasta and rice
  • Yogurt, pudding, and Jell-O
  • Smoothies and protein shakes

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

As you might expect, oral hygiene is still incredibly important following your surgery, and if things don’t remain healthy, your implant posts could experience complications during the osseointegration process. Here’s what you should keep in mind regarding your post-op oral hygiene:

  • You can brush as you normally would the day after your surgery, but show extra caution when brushing near the surgical sites.
  • Rinse your mouth with salt water 2 or 3 times a day, ideally after each meal.
  • Avoid using any mouthwashes that contain alcohol.

Dental Implant FAQs

Even after reading about dental implants, you undoubtedly still have some questions. After all, dental implants involve complex procedures, and they are a huge investment. That’s why it’s best to be as informed as possible before beginning treatment. Fortunately, we have answered some of the most common questions we get asked about dental implants down below. If you have a dental implant question that you don’t see answered here, give us a call and we’ll do our utmost to answer it right away.

Can I get dental implants if I smoke?

Yes, but there are some risks involved. Complications and implant failure are much more likely to occur if you smoke or chew tobacco. This is because tobacco hinders the mouth’s healing process, which is critical to the success of your dental implants. During the few months after implant placement, your jawbone actually grows around the implant post. Smoking can slow down this process and increase the chance that the implant will not fully integrate. This is why we recommend quitting smoking before getting implants placed.

Can I get dental implants if I have diabetes?

Yes, but as with smoking, it comes with some risks. Poorly controlled diabetes negatively impacts your body’s ability to fight off infections. As a result, there is a higher risk of implant failure than in non-diabetics. If you’re missing teeth and have diabetes, consult with your doctor before getting dental implants to further discuss the risks and how to minimize them.

Does getting dental implants hurt?

Most patients report very little discomfort during the procedure, thanks to plenty of local anesthetic and nitrous oxide sedation. In fact, many patients have preferred dental implant placement to a tooth extraction. You can expect minor swelling and soreness for a few days following the procedure, but that is completely normal for any kind of oral surgery. This can be managed by applying a cold compress to the area for ten minutes on, ten minutes off. You could also take over the counter or prescribed pain relievers. Just be sure to only take them as directed on the bottle.

Why do I need a bone graft before getting dental implants?

When you’ve been missing teeth for a while, your jawbone begins to shrink due to lack of stimulation from the roots of your natural teeth. When this occurs, your jawbone is typically not strong enough to support a dental implant. A bone graft is then necessary to make your jawbone able to accommodate implants. The procedure may add a few months of healing time to your dental implant journey, but it will ultimately be worth it to have a full, healthy smile once more.

How long does it take for dental implants to heal?

The healing process will be affected by a few factors, such as whether you’re diabetic or a smoker. Another factor would be the current density of your jawbone, and whether you’ll need a bone graft beforehand. Once you have scheduled a consultation with Dr. Korte, he can give you a more accurate timeframe.
