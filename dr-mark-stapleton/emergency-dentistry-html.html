How We Treat Dental Emergencies

Perhaps you don’t know how urgent dental care works. Even so, there’s no need to worry; our office can give you a quick rundown. To that end, here is what you can expect from an emergency dental visit:

  • Same-Day Visit Once you’ve called us, we’ll book your visit for the earliest time possible. Depending on availability, we may even see you the same day! Regardless, our staff will also provide first-aid tips over the phone.
  • Emergency Exam For the actual visit, our team will start off with an emergency exam. This action lets us trace the source and extent of your oral issue. Furthermore, it helps us learn how best to relieve your pain.
  • Review of Findings Our dentists will review their findings with you after the exam. In fact, they’ll even suggest potential procedures. Depending on your feedback, our office will then draft a custom treatment plan just for you. (This plan will explain the estimated price and timeline before the procedure.)
  • Needed Dental Care Our team will address your oral problem quickly, no matter its nature. The staff are trained to use dental crowns, root canal therapy, tooth extractions, and more. As such, you can trust us to get your oral health in order.

How Can Prevention Help You Save?

Some people have dental fear or anxiety that keeps them from coming in for their routine checkups and cleanings and preventive care. As a result, worsening oral health concerns arise that require more costly emergency treatment. By investing in your smile before any issues get worse, you can mitigate the harm to your oral health and impact on your wallet, as preventive and minor dental treatments are covered at a greater percentage by dental insurance.

Keys to Preventing Dental Emergencies

Even though it’s impossible to completely prevent dental emergencies from occurring, there are still ways you can reduce your risk of experiencing one. With this in mind, keep reading to learn some precautionary measures we recommend taking:

Visit Us Every Six Months for a Dental Checkup and Cleaning

In addition to taking great care of your teeth at home, we recommend coming to our Chaska dental office twice a year for a dental checkup and teeth cleaning. After all, oral health problems, like tooth decay can develop under the radar. Plus, we check for overall health issues as well, including oral cancer, and remove buildup plaque and tartar to help keep your teeth and gums happy and healthy. Simply put, these routine appointments greatly benefit your oral and overall health!

Keep Up With Your At-Home Oral Hygiene Routine

Each morning and evening, you need to brush your teeth for a full two minutes. Additionally, you should floss and rinse with mouthwash daily to clean between your teeth and prevent plaque buildup. It’s crucial that you use the proper techniques and products as well to prevent enamel erosion, soft tissue irritation, and the like. If you ever have any questions about your at-home oral hygiene routine, don’t hesitate to reach out to us we can provide you with the answers you’re looking for.

Avoid Bad Habits, Like Smoking

It’s no secret that using tobacco products can take a serious toll on your oral and overall health. That’s why we recommend quitting, especially if you want to keep your teeth and gums healthy. We also encourage our patients to break other bad habits, like biting their nails, chewing on ice cubes, or using their teeth as tools. These may seem harmless, but they greatly increase your risk of dental damage.

Don’t Chew on Items That Are Excessively Hard, Chewy, or Sticky

We mentioned above that you shouldn’t chew on ice cubes. You should also avoid eating excessively hard, chewy, and sticky foods, including hard pretzels, chewy steaks, and sticky candies. Instead, eat foods that will give your body the nutrients it needs to keep your enamel healthy, your jawbone strong, and your gums infection-free, like fresh fruits, raw vegetables, dairy products, healthy fats, and lean proteins.

Wear a Mouthguard During Sports

It goes without saying that people who participate in football, wrestling, and other contact sports should wear a mouthguard, but did you know that you need one for solo sports as well? Wearing one while you’re skateboarding, running, or rollerblading can protect your smile from harm if you accidentally fall. So, make sure you have (and wear) one. If you don’t have a mouthguard, get in touch with us!

Note: A mouthguard is also effective at preventing cracks, chips, and fractures from Bruxism (chronic teeth grinding or clenching).

Dental Emergency FAQs

What does chronic bad breath mean?

Bad breath could be the result of everything from your diet to your oral hygiene regimen. That’s why we recommend going back to the basics (i.e., steering clear of pungent foods, brushing your teeth after each meal, flossing daily). However, if the odor on your breath is persistent even after you brush your teeth or chew on some sugar-free gum then you should absolutely schedule an exam with our Chaska dental team so we can make sure an untreated oral health issue isn’t the root of the problem.

Do chipped teeth heal?

Sadly, your enamel cannot regenerate. So, if you chip a tooth, the only way to restore the missing structure is by coming in for the necessary restorative or cosmetic care. The good news is that treatments like direct bonding can fix noticeable chips in a single visit and without a large price tag!

What does throbbing tooth pain mean?

A large cavity and a significant crack are just two of the many reasons you may be experiencing throbbing tooth pain. Either way, the next best step is the same: contacting our Chaska dental office. Once we learn more about your symptoms and how long you’ve been experiencing them, we can determine if same-day care is needed. If that’s not necessary, we will still give you some helpful tips on how to manage your discomfort and protect your smile from harm in the meantime.

Are knocked-out baby teeth a dental emergency?

If your child’s baby tooth was already on the brink of falling out and they aren’t in any pain, then there’s likely nothing to worry about. If, on the other hand, their tooth was knocked out prematurely, they sustained a soft tissue injury, or they are in pain, then it’s absolutely a dental emergency. So, don’t hesitate to get in touch with us, explain what happened, and bring them in for a visit with our emergency dentist.

Note: If your child’s face is extremely swollen, they have broken their jaw, or they are bleeding severely, you should take them to the ER.

Will my toothache go away on its own?

Patients often cross their fingers in hopes that their toothache will go away on its own. It won’t. Quite the opposite since the longer you wait, the more time the root of the problem will have to progress. To prevent that from happening, it’s of the utmost importance that you call us when you first notice that something is wrong. This ensures that we can intervene at the earliest possible stage, saving your smile and your wallet in the process.

Toothaches

Is My Toothache a Dental Emergency? If you’re dealing with a toothache in Chaska, you should absolutely give us a call. That way, we can learn more about your symptoms, assess the urgency of the situation, and schedule an appointment with Dr. April Korte.

How You Should Handle a Toothache: To alleviate the discomfort you’re experiencing in the short term, we recommend using a cold compress, sticking to soft foods, not chewing on that side of your mouth, and taking OTC pain medication as directed.

How We Treat Toothaches: Fortunately, we offer several restorative treatments at our office, including metal-free dental crowns. Once we’ve pinpointed the cause of your toothache, we can discuss each one more in-depth, share our recommendations, and help you decide what’s best for your oral health.

Chipped Tooth

Is a Chipped Tooth a Dental Emergency? Although chipped teeth don’t usually require same-day care, they do warrant a call to our office. After all, the more time that passes, the higher the chances are of the tooth developing an infection or breaking further.

How You Should Handle a Chipped Tooth: In addition to scheduling an appointment with us, we recommend placing any broken pieces of your tooth in a small, clean container. It’s also a good idea to rinse your mouth gently with clean, room-temperature water and to place some sugar-free gum over the tooth to prevent the soft tissue in your mouth from getting cut.

How We Treat Chipped Teeth: We offer several solutions at White Oak Dental, including direct bonding and veneers. Rest assured, we will discuss both options and answer any questions you have during your visit. That way, you can decide which one is ideal for you.

Cracked Tooth

Is a Cracked Tooth a Dental Emergency? Yes, so it’s important that you call us ASAP. In addition to scheduling an emergency appointment at our office, we’ll share what you can do to prevent the tooth from breaking further until you arrive.

How You Should Handle a Cracked Tooth: It’s very important that you don’t place any pressure on the tooth. So, don’t chew on that side of your mouth and avoid hard pretzels, raw almonds, and other crunchy foods entirely. It’s also a good idea to rinse your mouth gently with clean, room-temperature water periodically.

How We Treat Cracked Teeth: Hopefully, the break will be minor enough that we can restore the look, health, and function of your tooth with a dental crown. If the damage is severe, however, then Dr. Korte will need to perform an extraction and fill the open space with a custom dental bridge or implant.

Very Sensitive Teeth

Are Very Sensitive Teeth a Dental Emergency? That depends on a few factors, including how severe your symptoms are. For example, if you’ve been dealing with considerable sensitivity for more than 24 hours, we do recommend scheduling an appointment with our emergency dentist, Dr. Korte.

How You Should Handle Very Sensitive Teeth: Sometimes, switching to a desensitizing toothpaste helps. If that doesn’t work, then stick to room-temperature water, don’t eat ice cream, popsicles, or any other cold foods, and take OTC pain medication as directed.

Knocked-Out Tooth

Is My Knocked-Out Tooth a Dental Emergency? Yes, so it’s important that you act fast. In addition to locating the tooth, we recommend calling our team at White Oak Dental so we can schedule an appointment at our office within the hour.

How You Should Handle a Knocked-Out Tooth: Remember, it only takes about 15 minutes for the tooth to start to die. So, it’s important that you find it, pick it up by the crown, and place it back in its socket. If it doesn’t slide back into place, don’t force it. Instead, carefully place it in a clean container filled with milk or saline solution.
