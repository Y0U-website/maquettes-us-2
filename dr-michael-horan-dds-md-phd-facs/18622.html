What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants serve as an artificial tooth root that is anchored to your jawbone and supports the dental implant crown, restoring your appearance, ability to chew effectively, and your oral health.

Here are the three basic components of the dental implant process:

  • The dental implant is physically placed in your jawbone to anchor your tooth replacement.
  • The abutment screws into the implant and holds the dental crown in place. This makes the process of replacing the crown easier.
  • The dental crown is what you see; it s the dental prosthesis that looks, feels, and functions like your natural tooth. The crown is shaped and shaded to blend with your surrounding teeth.

How Does the Procedure Work?

The dental procedure works in two processes: 1) surgical placement of the artificial root (the titanium post) and 2) attaching the dental crown or other replacement teeth. A dental implant is a small, thin titanium post that s surgically placed in the jawbone, replacing the tooth s missing root. Over time, the jawbone and implant bond together, becoming a sturdy and permanent foundation for the artificial tooth or teeth. This process, called osseointegration , can take a few months, but many people can still enjoy their daily routines without much disruption.

All-on-4

For patients who have used dentures for a long time or need multiple teeth restored, All-on-4 may be a good option. With this technique, only four dental implants are implanted to support complete dentures that do not shift or fall out.

Snap-On Dentures

Snap-on dentures get support from the dental implants they snap onto. While traditional dentures rest on the gums, snap-on dentures are secured to multiple implants. Unlike All-on-4, these dentures are removable and can be snapped back into place.

Can I Get Cavities with Dental Implants?

Dental implants are not susceptible to decay because they are made of titanium and porcelain. Dental implants are, however, susceptible to periodontal disease. Uncontrolled periodontal disease can lead to losing a dental implant, a condition called peri-implantitis. However, you can maintain good gum health by receiving regular dental cleanings and exams.

How Do Dental Implants Form a Tooth?

A dental implant comprises of the following:

  • Dental implant: This is the part that is inserted into the bone and makes up for the lost root of your tooth.
  • Abutment: This part forms the tooth structure that will attach the tooth s crown to the implant by a screw.
  • Dental crown or replacement teeth: This is the visible tooth, and it attaches to the abutment.
