Dental Implants

Dental Implants in Newburgh, NY Losing a tooth changes everything. How you eat, how you smile, even how you speak. Maybe you’re dealing with an old injury, severe decay, or gum disease that’s claimed one or more of your teeth. Whatever brought you to this point, I want you to know that dental implants can give you back not just your tooth, but your confidence and quality of life.

Unlike dentures or bridges, implants actually replace your tooth root with a titanium post that becomes part of your jaw bone. It’s the closest thing we have to growing back your natural tooth.

Why Implants Are Different

When you lose a tooth, you don’t just lose the part you can see—you lose the root too. And that root was doing more than just holding your tooth in place. It was stimulating the bone in your jaw, keeping it strong and healthy. Without that stimulation, the bone starts to shrink away, which can change the shape of your face over time.

Dental implants solve this problem because the titanium post actually bonds with your bone through a process called osseointegration. It’s pretty remarkable—your body essentially accepts the implant as part of itself, creating a foundation that’s as strong as your natural tooth root.

This means you can bite into an apple, chew steak, laugh without worry, and smile with complete confidence. Your implant teeth will look, feel, and function exactly like your natural teeth.

The Process: What to Expect

I know the idea of getting implants can feel overwhelming, so let me walk you through exactly what happens. The whole process typically takes 6-8 months, but most of that time is just waiting for your body to do its amazing work of integrating the implant with your bone.

During the first procedure, we place the titanium implant into your jawbone where your tooth root used to be. This is done under local anesthesia, and most patients are surprised at how comfortable it is. You’ll go home the same day with some temporary teeth if needed, and you’ll be able to eat soft foods while you heal.

Once the implant has fully integrated, we’ll attach a small connector piece called an abutment, and then place your custom-made crown. This final crown is crafted specifically for you to match the color, size, and shape of your natural teeth.

Sometimes, if conditions are right, we can do everything in fewer visits or even place a temporary crown right away. We’ll discuss what approach makes the most sense for your specific situation.

Why Choose Implants Over Other Options?

Implants offer advantages that other tooth replacement options simply can’t match. Unlike bridges, we don’t have to alter your healthy neighboring teeth. Unlike dentures, they don’t slip, click, or require adhesives. And unlike both bridges and dentures, implants can last a lifetime with proper care.

They also help preserve your facial structure by maintaining the bone in your jaw, something that other options can’t do.

The Investment in Your Quality of Life

I’ll be honest—implants require a bigger upfront investment than other options. But when you consider that they can last decades or even a lifetime, and the dramatic improvement in your quality of life, most patients find they’re worth every penny.

Many insurance plans cover at least part of the cost, and we offer financing options to make treatment more manageable.

Frequently Asked Questions

With proper care, dental implants can last a lifetime. The success rate is 95-98 % over the first 5 years, and studies show that 80-90 % are still functioning well after 10-15 years. The crown on top may need replacement every 10-15 years due to normal wear, but the implant itself is designed to be permanent.

Most patients are pleasantly surprised by how comfortable the procedure is. On a scale of 1 to 10, most patients report pain levels between 2 and 4. We use local anesthesia during the procedure, and you’ll likely experience mild pain and tingling in the first 24 hours, with soreness, bruising, and swelling also possible. Most people manage any discomfort easily with over-the-counter pain medication.

The initial healing is pretty quick. After 2 weeks, most patients are fully recovered from the surgical procedure itself. Swelling typically resolves by day 4, and most oral functions recover within 3 days. However, the complete integration process takes 3-6 months as the bone heals around the implant.

During the 3-month integration period, you’ll be limited to eating soft foods. This includes things like pasta, eggs, fish, cooked vegetables, and smoothies. Once your implant is fully integrated and your final crown is placed, you can eat virtually anything—that’s one of the best benefits of implants.

Age itself isn’t a limiting factor for implants. I’ve successfully placed implants in patients in their 80s and 90s. What matters more is your overall health and the condition of your jawbone. As long as you’re healthy enough for a routine dental procedure and have adequate bone to support the implant, age shouldn’t hold you back.

The cost varies depending on your specific situation, but typically ranges from $3,000-$6,000 per tooth, including the implant, abutment, and crown. While this seems like a significant investment upfront, when you consider that implants can last a lifetime, they often end up being more cost-effective than other options that need replacement every few years.

Will my insurance cover dental implants?

Many insurance plans are starting to cover implants, especially when they’re considered medically necessary rather than cosmetic. Coverage varies widely, but we’ll help you understand your benefits and file all the necessary paperwork. Even if your insurance doesn’t cover the full cost, the investment in your quality of life is usually worth it.

Can I get implants if I have gum disease?

We’d need to treat and control any gum disease before placing implants. Healthy gums are crucial for implant success. Once your gums are healthy, implants are definitely possible and can actually be easier to keep clean than natural teeth.

What if I don’t have enough bone for implants?

If you’ve been missing teeth for a while, some bone loss is normal. In many cases, we can rebuild the bone with grafting procedures before placing the implant. Modern bone grafting techniques are very successful, so lack of bone doesn’t necessarily rule out implants.

Caring for implants is actually easier than caring for natural teeth in many ways. You brush and floss just like normal, but you don’t have to worry about cavities in the implant crown. The key is keeping your gums healthy around the implant. Regular dental visits and good home care will keep your implants in great shape.

Recent studies show that up to 96 % of dental implants heal without any problems. Failures are rare and usually happen in the first few months if the implant doesn’t integrate properly with the bone. Factors like smoking, uncontrolled diabetes, or poor oral hygiene can increase the risk of failure.

Will people be able to tell I have an implant?

Absolutely not. When we’re done, your implant tooth will look, feel, and function exactly like your natural teeth. Even your dentist might have trouble telling which tooth is the implant without looking at your x-rays. That’s the whole goal—a completely natural result.
