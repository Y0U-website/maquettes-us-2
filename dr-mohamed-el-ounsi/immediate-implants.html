What Are Immediate Implants?

The concept is not new, but the technology and technique required to do it well have advanced considerably. Precise digital imaging, careful socket evaluation, and rigorous case selection are what separate a predictable outcome from a risky one. A 2022 systematic review published in the National Institutes of Health’s PubMed Central found success rates of 96.7–100 % for single immediate implants across multiple randomized controlled trials, underscoring just how reliable this procedure has become in the hands of a trained oral and maxillofacial surgeon.

Not every patient or every socket is a candidate, which is exactly why thorough evaluation matters. Bone volume, the presence of infection, soft tissue condition, and the patient’s overall health all factor into whether immediate placement is the right path. Our team reviews each case individually before recommending a protocol.

How Immediate Placement Differs from the Traditional Timeline

In a conventional implant sequence, extraction comes first, followed by a healing period that typically spans three to six months. Only then is the implant placed, and after osseointegration, the process by which the implant fuses with the jawbone, a crown is attached. From start to finish, the entire process can take the better part of a year.

The Immediate Placement Advantage

With immediate implants, the extraction and implant placement happen in the same surgical visit. In select cases, a temporary restoration can also be placed the same day, so patients leave with something in place rather than a visible gap. This approach offers several meaningful advantages for the right candidate:

It is important to understand that immediate placement does not mean the healing or osseointegration phase is skipped. The implant still needs time to integrate with the bone before a final crown is placed. What changes is when the implant goes in, not whether the body is given the time it needs to accept it.

  • Fewer surgical appointments: Combining two procedures into one reduces your overall treatment time and recovery burden.
  • Bone preservation: Placing an implant immediately after extraction helps maintain the bone and tissue that would otherwise begin to resorb once a tooth is lost.
  • Faster restoration: Patients reach their final crown stage sooner, meaning less time navigating daily life with a missing tooth.
  • Psychological benefit: Leaving the office with a tooth in place, even a temporary one, can make a significant difference in how patients experience the process.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Immediate Implants?

Candidacy for immediate placement hinges on several clinical factors that Dr. El-Ounsi assesses through a thorough examination and advanced imaging. Ideal candidates tend to have adequate bone density and volume at the extraction site, healthy surrounding gum tissue, no active infection at the time of extraction, and good overall health without conditions that significantly impair healing. Patients who smoke or have certain systemic conditions may still be candidates, but those factors are weighed carefully on a case-by-case basis.

For patients whose bone has already been compromised, bone grafting may be recommended either alongside extraction or as a preparatory step before implant placement. Dr. El-Ounsi’s individualized approach means that even patients who don’t immediately qualify for same-day placement receive a clear path forward tailored to their anatomy and goals.

Comfort and Sedation During the Procedure

One of the most common concerns patients bring to their consultation is whether the procedure will be painful. Immediate implant placement is performed under local anesthesia, and for patients who prefer a deeper level of comfort, our practice offers IV sedation and general anesthesia to ensure you remain completely at ease throughout the surgery. Dr. El-Ounsi is fully certified in BLS, ACLS, and PALS, and his training in IV sedation at Bellevue Hospital means sedation is administered with the same precision applied to every other aspect of your care.

Most patients report that the procedure itself is far more comfortable than they anticipated, and post-operative discomfort is typically well-managed with prescribed medications and straightforward aftercare instructions.
