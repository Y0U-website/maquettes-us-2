How Surgical Exposure Works

The goal of surgical exposure is to uncover the impacted tooth, bond an orthodontic bracket to it, and allow traction forces to gradually guide it into the correct position in the arch. The procedure requires careful planning and a detailed understanding of the tooth’s position, angulation, and proximity to surrounding structures, which is why imaging and a coordinated treatment plan are essential before any intervention begins.

Two main techniques are used to expose impacted canines: the open and closed approaches. In the open technique, overlying soft tissue is removed or repositioned to allow the tooth to erupt naturally or with minimal guidance. In the closed technique, a bracket and chain are bonded to the tooth, the flap is sutured back into place, and orthodontic traction begins gradually, with the tooth moving through tissue until it emerges in the proper position. The choice between techniques depends on the tooth’s depth, orientation, and the surrounding anatomy.

What the Process Looks Like for Patients

Most surgical exposure procedures are completed in a single appointment, often with the help of sedation to maximize comfort. Here is what patients can generally expect:

Treatment timelines vary depending on the severity of impaction, the patient’s age, and the technique used, but outcomes with surgical-orthodontic management are consistently strong when approached collaboratively.

  • Imaging and planning: Cone beam CT scans and detailed records are reviewed to map the tooth’s exact position and plan the safest surgical approach.
  • The surgery: The area is numbed, sedation is administered as appropriate, and the impacted tooth is exposed. A bracket is bonded to the tooth surface, and a small chain is attached for orthodontic use.
  • Coordination with your orthodontist: Following the procedure, your orthodontist activates gentle traction forces at scheduled appointments to guide the tooth into position over several months.
  • Recovery: Post-operative discomfort is typically mild and well-managed with medication. Most patients return to normal activity quickly.

Why Timing and Coordination Matter

One of the most important factors in a successful impaction case is timing. Younger patients tend to see faster, more predictable results because bone is more responsive and the tooth has greater potential to move. However, adults are not without options; outcomes remain favorable with proper planning and technique. The critical step is not delaying once impaction is identified.

Equally important is the level of communication between the oral surgeon and the orthodontist.
