Frequently Asked Questions

What are steps for getting a dental implant?

The dental implant process at Pineview Dental involves several steps and typically takes several months from start to finish. Here’s what you can expect:

  • Consultation and Planning The implant process begins with a comprehensive evaluation using advanced digital imaging. Dr. Fogel assesses your oral health and bone structure and discusses your goals to create a personalized treatment plan.
  • Preparatory Procedures Some patients require preliminary treatments like tooth extraction or bone grafting before implant placement.
  • Implant Surgery Dr. Fogel precisely positions the titanium implant post into your jawbone using meticulous surgical techniques and the highest quality materials.
  • Healing Period Over the next 3 6 months, your jawbone grows around the implant (osseointegration). Dr. Fogel monitors your progress during brief checkup appointments.
  • Abutment Placement Once healing is complete, a connector piece called an abutment is attached to the implant, followed by a short healing period of about two weeks.
  • Custom Restoration Using precise digital impressions, Dr. Fogel works with a trusted custom dental lab to create a dental crown that matches your natural teeth in color, shape, and size.
  • Final Crown Placement Your custom restoration is attached to the abutment with careful adjustments to ensure proper fit and bite alignment.

Can dental implants be placed if I’ve had missing teeth for years?

Yes, you can still get dental implants even if you’ve had missing teeth for several years! Although your bone density begins to decrease when your tooth’s roots are no longer there to stimulate it and keep it healthy, this is a slow process.

And if you need additional bone density to support your implant, a bone graft or sinus lift procedure could be the answer you’ve been looking for.

The Negative Effects of Missing Teeth

Even though you can likely get a dental implant even years after losing your tooth, missing teeth can affect your oral health.

  • Jawbone shrinkage Once you lose a tooth, your body begins to resorb the jawbone tissue, making it smaller.
  • Shifting teeth When you have a gap in your smile, your other teeth may begin to drift into the space where your tooth once was, causing alignment issues.
  • Sinus expansion If you’re missing your upper back teeth, your sinus cavity could drop down into that area.
