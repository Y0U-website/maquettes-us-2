All-on-4: a full arch of fixed teeth, secured on four implants.

Conventional implant work replaces teeth one at a time and can run the better part of a year. All-on-4 carries a whole arch on four implants — the two at the back angled forward to catch bone that’s still dense, which is why most patients avoid the graft they were told they’d need. The bridge goes on the same day.

What actually happens when an implant goes in.

Six stages, months apart, most of them shorter than the appointment you imagined. It plays on its own — or pick a stage.

  • 01 3D scan and planning A CT scan maps bone height, width and nerve position. The implant is placed on screen, in software, before it is placed in you.
  • 02 Preparing the site Guided by that plan, the site is opened and shaped to the exact width and depth the implant needs — no more, no less.
  • 03 Placing the implant The titanium root goes in along the planned axis, to the planned depth. Local anesthetic, and you feel pressure rather than pain.
  • 04 Checking position and stability Angle, depth, fit and primary stability are measured and confirmed before anything is built on top of the implant.
  • 05 Integration and healing Over the following months bone grows onto the titanium surface and locks it in place. This is the part that cannot be rushed.
  • 06 The crown goes on The final crown is fitted to the implant. It looks like a tooth because it works like one — you bite with it, you floss around it, you forget about it.
