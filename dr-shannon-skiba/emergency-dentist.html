Emergency Dental Questions

Do you offer emergency dental appointments in Jonesboro?

Yes. We hold room in the daily schedule for urgent problems and can usually offer a same-day or next-day appointment. Call (770) 478-1001 as early in the day as possible — that is when the most options exist. Our hours are Monday through Thursday, 8am–3pm, with Friday by appointment.

Are you a 24-hour emergency dentist?

No, and we would rather tell you that plainly than have you drive here at midnight. We are not a 24-hour clinic. What we do offer is same-day or next-day appointments during our regular hours for urgent problems. If you have a genuine after-hours emergency — uncontrolled bleeding, swelling affecting breathing or swallowing, or facial trauma — go to an emergency room.

Should I go to the emergency room or a dentist for tooth pain?

For a dental problem, a dentist. An emergency room can give you antibiotics and pain relief, but hospitals are not equipped to repair, extract, or treat the tooth itself — so the pain typically returns once the medication wears off, and you still need a dentist. Go to an ER first for swelling that affects breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding, a suspected broken jaw, or a head injury.

What should I do for a knocked-out tooth?

Act within minutes, not hours. Pick the tooth up by the crown — the chewing surface — and never by the root. Do not scrub or wipe it clean. If it is dirty, rinse briefly with milk or saline. Keep it moist in milk or the person’s own saliva, and call (770) 478-1001 immediately. A baby tooth should not be reimplanted, but still call us.

How can I relieve tooth pain until my appointment?

Over-the-counter pain relief taken as directed on the package, a cold compress against the outside of the cheek, rinsing with warm salt water, and sleeping with your head elevated all help. Do not put aspirin directly on the gum — it burns the tissue. Heat on a swollen area can make an infection worse. These measures manage the symptom; they do not treat the cause.

Can a dental emergency wait until Monday?

Some can, some genuinely cannot. Facial swelling, a knocked-out permanent tooth, and uncontrolled bleeding need attention right away. A lost filling or a chip that does not hurt can usually wait a few days. If you are unsure, call and describe what is happening — we would rather advise you than have you guess.

Do you take emergency patients who have never been to your office?

Yes. You do not need to be an existing patient. Call (770) 478-1001 and we will get the necessary details over the phone so your visit is as quick as possible once you arrive.

What does emergency dental treatment involve?

It depends entirely on the cause. Dr. Skiba will examine the area, usually take an X-ray, and get you out of pain first. Depending on what is found that may mean a filling, a crown, an extraction, or treating an infection. Root canal treatment is referred to a trusted endodontist. You will be told what is going on and what the options are before anything is done.
