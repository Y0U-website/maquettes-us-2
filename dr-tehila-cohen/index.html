Dental crowns are tooth-shaped caps that fit over damaged teeth to restore them to normal functioning.

Made from ceramic, porcelain, metal alloys, or some combination of those materials, crowns provide a durable covering that reinforces the structure of your existing tooth. The materials used will depend somewhat on where the crown is located in your mouth and whether you prefer it to match the color of your teeth.

In order to have a crown installed, you will need a series of at least two appointments. During your first appointment, your dentist will remove any tooth decay and shape your tooth into a base that will hold the new crown in place. Then your dentist will take a mold of your teeth and send that mold to a lab to be cast into a crown that will perfectly fit into your mouth.

Once the crown has been formed and fitted properly and you are happy with the look, color, placement, and feel of it, the crown will be bonded permanently into place.

Dental crown implants function similarly to non-implant crowns in that they are made from the same materials and serve as natural teeth.
