Recognizing the Symptoms of Sleep Apnea

Sleep apnea affects nearly every system in the body. While many people associate it with snoring, the symptoms often extend far beyond nighttime noise.

Common daytime symptoms include:

  • Difficulty waking up in the morning
  • Excessive daytime drowsiness
  • Morning headaches
  • Chronic fatigue
  • Dry mouth or sore throat
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Brain fog
  • Memory problems
  • Irritability
  • Mood swings
  • Depression
  • Falling asleep while driving
  • Reduced productivity at work

Common nighttime symptoms include:

Sleep apnea is far more than an inconvenience. It is a serious medical condition that deserves professional diagnosis and treatment.

  • Loud snoring
  • Pauses in breathing
  • Choking or gasping during sleep
  • Restless sleep
  • Frequent awakenings
  • Teeth grinding
  • Night sweats
  • Frequent nighttime urination

Why Early Sleep Apnea Treatment Matters

Many patients live with sleep apnea for years before seeking treatment because they assume their symptoms are simply part of getting older or living a busy lifestyle.

Unfortunately, untreated sleep apnea places tremendous stress on your body every night. As oxygen levels repeatedly decrease, your cardiovascular system works harder, your sleep becomes fragmented, and your body never fully recovers. Treating sleep apnea early may help improve:

The sooner sleep apnea is identified, the sooner treatment can begin protecting both your health and your quality of life.

  • Mental clarity and focus
  • Athletic performance
  • Mood and emotional well-being

  • Snoring at night

Common Warning Signs of Sleep Apnea

One of the challenges of diagnosing sleep apnea is that patients are asleep when the symptoms occur. In many cases, it is a spouse or family member who first notices that something is wrong.

Some of the most common warning signs include loud, chronic snoring, gasping for air, pauses in breathing, restless sleep, frequent awakenings, waking with a dry mouth, morning headaches, and feeling exhausted even after sleeping through the night.

Dr. Gross has even coined the term “Second Hand Sleep Apnea,” referring to bed partners whose own sleep is disrupted because of another person’s loud snoring and repeated breathing interruptions.

If these symptoms sound familiar, scheduling a professional evaluation can provide valuable answers and peace of mind.

CPAP and Oral Appliance Therapy in Hilton Head Island, SC

Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) remains the gold standard for treating many cases of moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea. A CPAP machine works by delivering constant air pressure through a mask to prevent the airway from collapsing during sleep.

Although highly effective, some patients find CPAP difficult to use consistently due to issues such as mask discomfort, air leaks, dry mouth, claustrophobia, difficulty traveling with the equipment, or trouble sleeping while wearing the mask.

For patients with mild to moderate sleep apnea—or for those who simply cannot tolerate CPAP—we offer custom mandibular advancement devices that provide an effective alternative.

These appliances are small, comfortable, portable, and custom-made specifically for your mouth.

Custom Mandibular Advancement Devices

Mandibular advancement devices are custom oral appliances worn while you sleep. They work by gently repositioning the lower jaw forward to keep the airway open and reduce the likelihood of airway collapse throughout the night. Unlike over-the-counter products, every appliance we provide is custom fabricated to fit your bite comfortably and precisely. A properly fitted appliance not only improves comfort but also increases the likelihood that you’ll wear it consistently, leading to better long-term results. Orthodontic Sleep Apnea Treatment

Sleep apnea and orthodontic treatment often go hand in hand. In some patients, the position of the teeth and jaws contributes to a smaller airway and restricted breathing during sleep.

Dr. Gross has developed his own custom mandibular advancement device specifically for patients who are undergoing orthodontic treatment. This innovative approach allows patients to receive airway therapy without interrupting their orthodontic progress.

Because of Dr. Gross’ extensive background in orthodontics and physiologic dentistry, he is able to evaluate how bite alignment, jaw position, and airway health work together.

This comprehensive approach allows us to improve airway function and bite alignment while improving long-term oral health and maintaining overall comfort. Rather than treating each concern separately, we focus on how the entire system functions.
