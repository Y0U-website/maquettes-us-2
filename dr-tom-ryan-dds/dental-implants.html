The Dental Implant Procedure

Receiving dental implants typically involves several phases spread over 3-6 months, though the timeline varies based on your specific situation. This comprehensive approach ensures optimal integration and long-term success.

Initial Consultation and Planning

Your implant journey begins with a thorough evaluation to determine if you’re a suitable candidate. This includes:

During this phase, we’ll address any preliminary procedures needed, such as extractions, bone grafting , or gum disease care.

  • Comprehensive oral examination
  • Detailed dental and medical history review
  • 3D cone beam CT imaging to assess bone volume and density
  • Discussion of your goals and expectations
  • Development of a customized treatment plan

Implant Placement Surgery

The implant surgery itself is precisely planned using advanced imaging technology:

Most patients report minimal discomfort following the procedure and can return to normal activities within a day or two. We monitor patients throughout the recovery period, though complications are rare with biocompatible titanium materials.

  • Local anesthesia ensures your comfort during this minor surgery ( sedation options are also available)
  • Small incisions in the gum tissue expose the jawbone
  • Precisely sized channels are created in the bone
  • The implant is carefully positioned for optimal support and aesthetics
  • Healing caps may be placed, and the gum tissue is sutured closed

Healing and Osseointegration

After placement, the implants need time to integrate with your jawbone through a process called osseointegration:

  • This biological process typically takes 3-6 months
  • During this time, the titanium implant and bone cells grow together
  • This integration creates the strong foundation needed for long-term success
  • In some cases, temporary prosthetics may be provided during this healing phase

Final Restoration Placement

Once osseointegration is complete, you’ll return for your final restoration:

  • Abutments are attached to the implant fixtures
  • Detailed impressions are taken for crafting your custom prosthetics
  • Your restoration (crown, bridge, or denture) is precisely placed
  • Your bite is checked and adjusted for optimal function and comfort

Frequently Asked Questions about Dental Implants in Tinley Park, IL

How much do dental implants cost?

The cost of dental implants varies based on the number of teeth being replaced and any preliminary procedures needed. A single dental implant typically ranges from $3,000 to $5,000, including the implant post, abutment, and crown. Full mouth restoration with All-on-4 implants ranges from $15,000 to $30,000 per arch. While the upfront cost is higher than dentures or bridges, implants last decades and often prove more cost-effective long-term. Many insurance plans cover a portion of implant costs, and we offer flexible payment plans to make treatment accessible.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper care, dental implants can last 25 years or longer—often a lifetime. The titanium implant post itself has a 95-98 % success rate and typically remains functional indefinitely once osseointegration is complete. The crown attached to the implant may need replacement after 10-15 years due to normal wear, but the implant post remains secure. Longevity depends on maintaining good oral hygiene, attending regular dental visits, avoiding tobacco, and not grinding your teeth. This durability makes implants the most cost-effective long-term solution for tooth replacement.

Does getting dental implants hurt?

The implant procedure itself is performed under local anesthesia, so you won’t feel pain during surgery. Most patients report the discomfort is less than having a tooth extracted. You may experience mild soreness, swelling, and bruising for a few days after surgery, which can be managed with over-the-counter pain medication. Most people return to normal activities within 1-2 days. Sedation options are available for anxious patients or those undergoing extensive procedures. Any significant pain beyond the first week should be reported to your dentist immediately.

How long does the dental implant process take?

The complete dental implant process typically takes 3-6 months from initial surgery to final restoration. This timeline includes the implant surgery, a 2-4 month healing period for osseointegration (bone fusion), and fabrication and placement of the final crown or denture. Some cases require preliminary procedures like extractions or bone grafting, which can extend the timeline. Immediate load implants like All-on-4 allow you to leave with temporary teeth the same day as surgery, though final restorations still require several months. Your specific timeline depends on your unique situation.

What is the dental implant success rate?

Dental implants have an excellent success rate of 95-98 % overall, with proper case selection and technique. Success rates are slightly higher in the lower jaw (98 %) than the upper jaw (90-95 %) due to bone density differences. Factors affecting success include adequate bone quality, good oral hygiene, not smoking, and proper surgical technique. Most failures occur within the first year if osseointegration doesn’t complete properly. With an experienced implant dentist and proper aftercare, the vast majority of patients enjoy successful, long-lasting results.

What can’t you eat with dental implants?

Once fully healed, dental implants allow you to eat virtually any food without restrictions—one of their major advantages over dentures. During the initial healing period (first few weeks after surgery), stick to soft foods like yogurt, soup, mashed potatoes, and smoothies. Avoid hard, crunchy, chewy, or very hot foods that could disturb the surgical site. After osseointegration completes (3-6 months), you can resume normal eating including steak, apples, corn on the cob, and other challenging foods. Avoid extremely hard items like ice or hard candy that could damage any teeth.

Can smokers get dental implants?

Smokers can get dental implants, but smoking significantly reduces success rates and increases complication risks. Nicotine restricts blood flow, impairs healing, and interferes with osseointegration. Smokers have implant failure rates 2-3 times higher than non-smokers. Many dentists recommend quitting at least two weeks before surgery and throughout the healing period. Some dentists won’t place implants in heavy smokers due to high failure risk. If you’re unable to quit permanently, minimizing smoking during the critical healing months substantially improves your chances of success.

How do wisdom teeth affect dental implants?

Wisdom teeth, if impacted or misaligned, can complicate the process of receiving implants. These may crowd or interfere with the available space in your jaw. If your wisdom teeth are causing discomfort or dental issues, your dentist may recommend removal before moving forward with implant treatment.
