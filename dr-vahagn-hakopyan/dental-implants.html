Are You a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Not every person is an ideal candidate for dental implants.

The dentist usually conducts several examinations first to assess the potential for dental implants.

An ideal candidate for dental implants is a person who is in good general and oral health.

A primary requisite is adequate bone in the jaw to support the implant. Since dental implants are intimately connected with the gums, having healthy gum tissue free of periodontal disease is crucial.

Dental Implant Procedure

Getting dental implants is a life-changing realization of a healthy and beautiful smile.

To make this journey successful, you need to understand all the steps involved from the beginning, so we will carefully plan the course of your implant journey with you before we begin. We want you to understand each step and be able to look forward to each step with confidence.

Dental implants are installed in two stages, explained below.

Stage 1 - Getting Dental Implants

The implants are inserted by an oral surgeon at his or her dental office. Implants require healthy jaw bone , so in cases where a patient doesn’t have enough jaw bone, a bone graft is inserted. This may not be necessary when working with an implant that requires less bone, such as dentures retained by implants where fewer individual implants are used.

Once the implants are placed and the gums are sutured, healing takes a few months. During this time, the implants fuse to the bone, becoming permanent and acting as “roots” for the crowns, bridges, or dentures that will be placed.

Stage 2 - Dental Implant Restoration

After the mouth has completely healed, the crowns, bridges, or dentures are attached at our office. Crowns may be attached to the implants by being screwed in or cemented in place in the case of certain types of implants.

At an earlier time, you will have chosen the shade of your artificial teeth. It’s important to note that artificial teeth cannot be whitened with whitening treatments, so when you select the shade of your crowns, bridge, or denture, this is your opportunity go for a smile as white as you want it to be.

Endosteal Implants

Endosteal, or in-the-bone implants, are the most common implant type. They’re recommended to people who are already using removable dentures or bridges.

Endosteal implants are shaped like a screw, cylinder, or blade and surgically inserted in the jawbone to support one or more prosthetic teeth. Each implant has the capacity to hold at least one tooth, and some are capable of holding multiple teeth.

Subperiosteal Implants

Subperiosteal, or on-the-bone implants, are placed on top of the jawbone, with a metal framework under the gum that holds it in place. This procedure is not so common and is recommended to patients who

  • Can’t use regular dentures
  • Have insufficient jawbone for an endosteal implant
  • Can’t or don’t want to go through bone augmentation

Same-Day Dental Implants

Same-day dental implants, or immediate-load dental implants or teeth-in-a-day, are implants and temporary teeth placed in a single appointment. This type can only be used if there’s enough healthy bone for immediate support.

Mini Dental Implants (MDIs)

Mini dental implants are small, toothpick-sized implants that stabilize lower dentures through less invasive techniques. They are used when the patient has significant bone loss or loose dentures.

All-on-4 Implants

All-on-4 implants are a complete set of top or bottom replacement teeth. They’re recommended for people who have lost a significant number of teeth and want an entire arch of new teeth.

What Happens After Dental Implant Restoration

Dental implants are a permanent dental restoration procedure. After a brief adjustment period, you should be able to speak, eat, and swallow effortlessly. There will be several follow-up visits to ensure the implant and the surrounding teeth and gums are healthy.

Your dental implant will require conscientious at-home care, just like you would care for regular teeth by brushing and flossing.

There is a slight chance for your body to reject the implants, but the success rate of dental implants is about 90-95 %. Some people are at greater risk of failure, such as smokers and people with certain health conditions like diabetes. We will discuss these factors with you at your consultation appointment.
