Types of Dental Implants

Dental implants are devices that are used to support replacement teeth. They come in three types: endosseal, subperiosteal, and all-on-4.

  • Endosseal implants are placed directly into the jawbone. They provide the most stable support and are often used in patients with healthy jawbones and teeth.
  • Subperiosteal implants are placed under the gum but above the jawbone. This type of implant is good for patients who have dentures or dental bridges that need extra support.
  • All-on-4 dental implants involve installing a full arch of dental implants in a single procedure. These implants are good for patients who want stable support should their jaw become injured or damaged. Finally, mini dental implants are smaller implants used to stabilize dentures and bridges. Potential Risks and Complications of Getting a Dental Implant

If you’re thinking about getting a dental implant, there are several things to consider. First and foremost, this procedure is not without its risks and complications. Pain or swelling during and after the implant may occur, which can be caused by the material used in the device or the anesthesia used to numb your mouth. Infection at the implant site is also possible, which can lead to swelling and pain. Nerve damage, which may cause pain, numbness, or tingling in your natural teeth or gums, is another potential complication of the procedure.

Additionally, improper placement of the implant can lead to tooth loss due to bone inflammation or infection around the implant site. However, with careful planning and monitoring, dental implants can be an effective means of replacing missing teeth and enhancing overall oral health.

Recovery Process for Dental Implants

Following aftercare instructions from the dental professional is vital during the healing process for dental implants. This includes keeping the area around the implant free of bacteria, avoiding biting and chewing on the implant site, and not smoking or using tobacco products around the implant.

It’s also important to understand the healing process for dental implants. The bone around a dental implant grows over it, forming a bridge between two pieces of bone. This allows for tooth replacement without any surgical intervention.

The bone tissue surrounding an implant forms new blood vessels and nervous system connections, which help to restore the normal function and appearance of your tooth.
