Keys to Preventing Dental Emergencies

Although accidents can happen unexpectedly, many common dental injuries can be prevented. That’s why it’s so important to incorporate healthy habits into your daily routine and break harmful ones! With this in mind, here are some recommendations from our team at Stratford Smiles:

Schedule a Dental Appointment Twice a Year

Remember, your biannual dental visits aren’t optional; they play a crucial role in preventing dental emergencies in Stratford. So, if it has been more than six months since your last appointment with Dr. Yuchen Sheng, then it’s time to get your next one on the calendar! That way, she can screen for oral cancer, examine the condition of your teeth and gums, and determine if any restorative care is needed. One of our dedicated dental hygienists will also perform a cleaning so plaque and tartar are removed before they negatively impact your oral health.

Maintain a Good Oral Hygiene Regimen At Home

Of course, taking care of your smile on a daily basis is crucial as well. The first step is to use a soft-bristle toothbrush and fluoridated toothpaste to brush your teeth each morning and evening (and for two full minutes each time). Then, you need to floss and rinse with mouthwash at least once a day. While completing your oral hygiene regimen, make sure to clean your tongue, too!

Eat Mostly Healthy, Nutrient-Dense Meals

Simply put, your body needs essential vitamins, minerals, and nutrients to thrive. So, it’s important that you eat mostly healthy, well-balanced meals that are filled with seasonal vegetables, fresh fruits, and the like. At the same time, do your best to limit your intake of cavity-causing foods, like ice cream, potato chips, and cookies.

Wear a Mouthguard During Sports

Oftentimes, patients assume that they only need to wear a mouthguard if they play a contact sport, like football or soccer. However, if you accidentally fall while riding your bike, you chronically clench your teeth while lifting weights, or you get hit in the face with a tennis ball, you could wind up with a serious dental injury as well. So, it’s important to protect your smile!

Tip: Don’t buy a one-size-fits-all mouthguard online! Instead, schedule an appointment with our team at Stratford Smiles so we can have a custom one made.

Break Harmful Habits, Like Smoking

So far, we’ve shared what you should do to protect your smile. However, there are also a few things that you should NOT do. To begin, you should steer clear of all tobacco products. You also should avoid biting your nails, chewing on ice cubes, or using your teeth as tools to open things. If breaking harmful habits like these proves to be difficult, don’t hesitate to get in touch with us so we can share helpful resources and recommendations.

What Do I Do in a Dental Emergency?

Keep Calm & Reduce Bleeding

Dental injuries can turn a normal day into a stressful and painful experience, but it s important to remain calm. Remember that we are here to assist you. By taking a few deep breaths and assessing the situation, you ll be better equipped to make the right decisions in an emergency. If your dental injury or accident is causing bleeding, apply pressure to the affected area with a clean piece of gauze. Keep the gauze in place until the bleeding stops or until you can reach our office for further treatment.

Control Swelling & Pain

Pain medication such as ibuprofen or acetaminophen can temporarily reduce pain or swelling. Avoid blood-thinning medications such as aspirin as they can make it difficult to control the bleeding. Along with medication, you can also reduce pain by using a cold compress, such as an ice pack, on the cheek or lip near the affected area.

Call Us Right Away

After composing your dental emergency, call us right away. Even if you re not certain if your situation is an emergency, our team will evaluate the severity of the injury and schedule an appointment as soon as possible to provide the necessary care. That way, you can get out of pain and restore the health of your tooth.

Frequently Asked Dental Emergency Questions

What should I do if I’ve knocked out a tooth?

If you have accidentally knocked out a tooth, it s important to act quickly to increase the chances of saving it. To recover the tooth, handle it by the crown (the top) rather than the root to prevent any further damage. Rinse the tooth with warm water to remove any debris, being careful not to scrub or use any abrasive materials. If possible, try to gently place the tooth back into its socket. Do not force it into place as it may cause additional damage. If you cannot put the tooth back, store it in a container of cold milk to keep it hydrated until you can get to the dentist.

For knocked-out teeth, time is of the essence, so contact us immediately to get guidance on how to handle the emergency and ensure that we are prepared for your visit. Remember, to save the tooth and avoid the need for prosthetics, the tooth should be replaced and splinted within 1-2 hours.

What should I do if my dental work has fallen out?

If you ve lost a piece of dental work, such as a filling or crown, it s important to act quickly to protect your oral health. Call us to schedule an appointment so we can treat it as soon as possible. Losing dental work can expose your teeth to bacteria and lead to further complications. Gently place the dental work in a clean plastic bag or container and bring it with you to your emergency appointment. We understand that losing dental work can be stressful, but rest assured that we ll do everything we can to restore your smile.

What should I do about a loose tooth?

If you have a loose tooth from a dental injury, get in touch with us and schedule a same-day appointment. Not treating it in time can damage the nerves supporting your tooth, causing it to fall out. Toothaches

Is My Toothache a Dental Emergency? Although toothaches don’t always warrant same-day care, we do recommend calling us when you first realize that something is wrong. That way, we can learn more about your symptoms, including how long they’ve persisted, and determine if an emergency visit is necessary.

How You Should Handle a Toothache: Until your appointment with Dr. Yuchen Sheng, we recommend rinsing with warm saltwater periodically, not chewing on that side of your mouth, and avoiding crunchy, sticky, acidic, and sugary foods entirely. If needed, you can take OTC pain medication as well.

How We Treat Toothaches: There are several ways we can treat a toothache in Stratford, including with a dental crown or root canal. Ultimately, the restorative treatment that’s best for you will depend on what’s causing your discomfort, which is why it’s so important for us to complete a comprehensive oral exam.

Chipped Tooth

Is a Chipped Tooth a Dental Emergency? The answer depends largely on one question: how severe is the damage? For example, if the chip is substantial enough that you’re experiencing abnormal symptoms, like sensitivity, then you should call us right away to schedule an appointment.

How You Should Handle a Chipped Tooth: In addition to gently rinsing your mouth with clean, room-temperature water, we recommend placing some dental wax or sugar-free gum over the sharp edge of your tooth. Until your appointment, stick to extremely soft foods (i.e., plain yogurt, cottage cheese, non-hot vegetable soup) since they require minimal chewing.

How We Treat Chipped Teeth: There are several ways we can restore the missing structure of your tooth, including cosmetic dental bonding, Lumineers, and veneers. Ultimately, the one that’s best for you will depend on a few factors, including the severity of the chip, your aesthetic goals, and your budget.

Cracked Tooth

Is a Cracked Tooth a Dental Emergency? Yes, so it’s important that you act fast! Call us ASAP so we can schedule an emergency appointment and give you helpful instructions on how to prevent your tooth from breaking further in the meantime.

How You Should Handle a Cracked Tooth: Fortunately, there are several ways you can alleviate any discomfort you’re experiencing and protect your tooth from further harm, including taking OTC pain medication and steering clear of raw almonds, tortilla chips, popcorn, and other crunchy foods.

How We Treat Cracked Teeth: First, we need to assess the severity of the damage. If it’s minor enough that your tooth can be saved with a dental crown, then that’s how we will proceed. If it’s so severe that it needs to be removed, then we will perform an extraction before filling the open space with a dental bridge or implant.

Very Sensitive Teeth

Are Very Sensitive Teeth a Dental Emergency? Dental sensitivity doesn’t always warrant an emergency visit. With that said, we do recommend giving us a call if you start to experience discomfort or any other abnormal symptoms. From there, we can ask you a few questions and determine if a same-day visit to our office is warranted.

How You Should Handle Very Sensitive Teeth: Fortunately, there are a few ways you can alleviate dental sensitivity in the short term, including taking OTC pain medication as directed, sticking to room-temperature water, and avoiding acidic and sugary foods.

How We Treat Very Sensitive Teeth: First, we need to pinpoint what the root of the problem is. For some, it’s a small cavity, which can be treated with a dental crown. For others, it’s a serious infection, which warrants a root canal.

Knocked-Out Tooth

Is My Knocked-Out Tooth a Dental Emergency? Yes, so it’s important that you call us ASAP. Over the phone, we will schedule an appointment at our office within the hour and walk you through how to handle this dental injury until you arrive.

How You Should Handle a Knocked-Out Tooth: First, locate the tooth and pick it up by the crown (the chewing surface). Then, use clean water to carefully rinse away dirt and any other debris. When you’re done, either place the tooth back in its socket or put it in a clean container filled with milk.
