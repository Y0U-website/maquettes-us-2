What Are Dental Implants?

Your teeth consist of two main parts: the crown and the roots. The crown is what you see when you smile, while the roots are hidden under the gums to keep your pearly whites in place. Traditional tooth replacement options like dental bridges and dentures seamlessly replace the crowns of missing teeth, but leave the roots missing. Naturally, this negatively impacts the stability and comfort of these prosthetics.

Dental implants are the only option available that rebuilds teeth from the roots up. A dental implant is a small titanium post that is inserted directly into the jawbone just like the roots of a natural tooth. Once the titanium has fused with the surrounding bone tissue, it can be used to support a custom restoration. The final result is a replacement that looks, feels, and functions, just like natural.

Day-to-Day Benefit

Tooth loss detracts from your daily quality of life because you rely on two complete arches of teeth to thoroughly chew food, clearly pronounce different words, and smile authentically without feeling self-conscious. Dental implants can help by:

  • Restoring chewing power. Once your jawbone fuses with the titanium rod of your implant, you’ll regain an estimated 80 % to 90 % of your original bite force to enjoy all your favorite foods.
  • Enhanced enunciation. Gaps in your grin can cause a lisp, slur, or other changes in your speech patterns that can be overcome once your restorations are placed.
  • Easy maintenance. Your basic dental hygiene routine of brushing and flossing twice daily effectively keeps them in good shape.
  • Boosted confidence. They’re customized for seamless results so you can look and feel like the best version of yourself.

Health Benefits

There are additional advantages to having dental implants that might be less immediately noticeable but are just as, if not more, significant. These restorations can also contribute to your oral and overall well-being in the following ways:

  • Jawbone preservation. Your jaw starts thinning without roots in place to stimulate new bone growth every time you bite down, and these replacements are the only ones surgically embedded in your bone to keep it active and healthy.
  • Prevent misalignments. Remaining teeth will likely shift to fill in spaces left behind by missing neighbors, which can wear down your enamel and increase your vulnerability to cavities or injuries. Filling in the gaps keeps everything in its proper place.
  • Protect oral health. Because they’re easier to clean than dentures and dental bridges, you’re less likely to develop tooth decay, gum disease, and other dental concerns.

Long-Term Benefits

Once your jawbone fuses with the metal rod of your implant, it essentially becomes a permanent part of your smile, so you can enjoy these long-term advantages:

Who Dental Implants Can Help

Missing 1 Tooth

To replace a single tooth, you will need a single dental implant. A custom-made crown goes on top of the implant to replace the top part of the tooth. This is among the most straightforward dental implant procedures and can prevent further tooth loss and other oral health complications.

Missing Multiple Teeth

An implant-retained bridge, which typically consists of two implants and a bridge to go on top of them, may work well for you if you are missing two, three, or more teeth in a row. If your tooth loss has occurred at multiple locations throughout your mouth, an implant-retained partial denture may work better for you.

Missing All Teeth

If you currently wear a denture or are planning to get one soon, talk with Dr. Beliveau about implant dentures. Traditional dentures rest on the gum tissue and are held in place by natural suction or denture adhesive. Special care and maintenance are required to keep dentures in good shape. Over time, the ridges of the gums wear down from friction. When this occurs, dentures must be relined. Some people never achieve a great fit, so they struggle with loose, wobbly, uncomfortable dentures.

Dr. Beliveau may recommend a few dental implants to hold your denture in place. In some situations, we can retrofit existing dentures with implant clasps, but usually a new denture is required. Implant dentures snap securely to dental implant posts for a solid, reliable fit. They’re easy to care for, and our implant denture patients rave about improved quality of life.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Immediately following your dental implant surgery, it is important to slow down and allow your smile time to heal properly. To that end, be sure to avoid any strenuous activity for at least 24 hours, leave any clots forming in your surgical sites untouched, and follow these aftercare rules as closely as possible:

  • Never spit. If you must empty your mouth, either swallow your saliva or allow it to drain into a tissue or sink.
  • Don’t drink from a straw until Dr. Beliveau has given you the green light.
  • Do not smoke for at least the first day after your procedure (longer if possible).
  • Avoid touching your incision sites with your fingers or tongue.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

It is completely normal to experience mild side effects following your dental implant surgery, especially in the first few days after they’ve been placed. These can include:

Should your symptoms not improve within about a week, or if they get worse instead of better, please let us know as soon as possible.

  • Intermittent bleeding that may last several days. To help mitigate this, gently bite down on rolled gauze or a dry washcloth.
  • Swelling may continue for the first 72 hours to over a week. You can reduce some of the inflammation and ease discomfort with warm or cold compresses applied to the face directly over the surgical site(s).
  • Some general discomfort is also normal; you can use either prescription or OTC pain relievers as directed by Beliveau .

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

Immediately following your dental implant surgery, Dr. Beliveau recommends eating a soft diet full of non-abrasive foods that are easy to chew. Here are some suggestions to have on hand:

You can begin eating a normal diet again as soon as you’re ready, but Dr. Beliveau recommends that you limit anything too hard or crunchy for as long as possible and avoid chewing directly on your implant site(s), so they have more time to heal.

  • Mashed potatoes
  • Scrambled eggs

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Maintaining your oral hygiene with dental implants is imperative to their success. Thus, it is important to begin caring for them properly as soon as possible.

  • On the day after your surgery, you can resume brushing your teeth as usual but be extremely careful around the implant site(s).
  • Be sure to rinse your mouth well with warm saltwater or prescription mouth rinse after each meal. Avoid using any mouthwash that has a high alcohol content like Listerine or Scope, as they can dry out the mouth.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Congratulations on restoring your beautiful smile with the help of dental implants! Though your new prosthetics have the amazing potential to keep your gorgeous grin looking complete, strong, and healthy for a lifetime, you’ll still need to take excellent care of them to make them last. That’s because unfortunately, much like your real teeth, if you don’t, they could become damaged or infected, which may lead to dental implant failure. The good news is that a few simple precautions and healthy habits can help you enjoy your newly rebuilt smile for the rest of your life!

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

While it’s true that your dental implants cannot get cavities like real teeth can, you’ll still need to practice excellent dental hygiene to prevent dangerous oral health conditions like gum disease that can cause your dental implants to fail. To protect your new smile, follow these care steps at home:

  • Brush your teeth (both real and prosthetic) twice daily.
  • Floss once a day.

Eat a Healthy Diet

One of the major benefits of dental implants is that you can eat whatever you want, so go ahead and indulge in a full, nutritious diet. However, be aware that consuming foods that are too hard, sticky, or sugary can be problematic to your prosthetics. Instead, stick to a diet that’s high in calcium and vitamin C to help keep your jawbone strong and your gum tissues healthy enough to support your newly rebuilt smile.

Break Bad Habits

Bad habits can be hard to break, but if you don’t, they can damage or cause your dental implants to fail. If you are a tobacco user, it’s important to quit, as this can slow down the healing process and encourage infection. Furthermore, don’t use your teeth as tools or chew on hard objects like fingernails or pen caps, as these can damage your restorations.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Though your new dental implants are strong and durable, they’re not indestructible. If you play sports, always be sure to wear a mouth guard to protect your smile. If you have bruxism (teeth grinding), wear your prescribed nightguard, as this will help protect your dental implant from excessive wear and tear.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Regular dental checkups and cleanings are essential for keeping your mouth healthy, whether you have one, multiple, or an entire mouth full of dental implants. These are Dr. Beliveau’s opportunities to monitor the condition of your gums, jawbones, restorations, and any remaining teeth, as well as to keep your smile looking and feeling sparkly clean. Remember, early intervention can often catch dangerous dental problems before they become more painful and expensive, and regular check-ups can save you both time, money, and your newly rebuilt smile!

Can I get dental implants if I have gum disease?

Gum disease is preventable; however, at least 50 % of adults have a form of it. The high prevalence of the infection makes it a leading cause of tooth loss. If you’ve lost teeth from gum disease, you need a viable solution to fill the empty spaces in your smile.

Unfortunately, if the infection is active, you cannot get dental implants. Don’t worry, you can become a candidate for them with periodontal therapy. After the infection is treated, you can replace your missing teeth with dental implants, but you may need additional procedures to repair any damage caused by the infection, like bone grafting.

Am I too old for dental implants?

Age is not necessarily a determining factor for your candidacy for dental implants. As long as you’ve finished growing, age has nothing to do with the success of the treatment. Instead, it’s determined by other factors, such as your oral and general health, lifestyle habits, and certain medications you take.
