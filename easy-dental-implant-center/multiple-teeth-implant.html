Types of Restorations for Multiple Missing Teeth

At West Orange Easy Dental Implant Center , we offer several options for replacing multiple missing teeth. The best choice depends on how many teeth are missing and where they’re located. Let’s break down some popular solutions:

Implant-Supported Bridge

If you’re missing multiple teeth in a row, an implant-supported bridge may be the best solution. Unlike a traditional bridge, which relies on neighboring teeth for support, an implant bridge is anchored by implants alone. Typically, we place one implant for each missing tooth, or, if spacing allows, suspend a pontic (false tooth) between two implants.

This approach provides stability, protects surrounding teeth, and maintains jawbone health.

Full-Arch Restoration

For patients missing most or all of their teeth in one arch, we offer full-arch restoration options like fixed complete bridges, implant-supported overdentures, and implant-retained dentures.

  • Fixed Complete Bridge : This solution uses implants to support a permanent bridge across the full arch. It looks and functions just like natural teeth.
  • Implant-Supported Overdenture : This option provides the stability of implants but allows for a removable denture, making cleaning easy.
  • Implant-Retained Denture : This removable option snaps onto implants for improved stability compared to traditional dentures.

The Dental Implant Process for Multiple Teeth Replacement

Restoring multiple missing teeth with implants involves a few key steps, each one essential for a successful outcome.

Step 1: Consultation and Planning

Your journey starts with an initial consultation where we’ll evaluate your dental and medical history, perform an exam, and take imaging like CT scans to assess your jawbone density. This process helps us understand your unique needs and develop a customized treatment plan.

Personal Tip: Always ask questions during this stage! Patients who are informed about the process tend to feel more relaxed and confident.

Step 2: Implant Placement

Once we’ve confirmed you’re a good candidate for implants, we’ll schedule your implant placement surgery. Each implant is positioned precisely in the jaw to provide optimal support. For some patients, a bone graft may be required beforehand if there’s been significant bone loss.

Step 3: Healing and Osseointegration

After implant placement, a healing period allows the implants to fuse with the bone in a process called osseointegration . This fusion is what gives implants their incredible strength and durability. While healing, some patients wear a temporary denture for comfort and aesthetics.

Step 4: Attaching the Final Restoration

Once osseointegration is complete, we’ll attach the final restoration—whether that’s a bridge, overdenture, or full-arch prosthesis. We carefully fit and adjust the restoration to ensure it looks natural, feels comfortable, and functions seamlessly.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: How many implants are needed for a full-arch restoration?

A: A full-arch restoration typically requires 4-8 implants per arch, depending on your specific needs and bone structure.

Q: How long is the healing period after implant surgery?

A: Healing usually takes 4-6 months, during which the implants integrate with the jawbone to provide a stable foundation.

Q: Will implants feel like my natural teeth?

A: Yes! Implants are designed to function like natural teeth, providing stability, comfort, and a natural look.

Q: What if I don’t have enough bone for implants?

A: In cases of low bone density, a bone graft can often be performed to strengthen the jaw, making implants a viable option.
