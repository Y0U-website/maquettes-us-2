When to See a General Dentist vs a Specialist

Your general dentist is the first call for most dental concerns. Routine checkups, cleanings, cavities, mild gum disease, and simple extractions are typically handled in general practice.

When advanced treatment is needed, your general dentist coordinates referrals to the appropriate specialist and stays involved in the overall plan. Starting with a general evaluation helps avoid delays and unnecessary appointments.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I need more than a routine dental cleaning?

Many people notice bleeding gums or mild discomfort and assume it is normal. These signs are easy to ignore, especially if they come and go.

If bleeding, swelling, a bad taste, or gum recession continue despite regular cleanings, it may be time for an exam. Checking gum pockets and inflammation early often allows care to stay simpler and more conservative.

What does general dentistry include?

General dentistry covers everyday dental care such as exams, check-ups, cleanings, dental X-rays, cavity fillings, and hygiene instruction. These services focus on prevention, early diagnosis, and treatment of common concerns. The goal is to keep problems small and avoid more complex treatment later.

How are ozone and PRF used in biologic gum care?

It is normal to want clarity before agreeing to newer treatment options. Patients often want to understand both the purpose and safety behind these tools.

In biologic gum care, medical-grade ozone may be used locally to help reduce harmful bacteria in specific areas. PRF, which is made from a small sample of your own blood, may be used to support healing and tissue response. These options are recommended based on exam findings and are not needed for every patient.

When should I call a general dentist instead of waiting?

You should call a general dentist if you notice pain, sensitivity, swelling, bleeding gums, or visible changes to a tooth, but it is often better to be seen before symptoms become noticeable.

Many dental issues develop quietly, and early evaluation can prevent discomfort and more complex treatment. Your general dentist can assess what is happening and guide next steps before problems progress.

How often should I schedule dental cleanings?

Many adults wonder if twice a year is enough. The right schedule depends on gum health, buildup patterns, and individual risk factors.

Most people benefit from cleanings every six months, while others may need more frequent visits to keep inflammation under control. Your dentist will recommend a schedule that helps prevent problems from coming back, with consistency mattering more than perfection.
