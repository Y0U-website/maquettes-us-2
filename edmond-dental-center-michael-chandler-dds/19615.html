Frequently Asked Questions

What should I know about dental implants?

Dental implants are the closest thing to natural teeth and have many advantages over other tooth replacement options. A dental implant is a titanium screw that is placed into the jawbone to act as a replacement for a natural tooth root. Once the implant has healed, an artificial tooth (or teeth), such as a dental crown, can be attached.

Advantages of Dental Implants

Dental implants look and feel like natural teeth, which means they won t affect your speech or eating habits. They re also very strong and durable, so you can eat all your favorite foods without worry. And because dental implants are embedded in your jawbone, they provide stimulation that helps keep your bone healthy and strong.

Aftercare and Maintenance

After you ve gotten dental implants, it s essential to take care of them just like you would your natural teeth. That means brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and seeing us for regular checkups and cleanings. With proper care, your dental implants can last a lifetime!

The Dental Implant Process

Before placing your dental implant , Dr. Chandler administers a local anesthetic to numb the area so you don’t feel any pain or discomfort.

Is it better to get a dental implant or a bridge?

Losing teeth can have a significant impact on both the function and appearance of your smile. Two common solutions to address the issue of missing teeth are dental bridges and dental implants. Both dental bridges and implants have their own unique set of benefits, depending on your individual needs and oral health.

Dental Implants vs. Bridges

A dental implant replaces your tooth’s root with a durable titanium post designed to support jaw health. Over the next few months, the post will then naturally fuse to your jawbone. Once your mouth heals from implant surgery, your dentist will test the implant to make sure it’s secure enough to hold your new dental crowns. The crowns are then screwed into the implant and sealed into place, preserving your mouth’s health and integrity. Dental implants typically last a lifetime and retain your face’s natural shape.

A dental bridge is commonly supported by your remaining teeth and requires your teeth to be whittled down to support the bridge. Bridges have a lifespan of around ten years and can be replaced with new, durable crowns.

What disqualifies you from dental implants?

Dental implants require the patient to be in good general health and have enough healthy bone structure to support the implant.

During your implant evaluation, Dr. Chandler uses x-rays and 3-D imaging to assess your overall oral health and bone structure. For example, if you have periodontal (gum) issues or tooth decay, Dr. Chandler may recommend treatment to address those concerns before proceeding with implant placement.

If you lack bone structure to support the implant successfully, Dr. Chandler may recommend a bone grafting procedure to rebuild your bone structure for proper implant support. If bone grafting is recommended for you, he will discuss the process and details of completing the procedure.

Alternative Tooth Replacement

If you decide that you can t or don t want to do implant placement surgery, a dental bridge may be an option to replace missing teeth. A bridge uses dental crowns as anchors, with prosthetic teeth attached in between to replace missing teeth.
