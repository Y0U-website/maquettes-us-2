How to Floss

Brushing is imperative, but what about the areas that the bristles can’t reach? That’s where flossing comes in. Flossing removes plaque that was left behind, but only with the proper technique.

If your gums hurt while flossing, you may need to apply less pressure. If your gums bleed or are a little sore while you floss during the first week, don’t be alarmed. Over time your gums will heal and any bleeding should stop.

How to Care for Sensitive Teeth

Teeth can become especially sensitive to hot and cold following dental treatment. If the mouth is kept clean, this will be short-lived. If it isn’t, the sensitivity will persist and become more severe. Consult Dr. Rezvan if your teeth are especially sensitive, as he can recommend medicated toothpaste or mouth rinse made especially for sensitive teeth.

How to Choose Oral Hygiene Products

The abundance of oral hygiene products on the market can make it hard to pick the best one for you and your specific dental needs. Here are a few suggestions that we recommend after seeing them work successfully for most patients:

Other questions? Dr. Rezvan can answer them and help you find the products best for you.

  • Electric toothbrushes (we recommend Rotadent and Interplak ) are safe, effective, and gentle enough for most users.
  • Water spraying devices, known as oral irrigators, will rinse your mouth thoroughly after brushing, but will not remove plaque. There is no substitute for brushing and flossing.
  • Some toothbrushes have a rubber tip on the handle intended for massaging the gums after brushing, but if used improperly, could cause injury. Ask Dr. Rezvan about proper use before you try these at home.
  • Fluoride toothpastes and mouth rinses can reduce tooth decay for adults by as much as 40 % (but remember they shouldn’t be used by children under 6).
  • While tartar control toothpastes reduce tartar above the gum line, they have not been proven to reduce the early stages of gum disease (which starts below the gum line).
  • Plaque fighting rinses, used in conjunction with brushing and flossing, contain ingredients that may help control signs of early gum disease.
