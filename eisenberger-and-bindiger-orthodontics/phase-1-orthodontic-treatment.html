What is Phase 1 Orthodontic Treatment?

Phase 1 orthodontic treatment, also called phase 1 interceptive orthodontic treatment or early interceptive orthodontics, is the first stage of orthodontic care for children, typically between the ages of 6 and 10. At this stage, the jaw and teeth are still developing, which makes it the ideal time to address orthodontic concerns before they become more complex.

Common issues treated during this phase include:

Severe crowding or spacing Bite problems such as overbite, underbite, or crossbite Early or late loss of baby teeth Thumb-sucking habits Speech difficulties Jaw growth concerns Adult teeth erupting in the wrong position By addressing these concerns early, Phase 1 orthodontic treatment can create a healthier foundation for your child’s smile and reduce the need for more extensive treatment later.

Orthodontic Appliances Used in Phase 1

Phase 1 Orthodontic Treatment often uses specialized appliances to gently guide jaw growth, create space for incoming permanent teeth, and correct bite issues. The appliance type depends on your child’s needs and treatment goals, and our team will always explain how each one works before starting care. Common appliances used during this stage include:

Palatal expanders – widen the upper jaw to create space and improve bite alignment. Partial braces – align select teeth to prepare for future treatment. Space maintainers – hold open gaps left by early tooth loss so permanent teeth can emerge properly. Habit-breaking appliances – help stop thumb-sucking or tongue-thrusting habits. Retainers – maintain progress and hold teeth in their corrected positions after treatment. These appliances are designed to work with a child’s natural growth patterns, making treatment more comfortable and effective.

Treatment Duration What to Expect

Phase 1 interceptive orthodontic treatment typically lasts between 6 and 18 months, depending on your child’s specific needs. At the first visit, we perform a thorough exam, take X-rays, and discuss our findings with you. From there, we’ll create a personalized plan designed to guide your child’s smile toward healthy growth and function.

Treatment during this phase may include appliances such as expanders, partial braces, space maintainers, or habit-breaking devices. Once Phase 1 is complete, many children enter an observation phase, where we monitor growth until Phase 2 treatment may be recommended in the early teen years.
