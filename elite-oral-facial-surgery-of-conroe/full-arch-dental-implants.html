Full Arch Dental Implants

Full arch implants are ideal for people who have lost most or all of their teeth, or who have teeth that are too damaged to be saved. They’re especially great for those who want a permanent, hassle-free solution and don’t want to deal with the limitations of removable dentures.

In a nutshell, full arch dental implants provide a permanent, stable, and natural-looking way to replace a full set of teeth. They help improve your ability to eat, talk, and smile just like you would with your natural teeth!

  • The artificial teeth : Once the implants are in place and have had time to heal (usually a few months), a set of permanent, custom-made artificial teeth is attached to them. These teeth look and feel like natural teeth.
  • The result : You get a full, functional set of teeth that are securely anchored into your jaw, so you can eat, speak, and smile confidently.
  • Stability : The implants are fixed securely in the jaw, so unlike traditional dentures, they won’t slip, shift, or click. This makes eating and speaking much more natural.
  • Comfort : Because the implants are permanent, you don’t have to worry about the discomfort of removable dentures.
  • Look and feel : The artificial teeth are designed to look like natural teeth, so you can smile with confidence.
  • Bone health : The implants help preserve the bone in your jaw by stimulating it, which can prevent bone loss that typically happens when teeth are missing.

How do I know if I’m a good candidate for full arch dental implants?

A good candidate for full arch dental implants is typically someone who:

  • Has significant tooth loss : This could be due to severe decay, gum disease, injury, or simply aging. If you’re missing most or all of your teeth in the upper or lower jaw, full arch implants could be a great solution.
  • Has healthy gums : Your gums should be free from infection or disease, as unhealthy gums can affect the success of the implant process. If you have gum disease, it may need to be treated first.
  • Has enough jawbone : For dental implants to work, you need a certain amount of healthy jawbone to support the titanium posts. If your jawbone has weakened or shrunk (often due to tooth loss), you might need a bone graft procedure before implants can be placed.
  • Is in good overall health : You should be in generally good health, especially with no conditions that could interfere with healing, such as uncontrolled diabetes or certain immune system disorders. It’s important that your body can heal well after surgery and that there are no factors that could hinder implant success.
  • Is tired of traditional dentures : If you’re using dentures but are frustrated with their slipping, discomfort, or maintenance (like needing adhesives), full arch dental implants can provide a more stable, permanent, and comfortable alternative.
  • Is committed to a long-term solution : Full arch implants require a multi-step process and some healing time, so candidates should be willing to commit to the treatment and follow-up care to ensure the implants succeed in the long run.

Working as a Team: Your Oral Surgeon and your General Dentist

When it comes to full arch dental implants, both a dentist and an oral surgeon often work together to ensure the best outcome for the patient. Each professional brings their own area of expertise, and their collaboration is key to providing a smooth and successful treatment process.

1. Initial Consultation and Evaluation

  • Dentist : The process usually begins with a visit to a general dentist, who will assess your overall oral health and determine if you’re a good candidate for full arch implants. They will check your teeth, gums, and jaw to ensure the implants will be successful.
  • Oral Surgeon : If you’re a good candidate for implants, the dentist will refer you to an oral surgeon for a more in-depth evaluation. The oral surgeon will take specialized scans (like 3D X-rays) to assess the jawbone and structure, and plan how to place the implants most effectively.

2. Treatment Planning

  • Dentist : The dentist will work closely with the oral surgeon to develop a detailed treatment plan. They’ll consider factors like the type of implants to use, the shape and alignment of your teeth, and how to create a smile that looks and functions naturally.
  • Oral Surgeon : The oral surgeon plays a key role in determining the surgical steps, especially when it comes to the precise placement of the implants into the jawbone. If bone grafting or sinus lifts are needed to prepare the jaw, the oral surgeon will handle these procedures.

3. Surgical Phase

  • Oral Surgeon : The actual surgery to place the dental implants is performed by the oral surgeon. They’ll ensure the implants are securely placed into the jawbone, using their expertise in surgical techniques and anatomy.
  • During this phase, the patient is usually under sedation or local anesthesia, and the surgeon will monitor their comfort and safety throughout the procedure.
  • Dentist : While the oral surgeon performs the implant surgery, the dentist may be involved in providing the patient with any temporary solutions (like a temporary denture) and ensuring that the patient is well-informed and comfortable.

4. Healing and Osseointegration

  • Oral Surgeon : After the implants are placed, the oral surgeon will monitor the healing process to ensure the titanium implants integrate properly with the jawbone (osseointegration). This healing period is crucial for the implants to become stable and strong.
  • Dentist : The dentist will continue to see the patient for follow-up visits during this period, making sure the gums are healing properly and checking on the progress.

5. Designing and Fitting the Permanent Teeth

  • Dentist : Once the implants have successfully integrated with the jawbone, the dentist takes over the process of designing and creating the final set of artificial teeth. The dentist works with dental technicians to make sure the teeth match your natural smile and fit comfortably.
  • The dentist is also responsible for adjusting the final arch to ensure proper alignment, function, and aesthetics.

6. Final Adjustment and Maintenance

  • Dentist : After the permanent teeth are attached, the dentist makes any necessary adjustments to ensure that the bite and overall fit are just right. They’ll also provide instructions on how to care for your new teeth, and give you tips on maintaining your oral health.
  • Oral Surgeon : While the oral surgeon may not be involved in routine check-ups after the procedure, they are available if any complications arise or if any follow-up surgery is needed (for example, for implant failure or bone issues).

How They Collaborate:

In short, while the oral surgeon focuses on the surgical procedure to place the implants and ensure the jawbone supports them, the dentist designs the artificial teeth, fits them, and ensures the overall aesthetic and functional result. Their teamwork ensures a comprehensive and successful treatment for full arch dental implants.

  • Throughout the process, the dentist and oral surgeon communicate closely to ensure the best possible outcome. The dentist focuses on the cosmetic and functional aspects of the teeth, while the oral surgeon handles the surgical side, making sure the implants are placed correctly and that the jaw can support them. By working together, they create a seamless experience for the patient, from the initial consultation to the final, permanent smile.
