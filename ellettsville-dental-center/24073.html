The Dental Implant Process at Ellettsville Dental Center

First, a surgeon will surgically implant a small biocompatible titanium post into your jawbone beneath the open socket of your missing tooth or teeth. As you heal from this minor surgery, bone tissue fuses with the post in a process called osseointegration. This way, a secure setting is established, and the post can now function as a prosthetic tooth root. And not only does the post perform in much the same way as a live tooth root, but it also encourages healthy new bone growth.

Without a root, your jawbone tends to diminish, which leads to that sagging facial appearance that is common among people who are missing teeth.

In a few months, after you’ve completely healed, you’ll return to our office, where we will bond a porcelain tooth restoration to the post.

If you have more than one missing tooth, we can surgically position implants along your jawbone to hold an implant retained denture or bridgework. If you already wear dentures and want to eliminate the embarrassment they can cause loose dentures, clicking sounds, and sore gums then talk to one of our dentists about implant-supported dentures.

How long does it take to recover from dental implants?

Ensuring a full recovery after dental implant placement is vital to the procedure’s restorative impact on your smile and bite integrity . While individual cases may vary, the post-placement recovery process typically lasts a few weeks.

Immediately after implant placement, some patients encounter discomfort, swelling, or bruising. These effects are usually fairly mild, temporary, and easily managed with antibiotics and over-the-counter painkillers.

Ensuring Optimal Long-Term Recovery

In the days after implant placement, to minimize strain on the freshly placed implants, patients are advised to brush with care around the area and maintain a liquid/soft food diet. Individuals with new dental implants are also advised to avoid hard and potentially sharp or jagged foods like tortilla chips and crusty bread. There’s no minimum amount for maintaining these cautious measures most patients gradually return to their normal routines as recovery progresses.

Over the following months, the implant fuses permanently with the jawbone through osseointegration. Once your dentist confirms full and proper integration, they will recommend a long-term dental restoration, like a dental crown in Bloomington , to maintain smile aesthetics and restore bite integrity.

What are dental implants?

Dental implants are an excellent solution to permanently replacing missing teeth . Unlike conventional removable dentures, dental implants are surgically placed in the jaw to integrate the jawbone and implant naturally. This creates a highly stable base for the prosthetic tooth or teeth, which recreates the strength of natural tooth roots.

What Does a Dental Implant Look Like?

A dental implant consists of three main parts: the implant post , the abutment , and the prosthetic .

The implant post is made of titanium, which works naturally with the body. The abutment is a small piece that connects the post to the prosthetic, and the prosthetic is the replacement tooth or set of teeth. A single crown can top a dental implant or hold a bridge or denture in place.

Once your dentist in Bloomington places a dental implant, it doesn’t require replacement. However, the prosthetic attached to the implant might need to be repaired or replaced after several years.

Over time, the prosthetic can become worn, especially if it’s not cared for properly. Fortunately, your implant dentist in Bloomington can repair your tooth replacements to ensure your smile remains fully functional, healthy, and attractive.
