Common Questions About Dental Implants Does getting a dental implant hurt? Most patients are surprised by how manageable the experience is. The procedure is done under local anesthesia, so you won&#x27;t feel pain during placement. Some soreness and swelling in the days after is normal, and over-the-counter pain relief is usually enough to stay comfortable. Dr. Deshpande and her team will walk you through what to expect every step of the way.

How long does the whole process take from start to finish?

The dental implant process typically takes several months from placement to the final crown, because your jawbone needs time to fuse with the implant post — a process called osseointegration. While it&#x27;s not a quick fix, the result is a long-lasting tooth replacement that looks and feels natural. We&#x27;ll map out a clear timeline for you at your consultation.

How much do dental implants cost, and does insurance cover them?

Dental implants are an investment, and the total cost depends on how many teeth are being replaced and whether any preparatory work is needed. Some insurance plans offer partial coverage for implant procedures. At Elmora Dental, we&#x27;ll review your benefits upfront and discuss payment options so you can make an informed decision.

Am I a good candidate for a dental implant, or is my bone too thin?

Adequate bone density is important for implant success, but even if your jawbone has experienced some loss, options like bone grafting may make implants possible. Dr. Deshpande will evaluate your bone structure and overall oral health before recommending a treatment plan. The best way to find out if you qualify is to book a consultation.

How long will a dental implant actually last?

With proper care, dental implants can last decades — many patients keep them for life. The implant post itself is designed to be permanent, and the crown on top typically lasts 10 to 15 years or longer before it may need to be replaced. Regular dental visits and good oral hygiene are the most important factors in making your implant last.
