The Dental Implant Treatment Process

Working with our implant general dentist in Durham begins with a thorough consultation. Here is what to expect:

Throughout your care, communication is clear and supportive. He ensures each step is explained so you know what to expect and how to care for the area as it heals.

  • Consultation and planning: Your goals, health history, and smile are reviewed. A complete exam assesses gum health and bone support to tailor the plan.
  • Preparatory care when needed: Some patients benefit from treating gum inflammation or addressing other oral concerns before placement.
  • Implant placement: The implant post is placed in the jaw with precision. Most patients describe a short procedure with local anesthesia and manageable post‑operative soreness.
  • Healing and integration: Over several weeks, the implant bonds with the bone. Temporary solutions may be used to maintain your smile during this period.
  • Custom restoration: After healing, a custom crown, bridge, or full-arch prosthesis is attached for a secure, natural look and feel.
  • Long-term maintenance: Routine checkups and careful home care help protect your investment and keep your gums and implant healthy.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants offer everyday comfort and confidence. Key advantages include:

Well-planned implant dentistry supports long-term comfort and function. Patients often find that day-to-day life feels easier and more natural with a fixed solution.

  • Natural function: You can speak and chew with stability that feels close to a natural tooth.
  • Bone preservation: Implants help stimulate the jawbone where teeth are missing.
  • Tooth protection: Neighboring teeth typically remain untouched, unlike some traditional bridges.
  • Durability: With proper care, implant restorations are designed for long-term service.
  • Confidence: A custom restoration improves appearance without removable clasps or adhesives.

Pros and Cons of Dental Implants

Pros

  • Fixed, stable solution that does not shift during eating or speaking.
  • Helps maintain jawbone shape and facial profile.
  • Protects adjacent teeth by avoiding removal of healthy enamel.
  • Custom esthetics that blend with your natural smile.

Cons

  • Requires a minor surgical procedure and healing time.
  • Candidacy depends on gum health, bone support, and overall health.
  • Multiple visits are needed from planning through final restoration.
  • Consistent home care and regular checkups are essential to success.
