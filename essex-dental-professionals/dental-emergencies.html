When Is It a Dental Emergency?

First things first, it’s important to understand what exactly a dental emergency is.

While it’s not always easy to tell whether your situation requires immediate care, in general, if you’re experiencing severe pain, swelling, bleeding, or trauma to the teeth or gums, you should treat it as a dental emergency. Delaying treatment can allow infection to spread, worsen damage, and potentially lead to more complex and costly procedures.

If you’re unsure, it’s always better to call. Our team can help you determine the urgency of your symptoms and guide you on next steps. So, there’s no need to worry about us turning you away just because you don’t need your dental care right this second. In fact, reaching out to our staff may even give you some peace of mind.

Below are some of the most common dental emergencies we treat at our Livingston office.

Severe Toothache

When you have a headache that seems to stick around, unfortunately, this might be a warning sign that something is wrong with your tooth. If it’s localized to one area and continues to progress, well, it’s time to act fast.

Persistent or intense tooth pain is one of the most common reasons patients seek emergency dental care. A severe toothache may indicate deep decay, a cracked tooth, or an infection that has reached the inner pulp of the tooth. When bacteria enter the pulp, inflammation builds pressure inside the tooth, leading to throbbing pain that may radiate to the jaw, ear, or head.

If your tooth pain lasts more than a day, worsens when biting down, or is accompanied by swelling or fever, it’s important to seek care right away. Prompt treatment — often in the form of a root canal or other restorative procedure — can eliminate infection, relieve discomfort, and prevent the need for more extensive treatment later.

Knocked-Out Tooth

A knocked-out tooth is a true dental emergency that requires immediate attention. The sooner you act, the higher the chance the tooth can be successfully reimplanted. Ideally, treatment should occur within 30 to 60 minutes of the injury. Time is critical because the cells on the root surface begin to deteriorate quickly once the tooth is out of the mouth.

If this happens, carefully pick up the tooth by the crown (the top portion), avoiding contact with the root. If possible, gently rinse it without scrubbing and place it back in the socket. If that’s not possible, keep it moist in milk or saliva and call our office immediately. Fast action can make the difference between saving and losing the tooth.

Broken or Cracked Tooth

A broken or cracked tooth may not always cause immediate severe pain, but it can expose the sensitive inner layers of the tooth to bacteria and irritation. Even small cracks can deepen over time, especially when chewing, leading to more significant damage or infection.

You may notice sharp pain when biting, sensitivity to hot or cold, or rough edges that irritate your tongue or cheek. Seeking prompt evaluation allows us to stabilize the tooth, relieve discomfort, and determine whether a crown, bonding, or other restorative treatment is necessary to protect your smile and prevent further complications.

Lost Crown or Filling

We all do our best to follow dental care best practices, but sometimes, things happen. If you notice that your crown is in shambles or your filling is coming out, it’s time to act fast.

When a crown or filling falls out, the underlying tooth structure is left exposed and vulnerable. This can lead to sudden sensitivity to temperature, pressure, and even air. In some cases, the tooth may feel sharp or unstable, making it uncomfortable to chew.

Although it may not seem urgent at first, delaying treatment increases the risk of fracture or infection. We can often replace or temporarily restore the crown or filling quickly, protecting the tooth and preventing the need for more extensive procedures in the future.

Swelling or Dental Abscess

Facial swelling, gum tenderness, or a pimple-like bump on the gums may signal a dental abscess. A dental abscess is a serious infection that develops when bacteria accumulate inside the tooth or surrounding tissue. Abscesses can cause intense pain, bad taste in the mouth, fever, and noticeable facial swelling.

In fact, a dental abscess that’s left untreated will spread beyond the tooth and impact your overall health. Because infections can progress rapidly, it’s essential to seek immediate care. Treatment may involve draining the infection, prescribing antibiotics when necessary, and addressing the underlying cause through a root canal or extraction.

The good news is that prompt intervention helps protect both your oral and general health. Plus, our team is perfectly equipped to help you with this. Whether it’s a routine check for something that’s concerning or you know you have one, at Essex Dental Professionals we have your back every step of the way.

What To Do Before You Arrive for Your Emergency Appointment

When you’re dealing with dental pain or trauma, it’s easy to feel overwhelmed. While the most important step is calling our office right away, there are a few things you can do to protect your teeth and reduce discomfort before you arrive. Taking the right action early can sometimes make a significant difference in treatment outcomes.

Below are helpful steps you can follow depending on your specific situation. If you’re unsure what to do, our team will walk you through everything over the phone.

If You Have a Severe Toothache

Rinse your mouth gently with warm salt water to help reduce bacteria and soothe irritated tissue. Floss carefully around the area to remove any trapped food debris that may be contributing to the pain. You can take over-the-counter pain relievers as directed, but avoid placing aspirin directly on the gums, as this can cause irritation.

If swelling is present, apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek in 15-minute intervals to help reduce inflammation.

Avoid chewing on the affected side until you’re evaluated by a dental health professional.

If a Tooth Is Knocked Out

Did you know that if a tooth is knocked out you may be able to save it?

That said, time is critical. Retrieve the tooth by holding it by the crown (top), not the root. If it’s dirty, gently rinse it with water. Make sure you do not scrub or remove any attached tissue. If possible, try to place the tooth back into its socket and hold it in place. If that’s not possible, keep it moist in milk or saliva while you head to our office in Livingston, NJ.

Call us immediately so we can prepare for your arrival. The faster we see you, the greater the chance we can save the tooth. But yes, that’s right, we just might be able to save your tooth if you act fast enough.

If You’ve Broken or Cracked a Tooth

Rinse your mouth with warm water to clean the area and apply a cold compress to reduce swelling if needed. If there are sharp edges, you can temporarily cover them with dental wax (available at most pharmacies) to protect your tongue and cheeks.

Avoid chewing on that side of your mouth and stay away from very hot or cold foods until your appointment. Prompt care helps prevent the crack from worsening.

If a Crown or Filling Falls Out

If you still have the crown, keep it safe and bring it with you to your appointment. In some cases, it may be possible to re-cement it. You can purchase temporary dental cement at a pharmacy to protect the tooth until you’re seen, but this is only a short-term solution.

Try to avoid chewing on that side and steer clear of sticky or hard foods that could cause further damage.

If You Notice Swelling or Signs of Infection

Swelling, facial tenderness, fever, or a bad taste in your mouth may indicate infection. Apply a cold compress to reduce swelling and call our office immediately. Do not attempt to drain the area yourself.

Infections can spread quickly, so urgent evaluation is essential. Our team will determine the appropriate treatment to eliminate the infection and protect your health. This is one of those situations where you really need to get here sooner rather than later, so make sure you call us as soon as possible.
