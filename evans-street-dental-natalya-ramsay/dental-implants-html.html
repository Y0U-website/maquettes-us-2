What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is essentially a small titanium tooth root that is placed into the jaw to restore one that has gone missing. Titanium is extremely strong and very biocompatible, which means when it’s positioned within the jaw, it actually bonds directly with the surrounding bone. This helps create a strong and reliable foundation for a crown, bridge, or denture that is attached on top. Basically, implants are built just like real teeth, which is why they look and function flawlessly.

4 Step Dental Implant Process

Start to Finish Convenience

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

The first step of rebuilding your smile with dental implants is to visit your McMinnville implant dentist Dr. Ramsay for a consultation. During this appointment, she’ll discuss your smile goals with you and address any concerns you may have. Then, she’ll review your medical history and carefully inspect your teeth, gums, and jaw to see if you’re a good candidate for dental implants.

Issues like gum disease and lacking jawbone density can cause your dental implants to fail. If needed, she will work with you to create a plan for completing preliminary treatments like PRP/PRF therapy or a bone graft first.

Dental Implant Surgery

Once your oral health is on track and you’re ready for your new smile, we’ll schedule your surgery. As a highly trained McMinnville implant dentist, Dr. Ramsay can perform every step of your dental implant treatment personally. After numbing your mouth, she’ll make a small incision in your gums. Then, she’ll place an implant post into your jawbone and cover it with a protective cap. This will protect it and maintain the shape of your gums until it’s time to place your abutment and final restoration.

Dental Implant Osseointegration/ Abutment

After your dental implant surgery, you’ll need to wait several months for your mouth to heal before we can move onto the next step. During this period, the implant post in your jaw will fuse with the surrounding tissue in a process called osseointegration. Depending on how many implants you need and your healing abilities, it can take anywhere from four to six months for implants to fully integrate into the jaw. Once you’ve healed enough, we’ll place abutments that will anchor your final restorations to the implant posts.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

Once we receive your custom dental crown, bridge, or denture, you’ll return for the last step. Using the abutment pieces, we’ll securely anchor your final restoration into place. After we’ve made sure that your bite feels comfortable, you’ll be all set to enjoy your brand-new smile!

Benefits of Dental Implants

More than 3 million people have dental implants, and hundreds of thousands more are placed each year. Why it is behind their booming popularity? Dental implants rebuild missing teeth from the roots up, so they offer benefits that other forms of tooth replacement simply cannot provide. They are truly the gold standard of tooth replacement.

Lifestyle Benefits

Soon after your new teeth are in place, you will begin to experience numerous benefits of dental implants in McMinnville:

  • The freedom to eat anything. A survey of denture wearers found that 34 % believed their dentures limited their ability to live life to the fullest . Dental implants are different! They provide a strong bite force that can enable you to eat virtually any food, including hearty meats and fresh vegetables.
  • Drink your favorite hot beverages. Dentures may warp when exposed to hot temperatures, but implant-supported restorations can stand up to coffee, tea, and other hot beverages.
  • Smile confidently. Implants and the prosthetic teeth they support are designed to complement your natural facial characteristics. They look incredibly lifelike!
  • No slippage. You will be able to speak without worrying that your prosthetic teeth will slip out of place.

Health Benefits

In addition to benefiting you in your day to day life, dental implants in McMinnville can benefit your overall health in several ways:

  • Jawbone preservation. After you lose a natural tooth, the bone that once supported it begins to deteriorate. Over time, the deterioration can reach the point where your face begins to take on a sunken appearance. Dental implants stimulate the jawbone and help to keep it strong, whole, and shapely.
  • No gum irritation. Implant restorations do not depend on your gums for support, so you shouldn’t have to worry about gum sores.
  • Preservation of natural teeth. Certain forms of tooth replacement, such as dental bridges, require that some of the remaining natural teeth be modified. Dental implants are self-supporting and thereby do not usually require that any of your natural teeth be altered.
  • Benefits for overall health. Research shows that individuals who have lost five or more teeth are at an increased risk of diabetes, cardiovascular illness, and other conditions. Dental implants support oral health, which is linked to overall health.

Long-Term Benefits

Did you know that extensive tooth loss is associated with a shortened lifespan ? Dental implants replace lost teeth and may help you to live longer. While that is one of their most outstanding benefits, they offer other long-term perks as well, including:

  • Monetary value. Dental implants have the potential to last a lifetime, which makes them a better monetary value than many types of tooth replacement that need to be replaced every few years.
  • High success rate. Even after 10 years, more than 90 % of dental implant patients are still enjoying their replacement teeth.
  • Easy maintenance. No one wants to spend a lot of time meticulously cleaning their teeth day after day, month after month, and year after year. Fortunately, caring for dental implants is similar in many ways to caring for natural teeth. Strict oral hygiene and regular visits to your implant dentist in McMinnville are a must.

Who is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

While more people are eligible to receive dental implants than ever, there are certain requirements that should be met in order to ensure the smoothest and most successful dental implant treatment possible. For example, patients should have:

  • Good overall health Dental implant treatment is considered a minor surgery. Specifically, the gum tissue is opened so that a titanium post can be placed into the jaw and given time to heal. That means you should good enough general health to complete this process.
  • Good oral health Those with tooth decay, gum disease, or oral infections need to have these issues handled ahead of time before any surgery can be completed.
  • Thick jawbone tissue If the jawbone is not thick enough, the implant won’t fuse properly with the jaw. If too much bone is missing, a bone graft will be recommended first.

Missing One Tooth

A custom-made crown can be positioned between two healthy teeth and held in place using a single implant. Unlike with a traditional bridge, the nearby teeth won’t need to be reshaped at all to make room for the prosthetic, and the crown itself will be color-matched to the rest of the smile. This way, you can expect your new restoration to blend in exactly with your teeth and only you and our team will know that you had it placed.

Missing Multiple Teeth

A small number of implant roots can be used to attach a bridge to the jawbone to restore multiple teeth at the same time. This approach gives these prosthetics the stability and longevity simply not possible with removable ones. The implant bridge works similar to a traditional bridge, but as an added bonus, you won’t necessarily need to have a dedicated implant for each tooth you intend to replace. For example, a pair of implants can hold a bridge consisting of three to four crowns.

Missing All Teeth

All it takes is just four to six implants placed strategically along the jaw to hold a full denture firmly in place. We’ll do our best to maximize as much bone tissue as possible to reduce the number of implants needed to hold the denture. Unlike a removable denture, an implant denture won’t shift or slide across the gums, leading to a more comfortable fit and much stronger bite to eat your favorite foods. You can also customize the denture to be fixed or removable if you prefer. Partial dentures can also be paired with dental implants if desired.

The Parts of Your Dental Implant

Advanced Dental Implant Procedures

Not everyone who rebuilds their smile with dental implants is a good candidate at first. As soon as a tooth goes missing, the jawbone will begin to deteriorate and shrink away. This is a problem because you need to have adequate jawbone density for your dental implants to be successful. Thankfully, we offer advanced procedures that can help get you ready for your brand-new smile!

Bone Grafting

After a tooth goes missing, the jawbone that used to support it actually starts to shrink and change shape. Over time, this can make the bone so weak and thin that it cannot be trusted to support a dental implant. But, Dr. Ramsay can build up the bone with a minor procedure called a bone graft. This increases the height and thickness of the bone right where it’s needed so a patient can get an implant and enjoy all the benefits that come with it.

PRP/PRF Therapy

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP), or platelet-rich fibrin (PRF), is a substance derived from a patient’s own blood that is rich with innumerable healing proteins and regenerative cells. When applied after dental implant-related surgery, it kicks the body’s natural healing response into overdrive, not only shortening someone’s recovery time but also reducing any inflammation and post-treatment pain. This creates a much more comfortable experience and allows a patient to achieve their final result in much less time.

Maintaining & Caring For Your Dental Implants

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

No matter how many teeth you have replaced with dental implants, you will need to continue your regular oral hygiene routine. Yes, it is true that implants cannot decay, but your natural, healthy teeth can. Brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste for two minutes, flossing at least once a day, and rinsing with an ADA-approved mouthwash will help to remove bad bacteria and food particles, keeping your real and artificial teeth looking and feeling their best.

Eat a Healthy Diet

If you’re like most people, you enjoy a good candy bar or bag of potato chips now and then. Unfortunately, too much of a sweet or starchy thing can be extremely bad not only for your waistline but also your smile. Incorporating healthy foods like fruits and vegetables, lean proteins, leafy greens, and dairy will help to strengthen regular tooth enamel and improve gum health, both of which are vital for the lifespan of your implants.

Break Bad Habits

If you want to avoid a trip to see an emergency dentist, it’s time to break bad habits. Whether it is smoking, chewing on pens and pencils, eating ice, or using your teeth to open packages and containers, all of these can damage your teeth, forcing you to spend valuable time and money fixing your dental implants (and possibly your regular teeth, too). The investment you make by choosing dental implants in McMinnville is costly, so you’ll need to make sure that you take every precaution to avoid practicing bad habits that will only damage and destroy your new smile.

Dental Implant Post-Op Instructions

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Immediately following your dental implant surgery, your biggest priority will be to leave the blood clot(s) forming in your mouth untouched so that your healing will happen smoothly. To ensure the best possible odds, follow these guidelines:

  • Don’t spit. Use tissues or swallow your saliva.
  • Do not drink from a straw.
  • Do not smoke for at least the first 24 hours.
  • Keep your fingers and tongue away from your surgical site(s).

Common Side-Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

It is completely normal to experience some mild side effects following dental implant placement. These may include:

These side effects should be temporary and should gradually subside as time goes on. If that is not the case, please contact us at once for help.

  • Intermittent bleeding, which you can lessen with gauze and light pressure. It is not uncommon for bleeding to continue for several days.
  • Swelling during the first 72 hours to potentially over a week. Apply cold or warm compresses to your face to help.
  • General discomfort, which can be managed by prescription or over-the-counter pain reliever.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

For the first few days post-surgery, we recommend consuming a diet consisting of softer, non-abrasive foods, including:

You may return to eating a normal diet as soon as you feel up to it; however, we recommend limiting your consumption of overly crunchy or tough foods at first and avoiding any direct chewing on the dental implant site(s).

  • Mashed potatoes
  • Yogurt
  • Ice cream
  • Pudding
  • Soup (not extremely hot)
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Pasta

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

It is safe to brush your teeth as usual on the day after surgery but be very careful near your surgical site(s). Rinse your mouth out two or three times a day (ideally after eating) with salt water and use prescription mouthwash if you have it. Please avoid any OTC mouthwashes that contain high levels of alcohol such as Scope or Listerine.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

The great news is that now that you’ve gotten your dental implant posts, the hardest part of your treatment is over! When your new crown, bridge, or denture is attached to the dental implant, some minor, temporary sensitivity to your gum tissue may occur, but this is easily managed with pain reliever. There should not be any swelling, bleeding, or extensive recovery at this point in treatment. In fact, you should be ready to go show off your new smile that very same day!
