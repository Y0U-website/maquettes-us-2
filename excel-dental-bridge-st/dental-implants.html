Single or Multiple Dental Implants

Dental implants are incredibly adaptable. If you’re missing just one tooth, a single implant and a replacement tooth can solve the issue. For several missing teeth in a row, a few well-placed implants can provide strong support for a permanent bridge. Likewise, if you’ve lost all your teeth, a complete bridge or denture can be securely attached in your mouth using the right number of implants.

Dental Implants vs. Dentures and Bridges

Unlike bridges and dentures, dental implants don’t just sit on top; they become a part of you. This ensures they feel stable and natural, just like your own teeth. You’ll regain the confidence to eat and smile without worry. What’s more, implants are tough cookies with a powerful biting force. They don’t rely on neighboring teeth for support, which is great news for your overall oral health. Plus, they’re built to last a lifetime, while bridges typically give you around seven to ten years, even less if a root canal is needed.

Taking Care of Your Dental Implants

Treat your new dental implants as you would your natural teeth. Give them the care they deserve by brushing and flossing at least twice daily. Don’t forget to schedule regular checkups and cleanings with your dentist. The more diligent you are in caring for your implants, the better their chances of serving you for a lifetime.

What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that is surgically placed into the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Once in place, a replacement tooth (crown) is attached on top, giving you a natural-looking and fully functional smile.

Am I a good candidate for dental implants?

Ideal candidates are non-smokers with good overall oral health, healthy gums, and no signs of gum disease. If you’ve lost one or more teeth due to injury or illness, you may qualify. A consultation at Excel Dental will help determine if implants are right for you.

Can dental implants replace multiple missing teeth?

Yes. Dental implants are highly versatile. A single implant replaces one tooth, while several strategically placed implants can anchor a permanent bridge for multiple missing teeth in a row. If all teeth are missing, implants can also support a full bridge or denture.

How are dental implants different from dentures and bridges?

Unlike dentures and bridges that rest on top of the gums or rely on neighboring teeth, dental implants are anchored directly into the jawbone. This makes them feel more stable and natural, preserves surrounding teeth, and gives you a stronger biting force. Implants are also designed to last a lifetime, whereas bridges typically last 7–10 years.

Does getting a dental implant hurt?

The procedure is performed under local anesthesia, so most patients experience minimal discomfort during placement. Some soreness and swelling after the procedure is normal and typically manageable with over-the-counter pain relief. Excel Dental prioritizes your comfort throughout the entire process.

Will my dental implant be permanent or removable?

Both options exist. Permanent implants are fixed in place and feel most like natural teeth. Removable implant-supported dentures can be taken out for cleaning. Your dentist at Excel Dental will recommend the best option based on your specific needs.

Can smokers or people with gum disease get dental implants?

Smoking significantly increases the risk of implant failure. Quitting or reducing smoking before and after treatment greatly improves success rates.
