Everything you need to know about dental care

What is scaling and root planing, and why is it different from a regular cleaning?

Scaling and root planing is a deep cleaning procedure specifically designed to treat gum disease (periodontal disease). A regular cleaning removes plaque and tartar from above the gumline only.

How do I know if I need scaling and root planing?

Your dentist will recommend this procedure if a routine check-up reveals signs of gum disease.

  • Swollen, red, or bleeding gums — especially when brushing or flossing
  • Persistent bad breath that doesn’t go away
  • Gum pockets deeper than 4mm measured during your exam
  • Visible tartar buildup below the gumline on X-rays
  • Gums pulling away (receding) from your teeth

Does scaling and root planing hurt?

At Excel Dental, local anesthesia is used to numb the area being treated, so most patients feel minimal discomfort during the procedure. Afterward, you may experience some gum soreness and sensitivity for a few days, which is completely normal.

How is the procedure performed at Excel Dental?

  • A thorough examination to assess the extent of gum disease
  • Application of local anesthetic for comfort
  • Use of dental hand tools, an ultrasonic device, or both to break up and remove deposits from tooth surfaces and below the gumline
  • Root planing to smooth root surfaces and remove embedded bacteria
  • Some cases may require the procedure to be completed in two separate visits—one side of the mouth at a time

How many appointments does scaling and root planing require?

Most patients need two appointments — one for each half of the mouth. This allows adequate healing time and keeps each session comfortable. In mild cases, it may be completed in a single visit.

A follow-up appointment is typically scheduled 4–6 weeks later to check how well your gums have responded to treatment.

What should I do after a scaling and root planing procedure?

Following your procedure at Excel Dental, the care instructions are

  • Wait until numbness wears off before eating to avoid accidentally biting your cheek
  • Eat soft foods for up to 72 hours after treatment
  • Avoid alcohol, hot foods, and spicy foods until gums heal
  • Do not use tobacco for at least 72 hours—nicotine slows healing significantly
  • Brush and floss gently the evening after your procedure
  • Take all prescribed antibiotics for the full course if provided

How long does it take to recover after scaling and root planing?

Most patients recover within 1 to 2 weeks. Mild gum soreness and sensitivity typically resolve within a few days. Your gums will gradually become less inflamed and healthier over the following weeks.

Can gum disease be reversed with scaling and root planing alone?

In the early stage (gingivitis) , gum disease can be fully reversed with scaling and root planing combined with improved at-home oral hygiene.

In more advanced periodontitis , the procedure effectively manages and controls the disease—stopping its progression and preventing tooth loss—but some damage may be permanent. Early treatment is always the better outcome.

How do I prevent gum disease from coming back after treatment?

After successful scaling and root planing, maintenance is critical:

  • Brush twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush using proper technique
  • Floss every day to remove plaque from between teeth and at the gumline
  • Use an antimicrobial mouthwash if recommended by your dentist
  • Schedule periodontal maintenance cleanings every 3–4 months
  • Quit smoking—tobacco use is the #1 lifestyle factor that worsens gum disease

Can I get dental implants or veneers if I have gum disease?

Not until your gum disease is fully treated and controlled. Active gum disease disqualifies patients from implants, veneers, whitening, and most cosmetic procedures. Scaling and root planing at Excel Dental is the essential first step that must be completed before any of these treatments can proceed safely.
