Puberty

With puberty comes a surge in sex hormones, which can make gums more susceptible to irritation from plaque and food remnants. This sensitivity can manifest as swollen, reddened, and tender gums.

Menstruation

In the days leading up to menstruation, some women might notice symptoms akin to those during puberty. This can include gum bleeding, pronounced redness in the gaps between teeth and gums, or even sores on the inner cheek. Generally, these symptoms recede as the menstrual period commences.

Pregnancy

Oral Contraceptives

Oral contraceptives, which utilize synthetic hormones, can lead to symptoms like gum swelling, bleeding, and tenderness. It’s vital to inform your healthcare provider about any medication you’re on, oral contraceptives included. This precaution ensures that potential drug interactions, like the one between antibiotics and contraceptives which can reduce contraceptive efficacy, are avoided.

Menopause

Menopause and the post-menopausal phase can bring about noticeable changes in your oral environment. Symptoms might encompass gum discomfort, a burning sensation, altered taste perceptions like salty or sour, and xerostomia (dry mouth). Regular oral care and professional cleanings can help mitigate these symptoms. For dry mouth, there are saliva substitutes available to offer relief.

Purpose of Periodontal Maintenance

This regular care program is designed to prevent new diseases in the gums and the bone that supports your teeth. By combining diligent home oral care with routine maintenance appointments with your dentist and Dr. Feldner, you stand a strong chance of retaining your natural teeth for life.

The Importance of Supportive Periodontal Care

Your susceptibility to gum disease necessitates continuous vigilance. Bacterial plaque, the primary cause of gum disease, is a colorless film that constantly forms on your teeth, releasing toxins that can harm your gums and teeth. Regular home cleaning helps minimize calculus (hardened plaque) formation, but professional intervention is necessary to manage plaque that evades daily brushing and flossing. Regular check-ups and professional cleanings are essential to catch hidden problems and remove hardened plaque, keeping your teeth and gums in optimal health.

Who Should Oversee Your Periodontal Care?

The severity of your initial periodontal disease will guide the frequency of your maintenance care. Dr. Feldner, in collaboration with your general dentist, will determine the most effective schedule for your supportive periodontal care.

Your Periodontal Maintenance Visit May Include:

  • Examining mouth tissues for abnormal signs.
  • Measuring pocket depths around your teeth.
  • Evaluating and advising on your oral hygiene practices.
  • Removing bacterial plaque and tartar.
  • Conducting x-ray evaluations to monitor your teeth and the supporting bone.
  • Checking for tooth decay and other dental issues.

Scaling and Root Planing

Essential Care for Periodontal Health

The Role of Scaling and Root Planing in Periodontal Treatment

Scaling and root planing are key non-surgical procedures in the initial treatment phase for periodontal disease. These techniques aim to remove causative agents like dental plaque and tartar, which are responsible for gum inflammation and disease. Effective both as a standalone treatment and as a preventive strategy, scaling and root planing are particularly crucial in managing gingivitis and moderate-to-severe periodontal disease.

Empty tab for showing all tabs closed (do not delete)

Understanding Scaling and Root Planing Procedures

Before proceeding with scaling and root planing, Dr. Feldner conducts a comprehensive examination of the mouth, which might include x-rays and a visual inspection. Based on the gums’ condition, the extent of tartar accumulation, pocket depth, and the progression of periodontitis, Dr. Feldner will determine the necessity of these procedures. Local anesthesia may be administered in certain cases to ensure patient comfort.

Scaling: Scaling involves removing plaque and calculus from tooth surfaces, with a focus on the area beneath the gum line along the root. This is achieved using a specialized ultrasonic scaling tool, often coupled with an irrigation process that delivers antimicrobial agents below the gums to reduce oral bacteria.

Root Planing: The process of root planing smooths the tooth’s root by removing cementum and surface dentin tainted with microorganisms, toxins, and tartar. Smoothing the root surface encourages healing and reduces future bacterial colonization.

Post-Procedure Care

Post-scaling and planing, Dr. Feldner may recommend antibiotics or antimicrobial irrigation to control bacteria growth leading to periodontitis. In certain situations, antibiotic fibers are placed in the periodontal pockets to manage infection and promote healing. When deep pockets exist between teeth and gums, making plaque and tartar removal challenging, surgery might be necessary to regain periodontal health.

Benefits of Scaling and Root Planing

Scaling and root planing offer several benefits, crucial for periodontal health:

Scaling and root planing are instrumental in maintaining oral health, preventing disease, and ensuring the longevity of your teeth, while also contributing to your overall well-being.

  • Disease Prevention: Research indicates that bacteria from periodontal infections can spread through the bloodstream, potentially affecting other body parts, and contributing to heart and respiratory diseases. Scaling and root planing effectively remove these harmful bacteria.
  • Tooth Loss Prevention: Deep gum pockets, exceeding 3mm, heighten the risk of periodontal disease. As these pockets deepen, they harbor more bacteria, leading to tissue destruction and eventual tooth loss.
  • Aesthetic and Hygiene Improvement: Besides enhancing the mouth’s appearance and reducing bad breath caused by food particles and bacteria, scaling and root planing also remove superficial tooth stains, contributing to a cleaner and more aesthetically pleasing smile.

Purpose of Crown Lengthening

Crown lengthening is a dental procedure often undertaken to enhance gum health, prepare for certain dental treatments, or address a gummy smile —a condition where an excess of gum tissue covers the teeth, impacting the aesthetic appeal of your smile. This procedure reshapes or contours the gum tissue and bone around the tooth to establish a new, more balanced gum-to-tooth relationship. It can be applied to a single tooth, several teeth, or the entire gum line.

Bite Adjustment

Enhancing Oral Function and Health

Understanding Healthy vs. Destructive Bites

A healthy bite is characterized by the presence of most, if not all, teeth which function normally and show no signs of damage from daily use. In contrast, a destructive bite is evident when teeth display excessive wear, looseness, or when there is damage to the TMJ (temporomandibular joint). Bite adjustment therapy aims to restore a bite that can function effectively without causing further harm or destruction to the teeth.

Components of Bite Adjustment Therapy

Bite adjustment involves a variety of strategies tailored to individual needs, which may include:

  • Reshaping Biting Surfaces: This involves modifying the surfaces of the teeth to reduce excessive pressure points. The goal is to distribute biting forces evenly across all teeth, alleviating stress on individual teeth.
  • Bite Splint Therapy: A custom-fitted plastic bite guard may be used to prevent the teeth from coming into contact. This guard can be worn during the day or night, or both, to protect the teeth and TMJ from excessive wear.
  • Orthodontic Treatment: Braces may be employed to realign teeth that have become misaligned or have drifted from their proper positions.
  • Replacing Old Restorations: Old, worn-out, or damaged fillings can be replaced to improve bite function and prevent further tooth damage.
  • Reconstructive Procedures: For teeth that are severely worn, or damaged, reconstructive treatments may be necessary to restore both function and aesthetics.
