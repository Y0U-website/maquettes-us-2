How It Works

Dr. Abreu places two to four dental implants in your jawbone (the exact number depends on your case). Over the next several months, the implants integrate with your bone — becoming as solid as natural tooth roots. Your custom denture is then fitted with attachments that snap onto those implants. The result is a denture that feels like your own teeth: no slipping, no adhesive, no clicking.

Two Common Configurations

Removable Implant Overdenture (snap-on)

Anchored to 2–4 implants. Snaps in for the day, comes out at night for cleaning. Most affordable implant-denture path. Ideal for patients who want a major upgrade from traditional dentures without committing to a fully fixed restoration.

Fixed Full-Arch (All-on-Four)

A permanent denture screwed onto 4 implants — never removed. Cleaned like natural teeth. Closest result to having your real teeth back. Dr. Abreu is a Diplomate of the International Association of Dental Implantology with full All-on-Four training.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are implant dentures different from regular dentures?

Traditional dentures rest on your gums — they slip, click, and need adhesive. Implant dentures snap onto dental implants placed in your jaw, so they stay put when you eat, talk, or laugh. No adhesive, no clicking.

Will I need to remove them at night?

It depends. Removable overdentures snap in/out for cleaning. Fixed full-arch (All-on-Four) stays in permanently and is cleaned like natural teeth. Dr. Abreu will recommend the right type for your case.

How much do implant dentures cost in Long Island?

Removable implant overdentures: typically $5,000–$15,000 per arch. Fixed full-arch (All-on-Four): typically $20,000–$35,000 per arch. We accept most insurances and offer Cherry financing.

Can I convert my existing dentures?

Sometimes — a well-fitting current denture can be retrofitted with attachments. Dr. Abreu will assess at consultation whether retrofit or new is the right path.

How long does treatment take?

3 to 6 months from implant placement to final denture, allowing the implants to integrate with bone.
