What is the difference between dental bonding and veneers?

Dental bonding and veneers both help in improving or enhancing your smile by hiding chipped, cracked, or discolored teeth. However, they differ in the following aspects:

  • Cost: Dental bonding is more affordable compared to veneers
  • Complexity: Unlike veneers, dental Bonding does not require enamel removal
  • Procedure time: Dental Bonding can be done in just one visit unless several teeth are involved. Dental veneers may require several visits.
  • Longevity: Dental bonding typically lasts between 5 to 10 years while veneers can last 10 years or more.

Can bonded teeth be whitened?

Bonded teeth do not respond to whitening treatments. For best results, whiten your teeth before the dental bonding procedure. This way, Dr. Kim can match the resin material to the current color of your teeth.

Do bonded teeth rot?

Yes, bonded teeth can rot. Since the resin material covers only your tooth surface, a cavity can still form. Luckily, you can practice the following preventive measures to preserve your bonded teeth:

  • Practice good oral hygiene (brushing, flossing)
  • Avoid coffee, tea, smoking, chewing tobacco products, or anything that can stain your bonded teeth
  • Schedule routine dental cleaning every 6 months.
  • Choose the food you eat and avoid directly biting into hard, chewy, or tough food.
