Digital Diagnostic Wax-Up

What is the Full Mouth Rehabilitation procedure like?

One very important aspect of a successful full mouth rehabilitation is the creation of an accurate digitally designed diagnostic wax-up also known as the Digital Smile Design; and the fabrication of excellent prototype (temporary) restorations.

Dr. Szabela has developed a detailed communication system for the CAD/CAM technician and ceramist to use in creating an ideal diagnostic wax-up. The digital diagnostic wax-up instructions and planning guide allow the dentist to communicate the patient′s desires and goals as well as the requirements to create the optimal bite for each individual patient.

A New Bite and Smile The goal of the digital diagnostic wax-up is to recreate the patient′s new bite and smile so that this can then be transferred to the temporary restorations or prototypes. One of the most important aspects of a successful full mouth rehabilitation is obtaining the correct bite and TMJ (jaw joint) position. Dr. Szabela has trained with leaders in this field to develop a predictable method of finding and recording the ideal bite position.

Blueprints for Your New Smile

Creating excellent temporary restorations is a key step in the full mouth rehabilitation because they allow Dr. Szabela and the patient to evaluate the smile , bite and jaw position and determine if any changes need to be made BEFORE the final restorations are made. These are the “blueprints” for the creation of your new smile!

Restoring Function and Beauty

What conditions does Full Mouth Rehabilitation address?

The Millburn & Clark full mouth rehabilitation treatment plan may address a variety of dental concerns, such as:

  • Missing teeth
  • Worn-down teeth
  • Cracked or chipped teeth
  • Uneven spacing
  • Misalignment
  • Gum disease

What procedures are typically involved in full-mouth rehabilitation?

Full mouth rehabilitation is a customized treatment plan, and the procedures involved will vary depending on your unique needs . However, some common procedures used in full mouth rehabilitation include:

Dental Implants: Implants replace missing tooth roots and provide a strong foundation for crowns, bridges, or dentures.

Dental Crowns: Crowns are custom-made caps that are placed over damaged or decayed teeth to restore their strength, shape, and appearance.

Bridges: Bridges are fixed dental restorations that replace one or more missing teeth by anchoring to surrounding teeth.

Veneers: Veneers are thin shells of porcelain or composite resin that are bonded to the front surface of teeth to improve their appearance.

Dentures: Dentures can replace all missing teeth in an arch and can be traditional or implant-supported for a more secure fit.

Gum Grafting: Gum grafting procedures can be used to address gum recession and improve the health and aesthetics of your gums.
