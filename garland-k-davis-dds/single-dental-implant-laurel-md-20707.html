What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small, titanium post that takes the place of your tooth’s root. An oral surgeon will implant this post deep into your gums. After it heals, we can attach a crown to the post, which is essentially your new tooth.

Dental implants are considered the best treatment available to replace missing teeth because they closely approximate the form and function of your natural teeth. Plus, once your crown is attached, no one will be able to tell it isn’t a natural tooth!

The Benefits of Dental Implants

In a natural tooth, the root extends into your jawbone to keep your tooth securely in place. When you chew, the pressure of your bite gets absorbed into your teeth and through the roots to your jawbones. This pressure stimulates your jawbones, which helps the bone maintain its strength and health.

When you are missing a tooth, the root is not there to stimulate that section of your jawbone. Without it, your jawbone slowly starts to deteriorate, meaning you actually lose bone mass in your jaw unless you replace the tooth. This can lead to negative consequences such as:

  • Altered shape of your jaw
  • Weakness in your bite
  • Teeth shifting out of place
  • Difficulty speaking
  • Change in facial features
  • Discomfort or trouble chewing

How Do Dental Implants Stay Secure in the Jaw?

The neat thing about titanium is that it encourages a process called osseointegration. The prefix osseo- comes from the Latin word osseus, meaning bone. And the word integrate means to combine one thing with another so they become whole.

Osseointegration is a process where your bone grows around and fuses to (or integrates with) an implant. This process can take several months to complete, but once it has, your dental implant will be held securely in place by your jawbone just like your natural teeth!
