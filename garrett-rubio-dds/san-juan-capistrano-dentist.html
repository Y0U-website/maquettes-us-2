Restorative Dentistry

Teeth can become damaged because of decay, injury, wear, or other oral health conditions. Restorative dentistry focuses on repairing or replacing compromised teeth while helping restore normal function.

For example, a filling may be appropriate for a tooth affected by a smaller cavity, while a dental crown may be recommended when a tooth has more extensive structural damage. Root canal therapy may allow an infected tooth to be treated and preserved rather than extracted.

For many patients, dental examinations and professional cleanings every six months are a good starting point. However, the appropriate schedule depends on your individual oral health, cavity risk, gum health, and treatment needs.
