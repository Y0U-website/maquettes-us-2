Oral Hygiene

Supporting Overall Health: Research shows links between oral health, heart disease, and diabetes. A healthy mouth contributes to a healthier body.

  • Gum Health: Keeping your gums healthy helps avoid gingivitis and periodontitis, which can cause pain and tooth loss.
  • Fresh Breath: Regular oral hygiene helps eliminate bacteria that cause bad breath.

What Does a Good Oral Hygiene Routine Look Like?

Maintaining oral hygiene doesn’t have to be complicated but requires consistency. Here are some tips to ensure your routine is effective:

We can guide you during your next visit if you’re unsure whether your current routine meets your needs.

  • Brush Twice a Day: Use a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste to clean your teeth gently for at least two minutes. Be sure to reach all surfaces, including the back teeth and gumline.
  • Floss Daily: Flossing removes food particles and plaque between teeth that a toothbrush can’t reach.
  • Rinse with Mouthwash: An antimicrobial or fluoride mouthwash can add an extra layer of protection.
  • Eat a Balanced Diet: Minimize sugary and acidic foods that can erode enamel and promote tooth decay.
  • Replace Your Toothbrush: Change your toothbrush every three to four months or sooner if the bristles become frayed.

Why Oral Hygiene is More Than Just a Routine

Good oral hygiene has far-reaching benefits. It boosts your confidence by keeping your smile bright, helps prevent painful dental conditions, and even supports your overall health. By committing to regular care, you’re taking an essential step toward a lifetime of wellness.

Did you know? Poor oral health can sometimes affect other parts of your body. For example, bacteria from gum disease can enter your bloodstream, potentially leading to inflammation in different areas. This connection makes oral care an essential part of overall health.
